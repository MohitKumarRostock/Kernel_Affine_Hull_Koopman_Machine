\section{Discussion and conclusion}\label{sec:discussion}

The results identify a conditional forecasting role for KAHM:
reconstruction can supply useful nonlinear departure features to a
finite, regularized residual decoder. Their value depends on the
reference geometry, target, retained predictor, and fitting procedure.
The relevant distinction is between a contribution to a specified
predictor and an advantage of the complete selected forecasting
procedure. The experiments provide positive evidence for the former
without establishing the latter against the strongest evaluated
non-KAHM policies.

The feature-span analysis gives a precise account of this
representation question. Some matched KAHM and RBF augmented raw spans
coincide, whereas an irregular reference configuration gives a distinct
KAHM function. An augmented-span equality permits changes to common
coefficients and therefore does not equate departure-only correction
families around a frozen common forecast. Nor do the raw-span results
determine the effect of separate radial transforms or isotropic ridge
penalties. Distinctness is consequently a property to characterize
under the specified representation and decoder, rather than a
sufficient condition for forecasting improvement.

The principal dimension-selected experiment establishes the prescribed
margins over common features and raw-input RBF, together with linear
noninferiority. The RBF-departure and boosting advantages fall below
the required margin, and the selected non-KAHM policy has lower mean
loss. The original six-criterion joint decision remains unsuccessful.
Local dimension changes the common-coordinate basis, projected kernel,
and departure together. Its selected-versus-cap-two improvement
therefore cannot be assigned entirely to the distinctive KAHM map:
the matched-bank decomposition gives a stronger common predictor and
a smaller incremental departure benefit. Full ambient rank is a valid
native construction, but is not a predictive guarantee. At history
width twelve, caps twelve and twenty coincide; this is not evidence
about a binding twenty-dimensional cap on longer histories.

The preprocessing follow-up reinforces this dependence on the complete
procedure. Tanh does not improve the selected construction, and joint
preprocessing/dimension selection does not establish superiority over
the independently selected RBF or the non-KAHM policy. Its separate
fixed-common experiment is more focused: the compared corrections share
the same fitted forecast and bias, and KAHM supplies useful incremental
predictions in the five specified settings. Those secondary comparisons
have a different training allocation and do not replace the primary
complete-procedure result.

The full-training common-predictor study extends that mechanism question.
Cross-fitted KAHM improves the shared baseline by \CFRGainBias\%, while
its \CFRGainRBF\% improvement over independent cross-fitted RBF does
not establish the declared 3\% margin. Its error is
\CFRPolicyPenalty\% higher than the expanded policy. In secondary
comparisons, the in-sample KAHM correction improves the same baseline
by \CFRISGainBias\% and independent in-sample RBF by \CFRISGainRBF\%.
It has lower loss than the cross-fitted correction in every nonlinear repetition
aggregate, but its mean error is still \CFRISPolicyPenalty\% above the
policy in a post-hoc description of the panel means. These results
identify the in-sample centered head as a candidate for fresh evaluation,
not as a replacement prespecified primary procedure.

A possible explanation for the cross-fit disadvantage is a difference
between the errors used as residual responses and those of the deployed
predictor. The fold common models use 48 episodes, the final common
model uses 64, and their B selections may differ. The correction methods
also make their own allowed selections. The comparison therefore does
not isolate a universal effect of cross-fitting or establish a causal
residual-mismatch mechanism. The squared-risk identity instead states
the relevant requirement: the fitted correction must align with the
predictable residual of the retained deployment forecast sufficiently
to offset its squared magnitude.

The correction bounds control one component of prediction. They are
generic to bounded-feature ridge regression and cannot alone support a
KAHM-specific novelty claim. Radial bounding is nonexpansive in its
feature-vector argument, but signed normalization and fallback
boundaries still require their own sensitivity analysis. The baseline
and targets are not uniformly bounded, so the result is neither a
uniform forecast-error bound nor a guarantee for recursive dynamical
prediction. The available zero multiplier returns the baseline exactly;
finite calibration can nevertheless select a correction that increases
future loss.

The evaluated laws were known during method development. Repetition
aggregates quantify variation under those laws, not across independent
application domains. Each study retains its own normalization,
allocation, search menu, and statistical family. The latest reproduction
reuses its originally declared cohort and adds no new independent
repetitions. The older natural-data study uses a constrained historical
representation, so it does not directly evaluate the newest centered
head; it also supplies no natural-series confirmation of that head.
The supporting adaptive failures and the absence of comprehensive
comparisons with modern neural or pretrained forecasting systems further
limit a general adoption claim.

A practical next question is whether making a frozen KAHM procedure
available improves an otherwise identical strong non-KAHM policy.
The present expanded policy excludes KAHM, so this incremental policy
value has not been evaluated. Favorable condition means do not show that
a selector can identify those cases without test outcomes. A future
comparison should preserve the common candidate menu, include a control
for additional search opportunities, account for the full selection
cost, and use a reserved cohort with an application-relevant improvement
criterion. Natural-series claims require reserved natural sources.

Resource interpretation also remains procedure-specific. Construction
and selection costs include every searched candidate; retained winning
state, complete task checkpoints, peak process memory, and elapsed
campaign time measure different quantities. Shared-host measurements
characterize the supplied implementations and do not establish minimum
algorithmic cost or an unspecified deployment budget.

KAHM's supported contribution is a mathematically characterized
geometric inductive bias with conditional value for residual prediction.
Its assessment requires both an identical-baseline mechanism comparison
and independently tuned complete alternatives. This framework explains
why useful reconstruction features, controlled correction magnitude,
and a general forecasting advantage are distinct scientific outcomes.

\section*{Reproducibility and research transparency}

The principal experiment retains its protocol, source freeze, fitted
states, forecasts, development and terminal arrays, selection records,
and analysis under \path{extensions/dimension_forecasting}. Its
independent audit checks all 192 tasks, 18,816 local bases, and 27
analysis tables. The preprocessing follow-up and residual-study
reproduction have separate records under
\path{extensions/preprocessing_dimension} and
\path{extensions/crossfit_residual_reproduction_20261101}.
Their table checks, selected-state calculations, and raw forecast-loss
coverage have different scopes, specified in the Supplementary Studies
and corresponding audit receipts. A successful document build, a
checksum check, selected-model replay, and refitting are distinct
operations; none constitutes external scientific replication.

The original residual-study completed-run records were unavailable.
Its retained reproduction preserves the scientific source, solver,
settings, and analyzer, with separate provenance and a corrected
verification implementation for zero-admissible-rank local banks.
The reproduction source was published before regeneration, with prior
outcome knowledge disclosed. This verifies that execution without
recovering the missing original records or creating a fresh
confirmatory cohort. Its numerical recovery events and verification
limits remain separate from those of the earlier experiments.

The manuscript's displayed values are generated from the released
study tables, with input and output hashes. Its source package contains
those displays and compiles without the numerical archives. Reporting
changes do not alter the frozen scientific procedures or original
analysis families. Full principal proofs and protocol remain in the
main appendices; the self-contained Supplementary Studies supplies the
later protocols and the older supporting investigations. Source and
manuscript editions have separate recorded identities.
