\subsection{Sensitivity to the Coefficient Budget}
\label{sec:budget-sensitivity}

The sensitivity budget is part of the prediction class being evaluated. Its
numerical value is not directly comparable across different feature maps, and
an unfavorable result at one value does not establish an unavoidable
representation error at every larger value. We therefore conducted a follow-up
study after inspecting the main experiment, using new development, calibration,
and test random streams. This study is not presented as having been specified
before the main results were seen.

The follow-up fixes history length 12 and compares $\kappa\in\{1,4,16\}$ on all
four systems, using three new seeds, 2701--2703. All other simulation, fitting,
geometry, and sample-count settings are unchanged: 128 fitting episodes, 512
validation episodes, 4,096 calibration episodes, and 2,048 test episodes per
replicate. A single history length leaves 21 frozen candidates per budget.
We use the same random streams across budgets within each system and seed, so
the data and geometric feature blocks are held fixed while the coefficient
constraint changes. The complete study contains 36 runs and 756 fitted models.

For each system, seed, and budget, we select the KAHM block stage using mean
validation MSE over the three horizons. No budget is selected using test error.
We allocate failure probability $0.05/3$ to each budget's full certificate and
paired-comparison family. By a union bound, the three families jointly have
confidence at least 0.95 within a system--seed replicate. This is not a joint
0.95 statement across all twelve system--seed replicates. Table~\ref{tab:budget}
reports test RMSE for the validation-selected KAHM predictor and the number of
replicates in which that predictor's upper bounds certify RMSE at most 0.35 at
all three horizons.

Increasing the budget from 1 to 4 materially improves fitted KAHM prediction.
For the harmonic system, mean one-step RMSE decreases from 0.3737 to 0.1414;
for the nonlinear autoregression, it decreases from 0.1059 to 0.0670. On the
quasiperiodic system, the number of validation-selected models certified at
tolerance 0.35 increases from zero to three out of three. Thus the norm budget
can affect both prediction and the practical certification decision.

Increasing the budget from 4 to 16 produces much smaller changes in these
experiments. Harmonic horizon-12 RMSE changes from 0.3900 to 0.3875, and none
of those KAHM models passes the all-horizon 0.35 criterion. Quasiperiodic and
nonlinear-autoregressive results are unchanged at the displayed precision.
Logistic horizon-12 RMSE remains approximately 0.5044, with no successful
all-horizon certification. These outcomes do not establish impossibility for
all richer geometries or budgets. They show that raising the coefficient budget
alone to 16 does not resolve the remaining forecasting limitations in this
fixed experimental configuration.

The same-budget autoregressive and quadratic baselines are retained in the
released sensitivity results. On the harmonic system at $\kappa=16$, their
mean horizon-12 RMSEs are 0.2377 and 0.2375, respectively, and both certify
all three replicates at tolerance 0.35. On the nonlinear autoregression at
$\kappa=4$, KAHM has smaller horizon-12 RMSE (0.0746) than the autoregressive
(0.0800) and quadratic (0.0809) baselines, while its one-step error is larger.
The comparison remains task and horizon dependent. Equal numerical budgets
do not imply equal effective capacity when the input metrics differ.

Every run retains its complete candidate descriptions, calibration losses,
pair scores, and test losses. An independent reconstruction audit passed for
all 36 runs, recomputing 2,268 candidate--horizon certificates and 324 paired
improvement statements. All recorded constrained optimizations converged with
reported gaps below $10^{-7}$. The common random streams across budgets were
also verified. These checks support numerical reproducibility and confidence
accounting; they do not convert the follow-up into a preregistered study.
