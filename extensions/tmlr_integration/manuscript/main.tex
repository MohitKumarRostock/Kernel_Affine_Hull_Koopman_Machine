\begin{abstract}
Kernel Affine Hull Machines (KAHMs) learn local reconstruction geometry,
but reconstruction quality alone does not determine predictive usefulness.
We separate retained information, residual predictive structure, finite
decoder access, fit feasibility, and measured cost. A controlled target
intervention identifies a positional distinction that matched affine scores
nearly erase and KAHM scores retain; locality-aware scores or explicit local
coordinates also repair it. An original fresh-sample study finds KAHM
local-coordinate gains on Van der Pol, alongside adverse comparisons on
autoregression and Duffing. In a separate eight-source natural study,
the four reserved sources give raw-plus-KAHM
\NaturalErrorIncrease\% higher scaled MSE than raw RBF at
\NaturalCostRatio{} times its measured pipeline cost; geometric descriptors
change none of 80 frozen diagnostic choices. A paired scaling sensitivity
leaves that aggregate ranking unchanged.
A new prospectively frozen extension preserves the actual raw forecast
and fits a separate KAHM, affine, or locality residual correction. It
crosses 32/128 training episodes with 16/64 raw landmarks on three laws,
using 12 fresh repetitions. Additive KAHM improves raw RBF in
\UtilityRawPositiveContrasts{} of 12 cells under simultaneous paired
intervals, but concatenated KAHM wins all four Van der Pol comparisons;
simpler additive controls also remain competitive. On four new archive
collections, geometric fitting fails on two of 24 configurations, leaving
full-roster geometric and policy losses undefined. A post-hoc matched-success
diagnostic on 22 configurations gives only
\UtilityKAHMGainPercent\% lower MSE for additive KAHM than raw RBF at
\UtilityKAHMCostRatio{} times its model-pipeline cost; raw ridge is
substantially more accurate and cheaper. The fixed validation policy
does not improve aggregate test error on that diagnostic. These results characterize
conditional geometric benefits while exposing the limits of preserving
raw information or raw predictions as an adoption argument. They do not
establish a general KAHM selection rule or minimum necessary complexity.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
Kernel Affine Hull Machines (KAHMs) provide nonlinear reconstruction maps
from local reference observations \citep{Kumar2024KAHM}. Their use as
predictive representations raises a sequence of questions: which target
variation survives the representation, can a finite decoder use it, and
does any resulting improvement justify the complete fitted procedure?
These questions concern different objects. A local reconstructor may recover
every point on an affine line exactly, while its zero residual omits position
along that line. A predictor receiving only this residual cannot use the
position, even though the reconstructor used it. Conversely, preserving
the raw input does not ensure that a particular regularized decoder benefits
from appended geometric features.

We examine this distinction in four connected but separately evaluated
steps. First, a controlled input distribution varies the target's dependence
on branch identity and signed local position. Matched banks separate the
reconstruction rule from reference selection, principal directions, and
score encoding. This intervention identifies a positional-information
bottleneck and controls that repair it. Second, a fresh-sample capacity
study tests the resulting local-coordinate architecture on three synthetic
forecasting laws. It finds reproducible KAHM gains on Van der Pol, harms
on neighboring laws, and dependence on the finite decoder.

Third, a separate natural-source study asks whether an augmented KAHM
procedure is useful at its measured computational cost, and whether a
frozen diagnostic anticipates its benefit on reserved sources. Its
confirmation results under its fixed standardization are unfavorable overall: raw-history-plus-KAHM
has scaled MSE \NaturalKAHMLoss{}, versus \NaturalRawLoss{} for the
matched raw-history predictor and \NaturalRidgeLoss{} for raw ridge.
Adding two geometric descriptors to metadata and a validation probe does
not change the diagnostic's confirmation decisions. These observations
delimit the practical conclusions that can be drawn from the favorable
controlled example.

A bounded follow-up then changes geometry scaling while preserving the raw
input blocks, seeds, target units, capacities, and validation grids. On the
same four formerly reserved sources, min--max KAHM has scaled MSE
\ScalingNaturalKahmMinmaxLoss{}, versus \NaturalKAHMLoss{} under standardization;
the natural-data ranking persists. This new sensitivity reuses inspected
data, so its paired comparisons have a different evidential status from the
original frozen evaluation.

The paper contributes (i) a controlled account of how KAHM reconstruction,
encoding, and local position interact with the target; (ii) a separately
specified, fresh-sample demonstration of conditional finite-decoder gains;
(iii) an auditable natural-source evaluation of accuracy, complete
measured pipeline dependencies, and an unsuccessful benefit diagnostic,
supplemented by a bounded analysis of scaling dependence; and (iv) a new
test of additive geometric corrections that preserves the actual raw
forecast while separating training sample size from raw-decoder capacity.
Local tangent features, finite kernel approximation, and feature-based
selection are established ideas. Our contribution is the evidence about
their use with KAHM and the implications for evaluating reconstruction-based
features, rather than a new universal learning principle or performance
certificate.

The stages have different estimands and implementations. The controlled
capacity study uses local-only representations, historical query arithmetic,
and 64/256 landmarks. The natural study uses stable query evaluation,
raw augmentation in its primary pair, 32/64 landmarks, independently fitted
alternatives, and its own data and tuning limits. It tests practical reach
under a new protocol; it is not an identical-parameter replication of the
Van der Pol effect. We keep their statistical analyses and conclusions
separate throughout.

The fourth stage directly addresses the design problem raised by the
earlier transfer failures. We retain the fitted raw prediction and learn
a separately regularized KAHM, affine, or locality correction with an
explicit zero-correction option. A synthetic factorial varies training
episodes and raw kernel landmarks independently. A fixed validation policy
then tests whether a correction merits use on four additional archive
collections, charging for selection even when it keeps the raw forecast.
This extension evaluates a different procedure on fresh evidence; it
does not erase the previous negative results or make its additive
construction a class-level risk certificate.
Its synthetic comparisons show that retaining a raw pathway can improve
that baseline while still losing to another representation--decoder
combination or a simpler correction. Its archive evaluation also exposes
a feasibility failure on one intermittent series at both capacities.
We retain the failed fits, leave the full-roster geometric losses
undefined, and clearly mark the separately amended common-support
diagnostic. Even within that successful subset, raw ridge is more accurate
and cheaper than the tested geometric procedures. This evidence makes
the justification question more demanding than whether an augmentation
occasionally improves one chosen baseline.

\section*{Reproducibility and research transparency}
The public repository preserves the original scientific source code,
execution freezes, and archived outcomes separately from the new
\path{extensions/forecasting_utility} study. Historical bundles retain
their original scope: the natural forecast arrays reconstruct 2,496
task--method losses, and the separate scaling analysis contains 1,664
natural method runs and 1,440 controlled head fits. Its compact controlled
episode losses and recorded full-prediction audit are distinguished from
recreating the omitted full controlled prediction arrays. Those archives
are not silently replaced by the present extension.

The new extension has its own protocol and dependency freeze, published
before full evaluation, and a reproducibility bundle retaining all 1,336
successful standalone forecasts from 1,344 attempted fits in 168
configurations. Eight geometric fits and four dependent policy evaluations
fail on two Car Parts configurations; their records remain in the bundle.
Successful policy forecasts reuse their selected standalone outputs.
A dated reporting amendment records the post-hoc matched-success archive
diagnostic, while preserving the undefined full-roster geometric losses.
The bundle also retains targets,
split/origin hashes, complete validation candidates, selected settings,
warning/failure accounting, dataset provenance, measured computation nodes,
and analysis tables. It covers this extension; it does not claim to embed
the original studies' complete bundles or the separate certificate paper.
A distinct manuscript-source bundle contains the editable \LaTeX{} and
generated displays for this revised research draft.

The independent extension audit reconstructs final losses from saved
predictions, checks the fixed selector decisions and their cost
dependencies, and verifies exact baseline recovery at zero correction.
The manuscript asset builder independently reconstructs the residual
alignment identity and checks the reported hierarchical aggregation.
Generated tables, figures, and numerical macros carry input/output hashes.
These computational checks establish consistency of supplied evidence;
they do not create independent replications, establish a universal
complexity criterion, or replace scientific review.
