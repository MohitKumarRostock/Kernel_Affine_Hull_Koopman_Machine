\section{Local dimension policies for retained KAHM representations}
\label{supp:dimension-policy}

This study separates the local PCA cap from the number of retained association
coordinates and examines their consequences for forecasting and risk decisions.
The construction in Section~\ref{supp:kahm-implementation} requests
$\min\{r_{\max},d_x,N_{\rm loc}-1\}$ at each actual local encoder, with native
default $r_{\max}=20$. Stored ranks can be smaller after the numerical
variance and projected-range rules. The dimension study uses the block-direct
decoder and training-standardized inputs. The rollout, preprocessing,
matched-geometry, and natural-data comparisons elsewhere in the supplement
retain their explicit cap-two configurations and their separate scopes.

\subsection{Development choices and frozen evaluation}

The six laws are the four reference episode generators and the Van der Pol
and Duffing numerical sensor laws in Section~\ref{app:protocols}. There are
five fitting repetitions per reference law and three per oscillator. Each
repetition has 128 training, 512 validation, 4096 calibration, and 2048 test
episodes of length 72. Histories $m\in\{4,12\}$ share origins
$11,15,\ldots,59$ and horizons $\{1,4,12\}$. The master seed is
2026110104. NumPy \texttt{SeedSequence} derives separate streams for every law,
repetition, and split; the frozen schedule stores their integer seeds.
Geometry uses at most 1600 training histories, at most 24 references per
local fit, and affinity exponent 16. The decoder has ridge $10^{-4}$ and
sum-of-block Frobenius norm budget $\kappa=4$.

The policies are fixed cap two, native cap twenty, and validation selection
over $\{1,2,4,8,12,20\}$. For each policy, the selection criterion is the best
prefix's mean validation MSE over the three horizons. It selects one complete
history--cap bank, with ties resolved by retained coordinate count, history
length, cap, and candidate name. All four prefixes of that bank are retained
for certification, and its validation-selected prefix is identified as the
forecasting model. A bank therefore retains the same earlier coordinate
functions when a later block is appended. Separate cap choices at each prefix
would not establish this nested-class relation.

Within a history length, caps share the geometry seed and training-reference
sampling. Caps at least as large as the ambient history dimension give the
same native request for fixed references; the tie rule favors the smaller
equivalent cap. Consequently, selecting cap four at history four can reproduce
the full ambient native representation. Frequencies of requested caps should
not be interpreted as comparisons among distinct realized ranks in every case.

RBF and affine-hull banks use their original definitions, including the
affine comparator's rank-two SVD normal-direction rule. Each chooses a
history by its best validation prefix and retains all four prefixes.
Ridge autoregression and quadratic regression each choose a history;
persistence and the training-mean model complete the comparison. The resulting
70 development candidates per repetition yield 24 evaluated model instances:
12 KAHM prefixes, eight geometric-comparator prefixes, and four other models.
Identical fitted models can share stored arrays but retain their declared
statements in the confidence allocation.

All \DimDevelopmentFits{} development fits finish before calibration, and
every retained model and validation choice is sealed. Calibration then
evaluates all 26 repetitions, and its complete results are sealed before test
episodes are generated. No calibration or test outcome selects a cap,
history, or prefix in this study. This design leaves the general
simultaneous-family selection rule of Section~\ref{sec:calibration}
available for other declared procedures.

\subsection{Sampling units and simultaneous confidence}

Losses average the thirteen origins within each independent episode.
Clipped forecasts and observed targets lie in $[-1,1]$, so these scores are
bounded by four. Upper calibration uses 4096 independent episode losses.
The lower witness averages matched origins within each disjoint episode pair,
uses the maximum Euclidean distance over retained blocks, and has bound two.
Its independent sample size is 2048. The within-episode origins do not multiply
either sample size. The other fixed feature maps use their declared
Euclidean metrics and budgets. These choices define the classes being
certified.

For each repetition, 24 models and three horizons give 144 designated
one-sided risk statements: a fitted-loss upper endpoint and a class-witness
lower endpoint for each model and horizon. The nine adjacent-prefix comparisons
of the three KAHM banks and three comparisons between their validation-selected
forecasts give a further 36 horizon-specific improvement lower endpoints.
The latter use a shifted loss difference bounded by eight. Empirical Bernstein
calibration applies to the independent episode or episode-pair scores.
One failure probability $0.05$ covers the entire dimension study:
\[
 \delta_{\rm tail}
 =\frac{0.04}{26\cdot144}
 =\frac{0.01}{26\cdot36}
 =\frac{0.05}{4680}.
\]
Only these designated tails belong to the event. It does not cover the other
studies in the paper. In particular, cross-study differences in interval
width cannot isolate dimension when the reference studies use a different
failure allocation.

At RMSE tolerance $\varepsilon$, all-horizon acceptance requires every
upper-MSE endpoint to be at most $\varepsilon^2$; exclusion requires at least
one lower endpoint above $\varepsilon^2$. Other cases remain unresolved.
A simultaneous upper/lower contradiction is retained with a conflict
diagnostic, since a finite-sample confidence event can fail. No such
contradiction occurred. Positive fitted-improvement endpoints concern the
two fitted forecasts, rather than differences between unknown class optima.

\subsection{Forecasting outcomes and local ranks}

Table~\ref{tab:dimension-full-results} retains all nine families and every
horizon. Selected cap improves the mean over horizons in \DimBetterRuns{}
repetitions and ties cap two in \DimEqualRuns{}, with no adverse repetition
in this sample. Its mean is lower on five laws and equal on quasiperiodic.
Selected cap and cap two choose the same history and prefix in all 26
repetitions. The selected policy uses caps one, two, and four in
\DimCapOneCount{}, \DimCapTwoCount{}, and \DimCapFourCount{} repetitions
(Table~\ref{tab:dimension-policy-choices}). Every selected KAHM forecast
uses the 30-coordinate prefix.

Native cap twenty has mixed effects, including
\DimNativeDuffingIncrease\% higher mean MSE than cap two on Duffing, with
higher error in every Duffing repetition. Its selected banks have 674
rank-four, 90 rank-twelve, and 16 rank-three local encoders
(Table~\ref{tab:dimension-policy-ranks}); the rank-three cases arise from
the native projected-range reduction. These ranks explain why a requested
cap of twenty is not a realized dimension of twenty on these histories.
Selected KAHM has lower mean error than the non-KAHM comparators on nonlinear
AR and logistic, while quadratic regression remains stronger on the other
four laws. These cross-family test-error comparisons are descriptive and
do not assume equal capacity from equal numerical coefficient budgets.

The selected-forecast decisions are identical across policies for every
repetition and tolerance. Each policy accepts \DimAcceptLow{}, \DimAcceptMedium{},
and \DimAcceptHigh{} of its 26 selected forecasts at tolerances
$0.2$, $0.35$, and $0.5$; the remaining decisions are unresolved.
All \DimSelectedLowerZero{} selected-KAHM horizon lower endpoints are zero.
Across all retained KAHM prefixes, cap two, native twenty, and selected cap
have 91, 81, and 88 positive lower endpoints. Their accepted/excluded/unresolved
counts at tolerance $0.35$ are $30/3/71$, $29/3/72$, and $30/3/71$,
respectively, among 104 prefix models per policy.
Table~\ref{tab:dimension-policy-resolution} gives the complete law-specific
counts at all declared tolerances.

Of \DimPrefixTails{} adjacent-prefix fitted-loss comparisons,
\DimPositivePrefix{} have positive simultaneous lower endpoints: 47, 40,
and 45 for cap two, native twenty, and selected cap
(Table~\ref{tab:dimension-policy-improvements}). None of the
\DimPolicyTails{} selected-policy comparisons has a positive lower endpoint.
Across the full family, \DimAllLowerZero{} of \DimAllLowerCount{} lower
endpoints are zero and no upper endpoint reaches the loss bound of four.
Dimension selection therefore improves descriptive forecasting accuracy
without improving the selected-forecast tolerance decisions in this study.

All candidate fits complete without numerical failure, and every iterative
fit meets its prescribed objective-gap criterion. The risk guarantees require
feasible fixed forecasts rather than exact optimization. Independent analysis
reconstructs all \DimTotalTails{} designated endpoints, the selection rules,
and all nine exported tables (\DimAuditTableRows{} rows) from archived
scores and metadata, without unpickling models. For the prespecified
repetition numbered zero of each law, complete calibration and test arrays
also verify \DimAuditPredictionJoins{} prediction-to-loss joins. The full
campaign has a score-level audit; full prediction arrays cover those six jobs.
The source package contains the small verified analysis inputs used to
generate the manuscript tables. The numerical-records archive contains
complete production records and their checksums.

The unknown class-optimal risks are not population ground truth supplied by
the test set. Test-error containment is therefore a consistency diagnostic,
not an estimate of true certificate coverage. The evidence is limited to the
six declared synthetic laws, three or five fitting repetitions per law, and
the standard-input block-direct procedure. The controlled two-dimensional
learned-geometry mechanism and larger-observation-block hierarchy retain
their distinct experimental roles.
