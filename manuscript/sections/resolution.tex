
\section{Why a valid certificate may remain unresolved}
\label{app:resolution}

This appendix quantifies three distinct limitations: finite-block population
bias, calibration uncertainty, and numerical slack. The analysis uses standard covering and
bounded-concentration arguments. The bounds are sufficient conditions,
rather than optimal sample-complexity rates.

\subsection{A finite-cover bound on population block bias}

For the scalar class in \eqref{eq:class}, write
$\Delta_b=R^*_\kappa-\mathcal W_b\geq0$. Let
$N_{\mathcal G}(\eta)$ be the size of a finite internal uniform
$\eta$-cover on the representation support: every representative belongs
to $\mathcal G_\kappa(d)$ and every class member is uniformly within
$\eta$ of one representative. The internal condition matters because the
representatives must have risks at least $R^*_\kappa$.

\begin{proposition}[A block-bias bound from an internal cover]
\label{prop:cover}
For every $\eta>0$ admitting such a cover,
\begin{equation}
 0\leq\Delta_b\leq
 \min\left\{\frac B4,\;
  2D\eta+B\sqrt{\frac{\log N_{\mathcal G}(\eta)}{2b}}\right\}.
 \label{eq:cover}
\end{equation}
The infimum over admissible $\eta$ is also a valid upper bound.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let the internal cover consist of $g_1,\ldots,g_N$. With $P_b$ denoting
the empirical average, the squared-loss Lipschitz inequality in the proof
of Theorem~\ref{thm:hierarchy} gives
\[
 a_b\geq\min_j P_b\ell_{g_j}-2D\eta
 \geq R^*_\kappa-\max_j\{R(g_j)-P_b\ell_{g_j}\}-2D\eta.
\]
Each difference in braces is centered and has exponential moment at most
$\exp\{t^2 B^2/(8b)\}$ by the bounded-variable exponential inequality.
Log-sum-exp and Jensen therefore give
\[
 \mathbb E\max_j\{R(g_j)-P_b\ell_{g_j}\}
 \leq\frac{\log N}{t}+\frac{tB^2}{8b}\qquad(t>0).
\]
Optimizing $t$ yields $B\sqrt{\log N/(2b)}$; when $N=1$ take
$t\downarrow0$. Since $T_b\geq a_b$ and
$\Delta_b\leq R^*_\kappa\leq B/4$, the result follows.
\end{proof}

For a metric support with a radius-$r$ cover of size $M(r)$ and
$\kappa>0$, take $r=\eta/(4\kappa)$ and divide $I$ into at most
$J_\eta=1+\lceil2D/\eta\rceil$ bins of width at most $\eta/2$.
Decoders occupying the same bins at all cover centers differ by at most
$2\kappa r+\eta/2=\eta$ everywhere on the support. Selecting one
admissible representative from each occupied pattern proves
\begin{equation}
 \log N_{\mathcal G}(\eta)
 \leq M\!\left(\frac\eta{4\kappa}\right)\log J_\eta.
 \label{eq:metric-cover}
\end{equation}
For $\kappa=0$ the class consists of constants and
$\mathcal W_b=\operatorname{Var}(Y)=R^*_\kappa$ exactly.
The metric entropy can make \eqref{eq:cover} pessimistic. It controls
informativeness of the centered hierarchy; it is not a complexity penalty
needed for validity of Proposition~\ref{prop:empirical}. Statistical
analysis of metric-space Lipschitz regression has direct prior work
\citep{gottlieb2017regression}.

\subsection{Separating the errors that prevent a decision}

Use $K=\lfloor n/b\rfloor\geq1$ predetermined disjoint iid blocks for
the coarse class and $N\geq1$ iid fitted losses for a development-fixed
refined head under the same target and evaluation law. Their samples may
overlap. Suppose rigorous enclosures satisfy
\[
 0\leq\underline T_k\leq T_k\leq\overline T_k\leq C_b,
 \qquad
 0\leq\underline\ell_i\leq\ell_i\leq\overline\ell_i\leq B.
\]
Let
$\nu_L=K^{-1}\sum_k(\overline T_k-\underline T_k)$ and
$\nu_U=N^{-1}\sum_i(\overline\ell_i-\underline\ell_i)$.
Form the KL endpoints from the lower block mean and upper loss mean at
allocations $\alpha_L,\alpha_U$. Denote those exact-real endpoints by
$L^{\rm KL},U^{\rm KL}$. Suppose the reported values obey
\[
 L^{\rm KL}-w_L\leq L\leq L^{\rm KL},\qquad
 U^{\rm KL}\leq U\leq U^{\rm KL}+w_U,
 \qquad w_L,w_U\geq0.
\]
These widths include certified KL-root and rescaling errors. Write
$r_r=R_r(\widehat g_r)$ and define
$h(C,m,a)=C\sqrt{\log(1/a)/(2m)}$.

\begin{theorem}[A sufficient resolution bound]
\label{thm:resolution}
The reported risk statements are jointly valid with probability at least
$1-\alpha_L-\alpha_U$. For separately chosen
$\beta_L,\beta_U\in(0,1)$, with probability at least
$1-\beta_L-\beta_U$,
\begin{align}
 R_c^*-L
 &\leq\Delta_b+h(C_b,K,\alpha_L)+h(C_b,K,\beta_L)+\nu_L+w_L,
       \label{eq:lower-resolution}\\
 U-r_r
 &\leq h(B,N,\alpha_U)+h(B,N,\beta_U)+\nu_U+w_U.
       \label{eq:upper-resolution}
\end{align}
If $E_L$ and $E_U$ denote the terms after $\Delta_b$ in these two
bounds, then on that event
\begin{equation}
 L-U\geq (R_c^*-r_r)-\Delta_b-E_L-E_U.
 \label{eq:gain-resolution}
\end{equation}
Validity and resolution hold together with probability at least
$1-\alpha_L-\alpha_U-\beta_L-\beta_U$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Validity follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:certificate}. The binary
divergence satisfies $\operatorname{kl}(q\Vert p)\geq2(q-p)^2$.
For fixed $p\in(0,1)$, differentiate the difference twice in $q$:
its second derivative is $1/\{q(1-q)\}-4\geq0$, and its value and
first derivative vanish at $q=p$. Boundary cases follow by limits.
Thus
\[
 C k_-(q,c)\geq Cq-C\sqrt{c/2},\qquad
 C k_+(q,c)\leq Cq+C\sqrt{c/2}.
\]
Write $\bar T=K^{-1}\sum_k T_k$ and
$\bar\ell=N^{-1}\sum_i\ell_i$ for the exact sample means.
Hoeffding's lower tail gives
$\bar T\geq\mathcal W_b-h(C_b,K,\beta_L)$ except with
probability $\beta_L$. Pointwise,
$K^{-1}\sum_k\underline T_k\geq\bar T-\nu_L$.
Apply the lower KL comparison and subtract $w_L$ to obtain
\eqref{eq:lower-resolution}.
Similarly, $\bar\ell\leq r_r+h(B,N,\beta_U)$ except with
probability $\beta_U$, and
$N^{-1}\sum_i\overline\ell_i\leq\bar\ell+\nu_U$ pointwise.
The upper KL comparison and $w_U$ prove
\eqref{eq:upper-resolution}. Subtraction and union bounds give the
remaining conclusions without requiring independence between endpoints.
\end{proof}

For an a priori power statement, the observed numerical widths must be
replaced by deterministic protocol bounds or covered by an additional
probability event. Inserting post hoc small widths into an unconditional
power promise is not justified. For a fixed MSE tolerance $\tau$, the
conditions $R_c^*-\tau>\Delta_b+E_L$ and $\tau-r_r\geq E_U$ are
sufficient for coarse-class exclusion and refined-head acceptance. The
first is strict because the exclusion decision is strict. The $\alpha$
allocations govern validity; the $\beta$ allocations govern how often
the certificate resolves. A grid of fixed-sample calculations is not an
optional-stopping guarantee.

\subsection{Safe transfer to empirical Bernstein endpoints}

For $N\geq2$ exact iid scores $Z_i\in[0,C]$, the empirical Bernstein
radius is
\[
 r_{\rm EB}(Z;C,\alpha)
 =\sqrt{\frac{2s_Z^2\log(2/\alpha)}N}
       +\frac{7C\log(2/\alpha)}{3(N-1)},
 \qquad s_Z^2=\frac1{N-1}\sum_i(Z_i-\bar Z)^2.
\]
Each one-sided endpoint has failure probability at most $\alpha$
\citep[Theorem~4]{maurer2009empirical}. Lowering individual numerical
scores can lower their variance enough to increase a naive lower endpoint;
the monotone-mean transfer used for KL is therefore insufficient.

If $[l_i,u_i]\subseteq[0,C]$ encloses each exact score, set
$c=(\bar l+\bar u)/2$. A valid variance upper enclosure is
\begin{equation}
 \overline V_N=
 \min\left\{
  \frac{\sum_i\max\{(l_i-c)^2,(u_i-c)^2\}}{N-1},\;
  \frac{C^2\lfloor N^2/4\rfloor}{N(N-1)}\right\}.
 \label{eq:variance}
\end{equation}
Indeed, the empirical mean minimizes squared distance to a common center,
so $\sum_i(Z_i-\bar Z)^2\leq\sum_i(Z_i-c)^2$. Every latter summand is
bounded by its larger interval-endpoint value. The second quantity is the
maximum sample variance on $[0,C]^N$, obtained by placing coordinates at
endpoints as in Theorem~\ref{thm:hierarchy}. Both bound the same
variance, so their minimum is safe.

Evaluate the radius with a rigorous upper enclosure of
\eqref{eq:variance}. Then $\bar l-r_{\rm EB}^{\rm upper}$ and
$\bar u+r_{\rm EB}^{\rm upper}$, clipped to $[0,C]$, widen the exact-score
interval pointwise. Their coverage survives coupled or adaptive numerical
computation. With only a verified lower score, the range fallback
$[l_i,C]$ is valid but may be weak; the observation must remain included.
Alternatively, a fixed local numerical function of each independent block
produces iid computed scores that may be calibrated directly. Neither
argument validates the sample variance of a data-selected empirical-risk
minimizer as a class-risk certificate.
