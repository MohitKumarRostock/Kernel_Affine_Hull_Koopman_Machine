\section{Additional forecasting evidence}
\label{sec:experiments}

These studies test predictive accuracy and the limits of calibration beyond
the controlled decision mechanisms. All prescribed configurations are retained
in the result archive. Reported test RMSEs average over complete fits.
Detailed study sections, tables, and fitting variability accompany the source
supplement; Appendix~\ref{app:protocols} specifies the generators, comparisons,
and independent sampling units. The matched comparison in
Section~\ref{sec:matched-geometry} subsequently allows validation to select
preprocessing.

\subsection{Bounded synthetic episodes}

Four length-72 generators cover harmonic, quasiperiodic, nonlinear-AR, and
logistic observations in $[-1,1]$. Each of five fits uses 128 training,
512 validation, 4096 calibration, and 2048 test episodes. Histories have
lengths 4 or 12; horizons are 1, 4, and 12. Training-standardized geometric
features and comparison models form 40 frozen candidates per fit. The
$0.05$ failure budget covers candidate risk and adjacent-prefix improvement
bounds within each repetition; overlapping origins do not increase the
independent sample size.

KAHM improves on long-horizon persistence for periodic signals, while
quadratic and autoregressive regressors are stronger. On nonlinear AR,
horizon-12 KAHM RMSE is $0.0743$, compared with $0.0802$ for ridge AR and
$0.0813$ for quadratic regression; these alternatives are better at one step.
At all-horizon RMSE tolerance $0.35$, the 800 candidates yield 301 acceptances,
24 class exclusions, and 475 unresolved decisions. No candidate certifies
$0.50$ on logistic data; this is not a proof of infeasibility.

Among 480 retained-KAHM candidate--horizon statements, 119 lower endpoints
are positive, but only two exclude $0.35$ and none excludes $0.50$.
Paired losses certify 72 of 360 adjacent-prefix improvements. Across all
62 main, oscillator, and budget-sensitivity fits, all 186 horizon-specific
lower endpoints of the complete-family validation winners are zero; 184 of
186 are zero when selection is restricted to KAHM. Candidate exclusion and
validation-winner behavior are therefore distinct findings.

\subsection{Oscillators}
\label{sec:oscillators}

Three new fits per system use independently initialized Van der Pol and
Duffing episodes with the same sample sizes and confidence allocation.
Horizon-12 KAHM sensor RMSEs are $0.3814$ and $0.2499$, compared with
$0.2990$ for Van der Pol affine-hull blocks and $0.1369$ for Duffing quadratic
regression. Iterated KAHM reaches $0.3408$ on Van der Pol, so direct fitting
also need not outperform rollout. At tolerance $0.35$, the 240 candidates
produce 55 acceptances, 30 exclusions, and 155 unresolved decisions. Paired
KAHM refinement gains are certified in 43 of 54 Van der Pol and 10 of 54
Duffing comparisons. The risk concerns the bounded sensor under the numerical
episode law, not physical-state error.

\subsection{Coefficient budgets and numerical checks}
\label{sec:budget-sensitivity}

Fresh streams compare $\kappa\in\{1,4,16\}$ on the four main systems, with
three fits each and joint confidence allocation across budgets. Increasing
$\kappa$ from 1 to 4 helps several tasks; increasing it to 16 gives little
further improvement, and conventional regressors retain advantages. Equal
numerical budgets do not imply equal capacity across representations.
Zero-ridge duplicate-block checks agree within solver gaps; they do not
establish invariance of ridge-regularized fits.

A separate 28,800-trial two-atom check has one pair-lower violation and no
centered-lower or fitted-upper violations. At complete collapse and $n=4096$,
the centered method's mean endpoint is $0.5327$; using known zero representation
variance gives an expected-endpoint lower bound about $0.9031$, compared with
mean pair endpoint $0.9177$. The original comparison therefore partly reflects
unused structural information, not a universal advantage over centered methods.

\subsection{Original pen trajectories}
\label{sec:pendigits}

PenDigits forecasting uses original, unnormalized trajectories and a fixed
acquisition-box transformation. There are 1751 eligible held-out digits from
13 inferred writer identifiers. The completed-stroke eligibility rule defines
a retrospective population. At horizons 1, 2, and 4, KAHM RMSEs are
$0.04661,0.06686,0.11269$, whereas quadratic regression achieves
$0.00768,0.01777,0.05184$; ridge AR also outperforms KAHM. Three fitting seeds
reuse this writer cohort.

Even assuming iid eligible writer records, generic upper-RMSE endpoints near
$1.56$ certify none of $0.2,0.35,0.5$. Neither digits nor overlapping windows
replace independent writers, and source identifiers do not establish the iid
assumption. These comparisons remain descriptive.
