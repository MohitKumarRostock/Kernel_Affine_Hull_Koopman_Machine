\section{Calibration, finite-cohort witnesses, and study protocols}
\label{app:protocols}
\subsection{Controlled study and numerical scope}
Each of the 24 controlled learning repetitions uses disjoint pools of 256 support episodes, 512 head-training episodes, 256 validation episodes, 4096 calibration episodes, and 1024 terminal episodes. The geometry menu crosses min--max or standard scaling, local caps one or two, and concentrations $0.5,4,16,64$. The two-dimensional input means cap two already permits full ambient rank. Prefix sizes two and six share the geometry selected by the refined prefix; ridge parameters $0,10^{-4},10^{-2}$ are selected separately for the two heads. There is no postselection refit.

The $\kappa=2$ block-norm constraint and clipping to $[-0.85,0.85]$ define admissibility. Calibration uses nested prefixes $n=256,1024,4096$ and observation-block sizes $2,4,8,16,32$. All candidate families and allocations are fixed before calibration. The 1008 one-sided events are
$24\times2\times3\times(5+1+1)$: five block lower endpoints, one recycled lower endpoint, and one fitted upper endpoint. Equal allocation per endpoint is not equal aggregate allocation between method families.

Both calibration methods share 190464 block programs over the full 4096-observation workload. Reuse adds KL inversion and bookkeeping, not a second solve. The independent audit regenerates sampled observations, checks candidate objectives and selection, replays dual values, and recomputes endpoints. Common native feature sentinels and guarded floating arithmetic do not constitute an independent end-to-end interval proof. The mathematical confidence statement assumes numerically valid inputs and witnesses; the computational evidence retains that limitation.

\subsection{Finite-cohort risk is a deterministic optimization problem}
Fix coordinates $z_1,\ldots,z_M$, targets $y_1,\ldots,y_M$, a metric $d$, and output interval $I=[a,b]$. Targets in this subsection need not belong to $I$. For
\[
\mathcal F=\{f:\mathcal U\to I:\ |f(u)-f(v)|\leq\kappa d(u,v)\},
\qquad
R_M^\star=\inf_{f\in\mathcal F}M^{-1}\sum_i(f(z_i)-y_i)^2,
\]
scalar extension gives
\begin{equation}
R_M^\star=\frac1M\min_{v\in I^M}
\left\{\sum_i(v_i-y_i)^2:
 |v_i-v_j|\leq\kappa d(z_i,z_j)\quad(i<j)\right\}.
\label{eq:cohort}
\end{equation}
Restriction of any class member is feasible. Conversely, a feasible vector is interpolated by
$\clip_I\min_i\{v_i+\kappa d(\,\cdot\,,z_i)\}$.
A finite minimum of equally Lipschitz scalar functions is Lipschitz, and the pair inequalities imply interpolation, including duplicate coordinates. Clipping is nonexpansive. The feasible set is nonempty and compact, so its minimum exists. This is classical Lipschitz extension and constrained regression \citep{gottlieb2017regression}.

Let $B_{\rm e}$ be an oriented edge-incidence matrix with rows $e_i-e_j$. Given rigorous distance upper bounds $d^+_{ij}$ and any signed rational edge vector $\lambda$, put $s=B_{\rm e}^{\mathsf T}\lambda$. Weak duality gives the lower witness
\begin{equation}
D(\lambda)=
\sum_i\min_{t\in I}\{(t-y_i)^2+s_it\}
-\sum_{i<j}\kappa d^+_{ij}|\lambda_{ij}|
\ \leq\ M R_M^\star.
\label{eq:cohortdual}
\end{equation}
Indeed, $\lambda^{\mathsf T}B_{\rm e}v\leq\sum\kappa d^+_{ij}|\lambda_{ij}|$ for every feasible $v$, and minimization over the larger box preserves the lower inequality. The scalar minima occur at $\clip_I(y_i-s_i/2)$. A rigorously feasible vector supplies an upper witness. With exact arithmetic one may take $L=\max\{0,D(\lambda)/M\}$ and $U=M^{-1}\sum_i(v_i-y_i)^2$ for such a vector; conservative outward enclosures preserve these directions. Consequently $L\leq R_M^\star\leq U$ can be verified without treating solver termination as proof.

With a fitted member $h$ and a verified cohort-risk interval $[\ell_h,u_h]$,
\[
\max\{0,\ell_h-U\}\leq R_M(h)-R_M^\star
\leq u_h-L.
\]
For an MSE tolerance $\tau$, class inadequacy requires $L>\tau$, while constructive adequacy requires $U\leq\tau$. A fitted head is proved to fail only when $\ell_h>\tau$; an upper endpoint above the threshold is insufficient. These statements use every fixed observation and require no sampling penalty. They do not imply a population-risk statement or a method for learning the witnessed function before seeing the cohort.

\subsection{Natural cohort and class-versus-head diagnosis}
The PTB-XL census reuses 4096 calibration and 4096 terminal rows from an already examined study. No new patient cohort was opened. The two-, six-, and fourteen-coordinate maps were selected separately; their relation is not feature nesting. Consecutive and feature-neighbor partitions of sizes 32, 64, and 128 relax the full-cohort constraints, with exact selected dual witnesses. In this census calculation, feature-neighbor grouping is a deterministic optimization device and is not asserted to be an iid pairing scheme. Canonical-feature enclosures are inherited premises. The source is PTB-XL version 1.0.3 \citep{wagner2020ptbxl,wagner2022ptbxl103}.

The Bike Sharing diagnosis uses the same previously exposed 172 decision observations per target and 18 maps (two raw, four Nystr\"om, twelve KAHM). It retains both scalings, KAHM caps $4,12,20$, two prefix choices, and original target values. There are 54 target/map classes and 108 inherited heads. Rational distance endpoints use 80-bit dyadic square-root floors checked by exact squared inequalities. Numerical QP proposals are repaired into feasible vectors using shortest-path Lipschitz envelopes and an exact convex blend. Exact dual values and the common range floor provide the lower endpoints. All 54 class brackets satisfy the prespecified precision flag; no numerical fallback occurs.

All 54 classes also have an adequate target-envelope witness without using the solver proposal, although the solver tightens the brackets. This diagnostic uses assessment labels and therefore is not a training-only repair. The three responses are casual, registered, and their sum; the data do not supply three independent applications \citep{fanaee2013bike}.

\subsection{Predictive and operational comparisons}
The accompanying records preserve complete configurations and outcomes for the following comparisons.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Deployment cost.} Five replications of one HMM law and one chronological Appliances record are assessed. Each of two KAHM procedures has six accuracy passes, zero byte-saving passes, zero latency passes, and zero joint deployment-utility passes.
\item \textbf{Public-data screening.} The Beijing and Bike Sharing panels yield no useful certificate-specific screening and no additional fits avoided. The Bike Sharing class-versus-head analysis establishes why exclusion is impossible for the same adequate classes and tolerances.
\item \textbf{Decoder diagnostics.} Stronger decoder families, regularization and extension choices, and optimization-resolution checks give mixed predictive results. Seoul \citep{uci2020seoul} misses the strong comparator despite a large improvement over its inherited head. The Tetouan/Metro joint selector \citep{salam2018tetouan,hogue2019metro} retains the default and fails both tasks; the Household/Jena comparison passes one task and fails the other.
\item \textbf{Affine-interface diagnostics.} The two-domain primary forecasting criterion is not met. The subsequent affine ablation reuses exposed periods and includes a strong raw-feature control and cases where norm projection or clipping leaves the selected predictions unchanged.
\end{itemize}
These protocols have different targets and evaluation scopes, so their outcomes are not pooled into one hypothesis test. Secondary and diagnostic comparisons do not replace failed primary criteria.
