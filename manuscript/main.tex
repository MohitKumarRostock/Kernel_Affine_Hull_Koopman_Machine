\begin{abstract}
A fitted forecast can fail an accuracy requirement even when its representation admits an adequate decoder. We study certificates that distinguish these possibilities for a fixed representation, metric, target, and decoder sensitivity budget. For bounded scalar targets, minimum squared errors on observation blocks, normalized by one less than block size, form a hierarchy whose first member is a pairwise obstruction. Expected scores are monotone and converge to optimal Lipschitz risk under unconditional iid sampling and compact metric support. Exact finite-block laws and a resolution analysis separate population relaxation, statistical calibration, and numerical slack. Jointly valid class-risk lower bounds and fitted-risk upper bounds can establish minimum feasible cost within a declared family. Reusing verified block duals provides an additional empirical-minimum calibration without further optimization solves. Controlled studies resolve necessity and adequacy; a natural-data census excludes one specified representation class. A complementary finite-cohort diagnosis certifies adequate capacity in 54 classes, including 39 for which both evaluated fitted heads fail. These results show why class inadequacy, fitted-model failure, and numerical prediction fidelity require different evidence. Learned KAHM representations provide a case study, with strong external comparators and unsuccessful screening and deployment results retained. Separate output checks certify numerical fidelity conditional on stored features, without implying future-response coverage or general forecasting superiority.
\end{abstract}
