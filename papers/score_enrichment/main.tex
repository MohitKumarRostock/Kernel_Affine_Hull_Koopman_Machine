\begin{abstract}
Geometry-induced soft-state abstractions describe an observed history through relative affinities to reference regions. Their geometric interpretation does not establish that they retain distinctions needed for forecasting or make those distinctions accessible to a restricted predictor. We investigate this gap using Kernel Affine Hull Machines (KAHMs) as the principal reconstruction method. We distinguish information discarded by score normalization, local-position information, and access through a finite decoder. Explicit inverse relations show how partition normalizers recover the reconstruction-score vector and give conditions under which gated local coordinates preserve the input. A separate comparison of matched raw reconstruction spaces clarifies why information retention, linear expressivity, and dynamical closure are different properties. We evaluate KAHM, affine, and positive-kernel reconstructions with linear ridge and finite nonlinear decoders, using validation-selected native dimensions and 64 independent repetitions of each of four simulation laws. In a prespecified family of 40 paired comparisons, local-position enrichment lowers KAHM membership-only loss in six of eight law--decoder comparisons. Advantages over alternative constructions are conditional: none of the four nonlinear-decoder comparisons against matched positive-kernel reconstruction resolves the ordering, and several comparisons favor affine reconstructions or raw histories. The findings explain why geometric fidelity alone is insufficient for selecting predictive features and why enrichment must be assessed with a specified decoder and common target. They support conditional predictive usefulness, not universal KAHM superiority or autonomous soft-state closure.
\end{abstract}
