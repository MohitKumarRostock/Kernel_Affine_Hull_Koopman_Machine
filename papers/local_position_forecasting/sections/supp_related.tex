
\section{Related evaluations and limits on generalization}\label{supp:related}
The experiments below evaluate different fitted procedures and populations from the representation-only comparison. They retain their original comparison families and do not add observations to its 32 repetition units. Their results are relevant because a feature-level gain need not improve a forecasting policy that can select alternative predictors. Complete source records remain organized by study in the research archive.

\subsection{Validation-selected dimension in residual forecasting}
The dimension study uses 24 repetition units and 192 law/noise tasks. Its forecasts retain a raw-history ridge predictor, fit geometric common/departure features to residual targets, and use C for calibration. Candidate geometric procedures select their own native caps on B. This architecture can receive information through the retained predictor that is absent from the geometric features, unlike the central representation-only experiment.

Its six-criterion family contains five 3\% nonlinear superiority tests and one 5\% linear noninferiority test. Table~\ref{supp:dimension} reports the complete family. For a superiority comparison with KAHM loss $L_K$ and comparator loss $L_m$, the lower-bound convention uses $0.97L_m-L_K$; for noninferiority it uses $1.05L_m-L_K$. A positive simultaneous lower endpoint establishes the criterion. The six bounds use one-sided Bonferroni paired-$t$ inference across 24 repetition units with 23 degrees of freedom. This sign convention is the negative of the candidate-minus-adjusted-reference convention used in the central experiment.

KAHM's nonlinear mean SMSE is 0.028912. It establishes the margins over common features and raw-input RBF, and linear noninferiority, but not the margins over independently selected RBF departures, histogram gradient boosting, or the selected non-KAHM policy. Three of six criteria are established, so the joint six-criterion requirement fails. In a descriptive matched-bank decomposition, dimension selection changes total loss by $-0.000973$: the common-feature contribution improves by $-0.002150$, while the incremental departure benefit weakens by $+0.001177$. This fitted-loss identity is not a causal decomposition.

\subsection{Preprocessing and residual-target evaluations}
The preprocessing experiment jointly selects input transformation and native cap on B. Transformations include standardization, unclipped min--max scaling, and the hyperbolic tangent of standardized histories; five common/departure combinations also include the two min--max/tanh hybrids. It uses 24 fresh repetition units and 192 tasks. Only linear noninferiority is established in its four-criterion family (Table~\ref{supp:preprocessing}). KAHM has higher nonlinear mean loss than the independently selected RBF procedure and the non-KAHM policy. Useful fixed-common corrections in a secondary split-sample comparison do not change those primary outcomes.

The residual-target experiment freezes a B-selected common predictor and a shared B-fitted residual-mean bias. A centered departure slope has no additional residual-mean intercept. Four episode-wise folds produce out-of-fold targets from common predictors fitted on 48 episodes, whereas the in-sample alternative uses the 64-episode deployed common predictor. B selects correction transformations, caps, and penalties; C selects a correction multiplier. Changes between these procedures include residual generation, training size, and selected settings, not just a single target vector with everything else fixed.

The retained residual results are a computational reproduction of the original 24 prescribed repetition units, with prior outcome knowledge and no additional independent repetitions. Original completed records were unavailable. The audit verifies the retained reproduction but does not authenticate the missing original execution. Two of four primary criteria are established: the out-of-fold KAHM correction improves the shared baseline beyond the 3\% margin and satisfies linear noninferiority, but does not establish its margin over out-of-fold RBF or the selected non-KAHM policy (Table~\ref{supp:residual}). Secondary in-sample results do not replace this family.

Both four-comparison families use one-sided Bonferroni paired-$t$ inference over 24 repetition aggregates, 3\% nonlinear margins, and 5\% linear noninferiority. In Tables~\ref{supp:preprocessing} and~\ref{supp:residual}, $L_c$ and $L_m$ denote mean candidate and comparator SMSE. For each repetition, the margin gap is $d_r=0.97L_{r,m}-L_{r,c}$ for nonlinear superiority and $d_r=1.05L_{r,m}-L_{r,c}$ for linear noninferiority. The reported lower gap is $\bar d-t_{23,1-0.05/4}s_d/\sqrt{24}$, where $s_d$ is the sample standard deviation of the 24 gaps. A positive lower gap establishes the criterion. Gain is $100(1-L_c/L_m)$. No bounds from separate studies are pooled.

\subsection{Policy augmentation and natural-series evaluation}
A separate policy experiment uses 24 fresh repetitions and 192 tasks. The base policy $P_0$ selects from ten non-KAHM predictor families on C. Policy $P_1$ adds a centered in-sample KAHM correction, while $P_0^+$ instead adds a centered raw-history Nystr\"om RBF correction. The additions retain the same common forecast and shared bias, and receive fifteen feature configurations, five ridge penalties, and one extra C-selection slot. Equal search counts do not imply equal width, capacity, or computation.

Nonlinear SMSE is 0.02726839 for $P_0$, 0.02704583 for $P_1$, and 0.02722502 for $P_0^+$. The 0.816\% and 0.658\% gains of $P_1$ do not establish the two specified 3\% superiority margins. Both linear noninferiority criteria are established. Table~\ref{supp:policy} retains all four comparisons. For $d_r=L_{P_1,r}-cL_{Q,r}$, with comparator $Q\in\{P_0,P_0^+\}$, $c=0.97$ for nonlinear superiority and $1.05$ for linear noninferiority, the upper endpoint is $\bar d+t_{23,1-0.05/4}s_d/\sqrt{24}$. Only a negative endpoint establishes a criterion.

The natural-series evaluation uses 24 Weekly and 24 Hourly M4 training series, selected by fixed ID hashes and length eligibility while excluding 72 pilot IDs \citep{M4DataSnapshot2026}. It does not use official M4 Test data. Chronological A/B/C/D blocks have twelve intervening origins, and every target in one block precedes the next block. D contains 32 rolling origins at horizons 1, 4, and 12. Models remain fixed. Seasonal naive and drift predictors replace the episode-based out-of-fold RBF family, giving eleven base families and twelve in each enlarged menu.

Aggregate SMSE is 0.127612 for $P_0$, 0.130512 for $P_1$, and 0.134159 for $P_0^+$. KAHM augmentation is 2.273\% worse than the base policy. Three of 48 decisions change: one improves terminal loss and two worsen it; the other 45 forecasts equal the base policy. Aggregation averages horizons within series, series within frequency, and frequencies equally, with A-only loss scales. Anonymous series may share sources, so no independence-based confidence interval is assigned. This is descriptive benchmark-development evidence, not an official M4 ranking or an independent-domain confirmation of the central feature result. The natural protocol was frozen locally before values were parsed and published after evaluation.

\subsection{Other retained research questions}
The archive also contains controlled score-representation experiments, natural-data transfer, scaling and sensitivity studies, additive utility evaluations, target-trained geometry, direct reconstruction training, and online adaptation. These procedures use different encodings, targets, rank policies, or update rules. In particular, older fixed-cap-two constructions can include an ambient $d-1$ restriction that is absent from the native dimension policy in the central experiment. Their observations do not enlarge the central cohort or establish its claims.

The research index identifies each study by scientific question and source directory. Complete reports and compact numerical records are retained in study-specific archives. Large data and fitted-model archives have separately documented availability. These records permit examination of unsuccessful configurations without counting exploratory observations as additional evidence for the central comparison.
