\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Soft abstractions and geometric representations.}
Soft state aggregation and its adaptive construction predate modern representation learning
\citep{singh1994soft}. Approximate state abstractions provide guarantees for decision
quality in reinforcement learning \citep{abel2016abstraction}, while latent transition
models can be learned through task-relevant prediction objectives \citep{gelada2019deepmdp}.
Kernel Affine Hull Machines construct geometric reconstruction models in reproducing
kernel Hilbert spaces \citep{kumar2024kahm,kumar2025collaborative}. We use their
reconstruction affinities to obtain soft coordinates; the underlying geometric
reconstruction method is inherited, rather than a new component of this paper.
Our statistical target is the forecasting error on the original output variables,
and every comparison uses the same target and evaluation law.

\paragraph{Koopman regression and learned dictionaries.}
Extended dynamic mode decomposition approximates the Koopman operator on a finite
dictionary \citep{williams2015edmd}. Learning that dictionary jointly with its
propagation model is established \citep{li2017dictionary}.
\citet{kostic2022regression} formulate RKHS Koopman estimation as a regression
problem and give finite-sample bounds for independent and mixing data.
The quality of a coordinate-space residual depends on the chosen basis;
\citet{haseli2023consistency} develop a basis-invariant consistency index with a
prediction-error interpretation. More recently, \citet{conradie2026trustworthy}
combine invariance diagnostics, multistep projection-error bounds, and dictionary
refinement, while \citet{coote2026residual} learn dictionaries using spectral
residuals and conditioning penalties. Consequently, certified forecasting and
dictionary refinement are not new at this level of generality. Our focus is a
finite-sample statistical decision about a fixed, raw-output prediction target:
certifying a fitted forecast, excluding an entire Lipschitz-bounded prediction
class, and preserving admissible forecasts under geometric refinement.

\paragraph{Lower bounds and model invalidation.}
\citet{kumar2026limits} give lower confidence bounds for norm-bounded linear
prediction of soft successor coordinates, based on the imbalance between
within-class successor variation and retained input variation. The present work
extends the prediction target and predictor class, and studies pairwise geometric
witnesses that do not estimate two root-variance terms separately. The prior paper also includes fitted-model upper risk bounds and independent-trajectory evaluation. We inherit that sampling principle; the matched raw-output pair witness, refinement geometry, and implemented selection procedure are the extensions developed here.
The broader objective of bounding the best risk in a model class is studied by
\citet{muller2025falsification}; \citet{wu2026minimal} give non-asymptotic bounds
in terms of minimal empirical risk. Dimension-free lower certification itself
is therefore not our novelty claim. Pairwise differences also underlie the Gamma
test for noise variance under smooth regression \citep{evans2002gamma}.
Lipschitz regularity has long supported set-membership identification
\citep{milanese2004setmembership} and trajectory invalidation through data-driven
abstractions \citep{jin2020invalidation}. Pairwise Lipschitz violations are used
by \citet{blum2026reliable} to compute prediction ranges robust to a bounded
number of corrupted observations. Those guarantees concern feasible functions
or adversarially corrupted interpolation. Here, the object is a lower confidence
bound on population mean squared forecasting error.

\paragraph{Risk calibration and temporal dependence.}
The concentration ingredients are classical bounded-mean inequalities
\citep{hoeffding1963} and empirical Bernstein bounds
\citep{maurer2009empirical}. Learn then Test explicitly casts predictive risk
control as multiple testing \citep{angelopoulos2025ltt}. We use this established
principle when selecting among models fixed before calibration, and do not
present a finite-family union bound as a new concentration theorem.
Non-asymptotic guarantees for time-series forecasting have also been developed
for standard statistical models \citep{mcdonald2017forecasting}.
Our principal experimental protocol uses independent episodes; the observations
within an episode may be dependent. For a single dependent series, a calibration
split alone does not restore independence. Concentration and decoupling require
additional assumptions, as illustrated by \citet{oliveira2024conformal} in the
distinct setting of conformal coverage. A confidence statement about expected
squared error is not a prediction interval and does not provide conditional
coverage for every observed history.

\paragraph{Scope of the extension.}
The contribution is the connection between geometric loss of distinguishability,
an explicit prediction-risk exclusion certificate, and refinement that preserves
the previous forecasting class. Soft gating and residual fitting themselves have
long histories \citep{jordan1994experts,buhlmann2003boosting}. The value of the
block norm used for refinement is likewise an instance of the established
group-norm and multiple-kernel-learning framework \citep{bach2008group}; its role
here is to retain a comparable prediction budget when geometric blocks are
added, including invariance to exact duplicate blocks. The proposed procedure
must therefore be assessed through its derived guarantees and
through comparisons that hold the raw forecasting task and calibration
procedure fixed, with explicit representation-cost accounting. A lower bound above a desired error tolerance
supports changing the representation or prediction class; a zero lower bound
does not establish adequacy, and a positive lower bound does not by itself prove
that a proposed refinement will improve prediction.
