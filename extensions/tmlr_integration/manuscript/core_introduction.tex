\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

A reconstruction of an observed history and a prediction of its future
solve different problems. Kernel Affine Hull Machines (KAHMs) construct
nonlinear reconstruction maps from local reference observations
\citep{Kumar2024KAHM}. A small reconstruction error does not establish
forecasting usefulness: a discrepancy that vanishes along an affine line
can discard position even when the future varies with that position.
A deterministic reconstruction cannot add information to its input
history, but it can change the functions available to a restricted
decoder and the effect of regularization on those functions.

We investigate KAHM as a geometry-dependent feature construction for
residual forecasting. Four questions organize the analysis. When does
reconstruction supply functions unavailable to a specified matched
representation? How can the magnitude of a fitted correction be
controlled? Does that correction improve an identical retained
forecast? Does the resulting complete procedure outperform alternatives
that receive their own tuning opportunities? Function-space arguments,
magnitude bounds, shared-baseline comparisons, and complete-procedure
comparisons address these questions separately.

The features are \emph{reconstruction departures}: the difference
between a local nonlinear reconstruction and its affine projection,
multiplied by a locality weight. Common features contain the same
locality weights and weighted principal coordinates. A positive-kernel
RBF reconstruction using the same references, kernel, and gates provides
a matched nonlinear control. KAHM instead applies the inverse
regularized Gram matrix before normalizing its signed reference
weights. The comparison concerns the predictive functions induced by
that construction, rather than nonlinearity alone.

The algebra identifies both redundancy and distinctness. Normalized
target transfer is a ratio of two kernel-ridge predictions, connecting
the established KAHM construction to rescaled kernel interpolation
\citep{Deparis2014Rescaled,DeMarchi2017Rescaled}. Under a constant-row-sum
and invariant-subspace condition, KAHM and RBF departures have the same
augmented raw span. An irregular three-reference construction has
distinct spans. Equality of augmented spans permits the common
coefficients to change; it does not equate correction-only families
around a fixed common forecast. The results also precede feature
standardization, radial bounding, and ridge fitting. They establish
neither identical regularized predictions nor a predictive advantage
for every reference geometry or target.

We consider two residual architectures. The principal experiment fits
common and departure features jointly to raw-history ridge residuals,
with a free intercept and a calibrated multiplier. The later mechanism
studies retain an already fitted common forecast and a shared bias, then
add centered departure slopes without another response-mean intercept.
The distinction prevents a change in the fitted common component from
being attributed automatically to reconstruction. For both heads,
bounded feature blocks and the spectral form of centered ridge yield
uniform correction-magnitude bounds. These are properties of
bounded-feature ridge regression, applicable to the matched controls
as well as KAHM; they do not bound the baseline or total forecast error.
An elementary squared-risk decomposition relates usefulness to the
correction's alignment with the predictable residual of the retained
forecast.

The principal experiment compares ten procedures on four numerical
laws, two added-noise levels, and 24 independent repetitions. Separate
episode sets provide fitting, B selection, C calibration, and terminal
D evaluation. Each geometric procedure selects its native local cap
independently before calibration. Five simultaneous comparisons test a
3\% nonlinear improvement margin and a sixth tests 5\% noninferiority
to ridge on a linear control. KAHM establishes the margins over common
features and raw-input RBF, together with linear noninferiority: three
of six criteria. The margins over independently selected RBF departures,
boosted trees, and the selected non-KAHM policy are not established.
Dimension selection improves the complete KAHM procedure, but the
matched-bank decomposition attributes the improvement to stronger
common features despite a smaller additional departure benefit.

Section~\ref{sec:followup-experiments} presents two separately specified
follow-ups. The first studies history preprocessing and native dimension
selection, including a secondary shared-baseline experiment with
disjoint common-fit and residual-fit episodes. Tanh does not improve the
complete KAHM procedure in that design. The second compares cross-fitted
and in-sample residual targets around a common predictor fitted on all
designated training episodes. Cross-fitted KAHM improves the identical
common-plus-bias baseline, but does not establish the required RBF or
policy advantage. In-sample KAHM is the stronger secondary candidate,
while its mean loss remains above the expanded policy. Its selection
as a direction for further research is informed by those outcomes,
rather than a new confirmatory result.

The studies have separate procedures, source checkpoints, cohorts, and
comparison families. Their observations are not pooled. The principal
and preprocessing source freezes preceded their development data, but
were not public preregistrations. The residual study is reported through
a computational reproduction of its originally prescribed cohort
because the original completed-run records were unavailable. It
verifies the newly retained execution, adds no independent repetitions,
and records prior outcome knowledge in its provenance. The companion
\emph{Supplementary Studies} gives complete follow-up protocols and
retains the older controlled, natural-data, and adaptive experiments
with their own construction policies and limitations.

The contribution is a characterization of reconstruction-induced
forecasting features and their conditional value under matched
evaluation. The feature-span results identify when the representation
can differ; the correction bounds specify what its use controls; the
experiments distinguish an incremental residual benefit from a general
advantage of the complete forecasting procedure. Full proofs and the
principal protocol appear in
Appendices~\ref{app:reconstruction-mechanism}
and~\ref{app:reconstruction-results}.
