# Interpretation of the forecasting evidence

The experiment tests whether additional features improve prediction from normalized reconstruction affinities. With each procedure selecting its own settings on the validation data, adding local coordinates to KAHM memberships reduces mean loss in six of eight distribution–predictor combinations. Adding log normalization constants reduces mean loss in three. KAHM local-coordinate features outperform the affine reconstruction control in one comparison and have higher loss in four. They outperform the kernel-weighted averaging control in three linear-predictor comparisons; all nonlinear-predictor comparisons with that control are unresolved.

On Van der Pol with the nonlinear predictor, KAHM-local has mean SMSE 0.002145667, compared with 0.005660276 for raw input, 0.002200870 for affine-local, and 0.002146480 for kernel-weighted local features. The raw and affine comparisons resolve; the kernel-weighted averaging comparison does not. On Duffing, the same nonlinear procedure is worse than raw and affine-local. Linear autoregression favors affine-local and raw input under both predictors. All ten nonlinear-autoregression primary comparisons are unresolved under the prescribed mean-difference intervals.

The family consists of 40 two-sided Bonferroni paired-Student intervals, with 64 repetition units per data-generating distribution. No practical or equivalence margin is specified. Skewed paired differences, including the normalizer comparison on nonlinear autoregression, limit the reliability of a Gaussian/Student approximation. The complete differences, sign counts, extrema, and deletion diagnostics are retained rather than used to select a different inferential method.

Preserving the input information does not guarantee that a specified predictor can use it accurately. Memberships and the log normalization constants recover the full reconstruction scores in exact arithmetic. Membership-weighted local coordinates recover the input when the relevant weights are positive and the combined coordinate matrices have full column rank. Neither identity guarantees good numerical conditioning or a useful fitted forecast. Matched reconstruction methods can span the same space of reconstruction vectors even when their formulas differ. This does not imply that their nonlinear scores or normalized affinity features span the same space.

In the secondary analysis, the dimension limit is selected by the affine reconstruction control with a linear ridge predictor and then shared across reconstruction methods. The ordering of mean losses reverses in five comparisons when this shared dimension limit replaces separate dimension selection. Its favorable results cannot substitute for unfavorable or unresolved primary results. Comparisons of scores and normalization features from the same fitted reconstruction models examine predictor parameterization. They do not establish a difference in the information preserved by those features.

The [research index](docs/RESEARCH_INDEX.md) preserves the separate controlled, residual, and policy evidence, including the unfavorable natural-series result. The central evaluation uses simulated data and direct forecasts. It does not fit an autonomous transition model or empirically test Koopman closure. Claims about Markov sufficiency, recursive stability, universal superiority, and efficient deployment remain unsupported by this study.
