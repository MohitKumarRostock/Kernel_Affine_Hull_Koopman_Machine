KAHM GEOMETRY STUDY: MATHEMATICAL INTERPRETATION
Prepared before confirmation; supporting facts, not claims of novelty

1. THE FIXED-INPUT TARGET-ALIGNMENT LAW

Let S be equiprobable on {-1,+1}, T uniform on [-1,+1], independent, and let
epsilon be independent with mean zero and variance sigma^2. For a>0, set

  X = (T, c*cos(pi*T) + a*S),
  Y_lambda = sqrt(1-lambda)*S + sqrt(3*lambda)*T + epsilon,
  0 <= lambda <= 1.

The signal mean is zero and its variance is
(1-lambda)*Var(S) + 3*lambda*Var(T) = 1. Since T=X_1 and
S=(X_2-c*cos(pi*X_1))/a, the raw-history conditional mean is

  m_lambda(X) = sqrt(1-lambda)*S + sqrt(3*lambda)*T,

and the raw-history Bayes MSE is exactly sigma^2 for every c,a>0,lambda.
If |epsilon|<=b, Cauchy-Schwarz gives |Y_lambda|<=2+b uniformly in lambda.
Changing lambda with a fixed input sample and feature bank changes predictive
relevance without changing input geometry, signal variance, or future-noise
variance. It can still change how easily a restricted decoder represents
the conditional mean.

If U=h(S), where h distinguishes the two regimes and retains no other
information, then E[Y_lambda|U]=sqrt(1-lambda)*S and the Bayes MSE based on U
is sigma^2+lambda. This is a property of that idealized encoding. It is not
assumed to hold for a learned KAHM bank. If S is not recoverable from U,
additional regime-information loss can occur.

2. EXACT NORMALIZER RESTORATION

For group scores T_c in [0,1], known omega>0 and tau>0, let

  ell_c = omega*log(1-T_c+tau),
  z = log(sum_c exp(ell_c)),
  phi_c = exp(ell_c-z).

Every phi_c is strictly positive in exact arithmetic. Therefore

  ell_c = log(phi_c)+z,
  T_c = 1+tau-exp((log(phi_c)+z)/omega).

Thus (phi,z) and the complete score vector T generate the same information
in exact arithmetic, when omega and tau are known. For multiple retained
blocks, one normalizer is required for each separately normalized block.
If an implementation clips T before normalization, this identity restores
the clipped score vector, not values discarded by clipping.

The identity does not imply equal performance for affine heads. The inverse
transformation is nonlinear, so affine function classes on T and on (phi,z)
are generally different; their Euclidean geometries and Lipschitz budgets
also differ. Feature standardization changes these geometries again.

Nor does restoration of T restore full reconstruction vectors. A scalar
reconstruction discrepancy can already have discarded position or direction.
Adding z cannot recover information that was absent from all scores T_c.
Floating-point underflow and roundoff are implementation issues; use stored
log associations and log-sum-exp to check the algebra numerically.

3. SYMMETRY OF THE ACTUAL SCORE FORMULA

Let Q be an orthogonal transformation of the preprocessed history space.
Suppose each group reconstruction R_c is equivariant and group identities
are preserved:

  R_c(Qx) = Q R_c(x).

For multiple local reconstructors in a group, it suffices that Q permutes
the local reconstructors within that group while preserving their set.
Euclidean norms and inner products are invariant under Q. Hence both

  e_c(x) = 1-exp(-||x-R_c(x)||),
  theta_c(x) = arccos(cosine(x,R_c(x)))/pi

are invariant under Q, including a common invariant zero-norm convention.
Their combined scalar score and the group's minimum over local models are
therefore invariant. Normalized affinities and their log normalizer are
also invariant:

  T_c(Qx)=T_c(x), phi(Qx)=phi(x), z(Qx)=z(x).

This result assumes equivariance of the fitted reconstructors and preservation
of group identities. A learned finite reference bank, an asymmetric fitted
scaler, a non-equivariant group fit, or group permutation can break these
conditions. In particular, if Q permutes distinct groups, the affinity vector
can be permuted rather than unchanged and may retain the relevant information.
No universal KAHM invariance claim follows without checking these conditions.

For the cosine-branch law, Q(x_1,x_2)=(-x_1,x_2) preserves the input law and
preserves S while changing T to -T. An invariant U therefore satisfies
E[T|U]=0. Independence and symmetry imply

  R_U^Bayes = sigma^2 + lambda
             + (1-lambda)*E[(S-E[S|U])^2].

Thus R_U^Bayes >= sigma^2+lambda, with equality when U determines S.
U may retain |T| or other even functions without recovering the missing odd
component. This is an information-loss floor for every measurable decoder,
not merely a failure of an affine or norm-bounded head.

4. APPROXIMATE SYMMETRY AND A DECODER BUDGET

Assume X and QX have the same distribution, Q is an involution (Q^2=I),
m(X)=E[Y|X] is square integrable, and U=Gamma(X) is fixed before evaluation.
Let d be a measurable representation pseudometric, let g be kappa-Lipschitz
with respect to d, and put f(X)=g(Gamma(X)). Define

  m_o(X) = (m(X)-m(QX))/2,
  delta_Gamma = sqrt(E[d(Gamma(X),Gamma(QX))^2]).

Assume delta_Gamma is finite. Symmetry gives an orthogonal decomposition of
each square-integrable function into its even and odd parts. In particular,

  ||m-f||_L2^2 >= ||m_o-f_o||_L2^2,
  f_o(X) = (f(X)-f(QX))/2.

The decoder budget gives ||f_o||_L2 <= kappa*delta_Gamma/2. The reverse
triangle inequality and conditional-mean risk decomposition yield

  R(f) - R_X^Bayes
    >= (||m_o||_L2 - kappa*delta_Gamma/2)_+^2.

For the cosine-branch law, ||m_o||_L2=sqrt(lambda), so

  R(f) >= sigma^2 + (sqrt(lambda)-kappa*delta_Gamma/2)_+^2.

This is a population inequality. An empirical estimate of delta_Gamma is
not automatically a certified upper bound on it. Substituting an estimate
into the right-hand side creates a descriptive diagnostic unless uncertainty
is controlled in the correct direction. The result also requires a verified
decoder Lipschitz constant in the exact feature metric used. Unconstrained
ridge or a flexible fitted head is not covered by an arbitrary chosen kappa.
If preprocessing changes the symmetry, evaluate Gamma(QX) by applying the
raw-space Q first and then the exact frozen pipeline; do not silently assume
that a fitted scaler commutes with Q.

5. RECONSTRUCTION-TO-PREDICTION BRIDGE

Let U=Gamma(X), and suppose m(x)=E[Y|X=x] is L-Lipschitz on a domain that
contains X and a reconstruction h(U). Suppose

  E||X-h(U)||^2 <= epsilon_rec^2.

Conditional expectation is the best L2 predictor from U. Therefore

  E||m(X)-E[m(X)|U]||^2
    <= E||m(X)-m(h(U))||^2
    <= L^2*epsilon_rec^2.

Consequently R_U^Bayes <= R_X^Bayes + L^2*epsilon_rec^2. If h is also
L_h-Lipschitz in the declared representation metric and kappa>=L*L_h,
then m composed with h belongs to the kappa-Lipschitz decoder class, yielding

  R_kappa^*(Gamma) <= R_X^Bayes + L^2*epsilon_rec^2.

For a particular admissible affine or clipped head f, if
||m(h(U))-f(U)||_L2 <= delta_head, then

  R(f)-R_X^Bayes <= (L*epsilon_rec+delta_head)^2.

The essential assumption is reconstruction through U. A KAHM reconstructor
R_c(X) receives the original history X; a small ||X-R_c(X)|| does not prove
that there exists an h reconstructing X from the score-only U=phi(X).
The scalar-score stage can discard information already used successfully by
R_c. The generic inequality is a supporting mathematical fact, not evidence
of a KAHM-specific approximation rate or a guarantee for the fitted head.

6. TARGET-RELEVANT DISTORTION WITHOUT FUTURE NOISE

For independent X,X', define

  D_kappa(Gamma) = 0.5*E[(|m(X)-m(X')|
                          -kappa*d(Gamma(X),Gamma(X')))_+^2].

The pairwise Lipschitz argument applied to the deterministic target m(X)
gives R_kappa^*(Gamma)-R_X^Bayes >= D_kappa(Gamma). This separates the
unpredictable future-noise term from the geometric conflict but still
combines information loss and sensitivity approximation. On the controlled
law m is known. On natural data a fitted m does not automatically make the
plug-in quantity a valid certificate.

7. INTERPRETATION OF THIS STUDY

The study tests whether these mechanisms describe actual fitted banks; it
does not assume the answer. A local-coordinate or log-normalizer advantage
can be due to information recovery, easier affine decoding, changed scale,
or a combination. Flexible heads, full score vectors, reflection diagnostics,
and same-component geometric comparisons constrain those explanations.
No result here by itself establishes a novel theorem, a sufficient state for
arbitrary forecasting, broad KAHM superiority, or global computational optimality.
