\section{Related work and contribution boundary}
\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{Representations, KAHM and earlier prediction certificates.}
KAHM reconstruction models are established geometric feature constructions
\citep{kumar2024kahm,kumar2025collaborative}. Their use here does not assume
that reconstruction affinity implies predictive sufficiency. The earlier
analysis of \citet{kumar2026limits} already provides prediction-risk
certificates, fitted-risk upper bounds, empirical-minimum comparisons, and
independent evaluation, including common-target forecasting comparisons. It also
recognizes the need for joint coverage when lower and fitted upper certificates
are combined.
The present mathematical focus is the centered scalar observed-output
hierarchy and decisions over a declared retained family. The comparison in
Table~\ref{tab:prior-comparison} concerns central certificate constructions,
not a claim that every ingredient in the present pipeline is new.

\paragraph{Sample-average optima and the centered hierarchy.}
Monotone lower expectations of ordinary sample-average optimal values are
established in stochastic programming \citep{mak1999montecarlo}.
Our statistic is $T_b=b a_b/(b-1)$, where $a_b$ is the minimum empirical
squared error under the fixed Lipschitz constraints. Since $b/(b-1)$
decreases, ordinary sample-average monotonicity alone does not imply
monotonicity of this centered sequence. Translation of the fitted values
and an average-over-subsets variance identity provide the required
argument. The specific construction joins an exact positive-part pair
member to a monotone scalar hierarchy, a compact-support population limit,
and finite-block laws that expose its slack. None of these population
statements implies that finite-sample confidence endpoints improve with
block size.

\paragraph{Model-class falsification and empirical-infimum calibration.}
Lower inference for the best risk in a model class is an established
problem \citep{muller2025falsification}. The empirical infimum is bounded
above by the empirical risk of each fixed population competitor; this is
the basis of earlier optimal-value lower confidence bounds
\citep[Appendix A.2, equations (A10)--(A11)]{guigues2017confidence}.
The bounded-loss construction of \citet[Appendix A.1]{muller2025falsification}
also uses a nearly optimal fixed competitor. We specify the binary-KL
inversion used here and prove how a pointwise optimization lower value can
replace the exact empirical infimum. This is a calibration and computational
construction for the declared problem, not a new generic concentration
principle. Summing valid lower values from disjoint blocks supplies one such
value; all observations, including those outside the blocks, retain their
place in the full-sample normalization.

\paragraph{Pairwise obstructions and Lipschitz regression.}
The paired-residual argument in \citet[Lemma~2]{akash2021inverse}
uses target separation and a Lipschitz restriction to lower-bound an
adversary's squared error. Finite-sample Lipschitz regression and extension
also have established convex-optimization formulations
\citep{gottlieb2017regression}. We use these ingredients to study a
conditional squared-positive-part witness, its exactness and limitations,
and its relation to the centered block hierarchy. The conditioning law,
marginal target risk, decoder budget and representation metric are declared
together. A positive class-risk lower bound alone does not distinguish
irreducible noise from representation loss or a restrictive sensitivity
budget.

\paragraph{Calibrated decisions over representations.}
Learn then Test connects predictive risk control to simultaneous hypothesis
testing \citep{angelopoulos2025ltt}; statistical selection among candidate
state abstractions has earlier finite-sample guarantees in model-based
reinforcement learning \citep{jiang2015abstraction}. Here joint lower and
upper endpoints answer a specific observed-output question: whether a
declared decoder class is excluded by a loss tolerance, and whether a
particular admissible fitted predictor meets that same tolerance.
Excluding all cheaper declared classes while accepting one fit establishes
minimum feasible cost within that family. This decision can add necessity
assurance even if upper-only selection chooses the same forecast. It does
not imply a better forecast, a globally optimal representation, or a
computational saving from certificate-directed search.
