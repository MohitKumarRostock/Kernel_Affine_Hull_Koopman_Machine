\section*{1. Outcome and scientific judgment}

The KAHM forecasting manuscript has been revised in its editable LaTeX
sources. Its new title is:
\begin{quote}
\textbf{Kernel Affine Hull Features for Time-Series Forecasting:\\
Feature Spans, Bounded Corrections, and Matched Evaluation}
\end{quote}
The subtitle replaces ``Distinctness, Bounded Corrections, and Predictive
Limits.'' It describes the main results directly and distinguishes this
paper from the risk-certificate manuscript revised in Step 1 and the
earlier prediction-limit paper [4,5].

\textbf{The paper's strongest case is an analysis of when KAHM features
add functions to a matched decoder and whether those features improve
forecasting in the declared experiment.} The evidence supports a useful
feature contribution beyond the shared local-coordinate basis. It does
not establish the prespecified forecasting advantage over the entire
comparison family. That distinction is retained throughout the revision.

The work completed here includes a contribution and literature review,
proof review, a repair to one supporting proof, explicit matching of the
comparison scopes, fresh independent mathematical diagnostics, and
independent arithmetic checks of the principal saved forecasts. No new
forecasting experiment was run and no reported experimental value was
changed.

\subsection*{What the revised manuscript establishes}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A precise comparison of raw feature spans.} Sufficient
reference-symmetry conditions make the augmented KAHM and positive-kernel
RBF spans equal. An explicit irregular reference bank gives a KAHM
coordinate outside the specified RBF span. This is not a universal
ordering of the two methods.
\item \textbf{A deterministic bound on the fitted correction.} A bounded
feature map and centered ridge calculation control the residual head,
including its free intercept. The result applies to other bounded
features as well; it does not bound total forecast error.
\item \textbf{A reproducible, qualified empirical finding.} KAHM improves
on the common local-coordinate basis under the declared nonlinear
criterion and satisfies the linear noninferiority criterion. The
remaining four nonlinear criteria are not established.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Delivered material}
The revised main paper has \textbf{23 pages}; the self-contained

\section*{3. Mathematical review and proof repair}

\subsection*{Normalized target transfer and its antecedent}
For $C=(K+\lambda I)^{-1}$ and $s(z)=\mathbf 1^\top Ck(z)\ne0$,
the normalized weights satisfy
\[
 \omega(z)^\top y
 =\frac{k(z)^\top(K+\lambda I)^{-1}y}
 {k(z)^\top(K+\lambda I)^{-1}\mathbf 1}.
\]
The numerator and denominator are kernel-ridge predictions without an
additional intercept. Symmetry of $C$ establishes the identity. The
revised attribution credits rescaled RBF interpolation [1,2] and the
original normalized KAHM construction [3], avoiding novelty claims for
the normalization alone.

\subsection*{What the span results do and do not imply}
If $\mathcal S_0$ contains the common gated affine coordinates, then
\[
 \operatorname{span}\{\mathcal S_0,g(A_K-A_A)\}
 =\operatorname{span}\{\mathcal S_0,gA_K\},
\]
and the same statement holds for $A_R$. The equal-span proposition uses
constant Gram row sums and invariance of
$\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf1,Q_{:1},\ldots,Q_{:d}\}$ under $C$.
The restriction of $C$ to that invariant finite-dimensional space is
onto; the auxiliary coordinate matrix $B$ need not be invertible.
Redundant reference-coordinate columns therefore do not invalidate the proof.

The irregular-bank example establishes a function outside the specified
comparison span, not universal containment or distinctness for every
fitted bank. Equal raw spans need not give identical ridge fits:
coordinate changes alter the isotropic penalty, and separate nonlinear
radial transformations can change span relations. Exact quotient
statements do not automatically extend across a fallback boundary.

\subsection*{The correction bound includes the free intercept}
Eliminating the intercept from the mean-squared-error ridge objective
gives $b=\bar e-\bar\psi^\top w$. Its centered spectral calculation yields
\[
 \|w\|_2\leq\frac{\sigma_e}{2\sqrt\alpha},\qquad
 |\gamma q(x)|\leq|\gamma|\left[
 |\bar e|+(R+\|\bar\psi\|_2)\frac{\sigma_e}{2\sqrt\alpha}\right].
\]
The conditions are $\alpha>0$, $n_A\geq1$, and $\|\psi(x)\|_2\leq R$.
The baseline and full history-to-feature sensitivity remain uncontrolled;
the radial map is nonexpansive only in its own input.

\subsection*{Repair to the supporting reconstruction bridge}
The supporting proposition assumes that the scalar conditional mean $m$
is $L$-Lipschitz on a domain $D$ containing $X$ and $h(U)$ almost surely.
The original argument used $m\circ h$ as a globally admissible decoder,
although $h(u)$ need not belong to $D$ at every representation point.
The revised proof defines
\[
 \widetilde m(x)=\inf_{z\in D}\{m(z)+L\|x-z\|_2\}.
\]
This finite, globally $L$-Lipschitz extension agrees with $m$ on $D$.
Then $g=\widetilde m\circ h$ is measurable and globally
$LL_h$-Lipschitz, agrees with $m(h(U))$ almost surely, and retains the
original square-integrability and risk bound. This closes the domain
gap without changing the proposition's assumptions or conclusion.

\section*{4. Principal experimental finding retained}

The principal study contains four fixed simulation laws, two observation
noise conditions, and 24 repetitions: \textbf{192 task suites}. The
independent statistical units are the \textbf{24 repetition aggregates},
not the individual forecast windows or all 192 task suites. Histories
have length 12 and forecasts use horizons 1, 4, and 12. Evaluation D
contains 1,664 origins per task. The protocol remains locally prospective,
informed by supporting work, and not publicly preregistered.

\subsection*{Nonlinear comparisons}
The bounded KAHM-departure procedure has mean normalized MSE
\textbf{0.0299267}. Positive gain below means lower KAHM loss relative to
the comparator; negative gain means higher KAHM loss.

The nonlinear margin contrasts are $0.97L_{\rm comparator}-L_{\rm KAHM}$.
Their one-sided lower bounds use the original Bonferroni allocation over
all six primary comparisons. The table is recomputed from the saved
forecasts; its values agree with the reported analysis to floating-point
tolerance [8].

For the linear control, KAHM mean NMSE is $0.898104$ versus ridge
$0.896320$. The lower bound for the specified 5\% noninferiority contrast,
$1.05L_{\rm ridge}-L_{\rm KAHM}$, is $0.0416102$, so that criterion is
established. In total, \textbf{two of six primary criteria are established}.
The overall prespecified criterion is not met.

The raw-RBF comparison illustrates why a point gain is insufficient: its
3.92\% estimated gain exceeds the nominal 3\% target, but its
simultaneous lower margin bound is below zero. That is an unresolved
margin claim, not evidence that the procedures are equal. Boosting and
the C-selected non-KAHM procedure have lower aggregate point loss;
the displayed tests do not themselves certify reverse superiority.

\subsection*{What was actually matched}
The bounded KAHM and RBF departure controls share reference geometry,
kernels, gates, feature dimensions, transformation rules, and decoder
grids. Each has 42 common plus 168 departure coordinates in the declared
configuration. Their fitted coefficients, fitted scales, and selected
settings remain procedure-specific. Raw RBF uses the 12-coordinate
history with at most 64 Nystr\"om coordinates. Boosting and the selected
policy have different representations and search spaces. Those
comparisons assess complete procedures; they do not isolate one
feature block at equal computation.

The original paired Student-$t$ calculation uses 23 degrees of freedom
and critical value approximately $2.582017$ for a one-sided family
allocation of $0.05/6$. These are nominal approximate intervals under the
declared sampling design. Arithmetic replay does not validate exact
coverage, expand the set of application domains, or turn the research
margins into deployment requirements.

\subsection*{Useful diagnostic details}
The normalized-weight diagnostics include 156 query rows with negative
weights, so they do not silently substitute positive convex weights.
Their largest affine-target identity discrepancy is approximately
$3.55\times10^{-15}$. The independent ridge solver formulations differ
by at most $4.93\times10^{-13}$; an attaining example reaches both
reported bounds. The recorded experimental maximum-error ratio is
$1.5534154$, and no recorded feature, coefficient, correction, or
stability violation was found under the original checks.

\textbf{Limits of this verification.} The principal forecast arrays are
original archived data, not new observations. No forecasting models were
refitted, no selected fitted objects were loaded, and no hardware timings
were repeated. The larger supporting studies were reviewed in the
manuscript but were not all independently replayed. Floating-point
diagnostics supplement the proof review; they are neither formal proofs
nor interval-arithmetic certificates.

\section*{7. Positioning the papers and choosing the next research step}

\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.23\linewidth}YY@{}}
\toprule
 & Risk-certificate manuscript & KAHM forecasting manuscript \\
\midrule
Main question & Can a declared representation/decoder class meet a risk
tolerance, and can cheaper declared classes be excluded? & When do
KAHM departures add raw functions beyond matched coordinates, and does
the resulting fitted procedure improve forecasting? \\
Core result & Calibrated lower and upper risk evidence; a hierarchy of
representation assessments under stated assumptions. & Raw-span
equalities and a distinctness example; an intercept-inclusive residual
bound; matched and complete-procedure comparisons. \\
Meaning of a guarantee & A statement about a specified class and
evaluation law under a simultaneous probability event. & An algebraic
statement or deterministic fitted-correction inequality, plus an
approximate statistical comparison under the experiment. \\
Link to adoption & Can support a declared feasibility/cost decision
when informative certificates are available. & Provides a candidate
feature construction and evidence about where its value is or is not
established. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

The risk paper's learned implementation primarily uses simplex-valued
affinities with constrained decoders. The present paper uses gated
reconstruction departures, separate radial transformations, and fitted
residual heads. Its baseline and targets are not uniformly bounded.
The risk paper's guarantees therefore do not automatically apply to
this pipeline. Any future connection must specify the representation,
decoder budget, target assumptions, and evaluation law explicitly.

\subsection*{Publication assessment}
The present contribution can be argued as a theoretical and empirical
study of a representation's behavior. TMLR's stated criteria emphasize
well-supported claims and useful, clear findings; a new state-of-the-art
score or a novel studied method is not required [6]. This supports the
chosen positioning, not a prediction of acceptance.

Before submission, the authors should review the new attribution and
proof repair and check exact overlap with related manuscripts. TMLR
permits overlap with preprints but restricts reuse from published,
accepted, or parallel archival submissions [7]. Distinct research
questions alone do not establish compliance; a complete cross-manuscript
originality audit was not performed here.

Both PDFs retain anonymous TMLR review typography. The template's
``under review'' header is a formatting mode, not a statement that a
submission has occurred. The editable source ZIP retains author licenses,
provenance, and this author-facing review; it is not an anonymous review
upload. Nothing has been submitted or published during this work.

\subsection*{Step 3 should test a concrete adoption decision}
The next experiment should ask when a user should choose KAHM for
specified series, horizons, sample sizes, and resource limits. Predeclare
that decision, retain strong comparators and selection costs, and test
it on new held-out tasks. The present simulations do not validate a
new-domain selection rule. That adoption evaluation remains Step 3.
