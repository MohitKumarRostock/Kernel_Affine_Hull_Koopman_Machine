\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{KAHMs and soft geometric representations.}
KAHMs construct geometric reconstruction models in reproducing kernel Hilbert
spaces (RKHSs) \citep{kumar2024kahm,kumar2025collaborative}. We use their
reconstruction affinities as soft dictionary coordinates for forecasting the
observed signal. The reconstruction algorithm is inherited; our development
concerns constrained output decoders, refinement with a comparable prediction
budget, and decisions based on risk bounds. Soft state aggregation and its
adaptive construction predate modern representation learning
\citep{singh1994soft}. Approximate state abstractions provide guarantees for
decision quality in reinforcement learning \citep{abel2016abstraction}, and
latent transition models can be learned through prediction objectives relevant
to the task \citep{gelada2019deepmdp}. In our comparisons, the observed-output
target and its evaluation law remain fixed as the representation changes.

\paragraph{Koopman regression and learned dictionaries.}
Extended dynamic mode decomposition approximates the Koopman operator on a
finite dictionary of functions \citep{williams2015edmd}. Jointly learning the
dictionary and its propagation model is also established
\citep{li2017dictionary}. \citet{kostic2022regression} formulate RKHS Koopman
estimation as regression and give finite-sample bounds for independent and
mixing data. A coordinate-space residual depends on the chosen basis;
\citet{haseli2023consistency} develop a basis-invariant consistency index with
a prediction-error interpretation. More recently,
\citet{conradie2026trustworthy} combine invariance diagnostics, multistep
projection-error bounds, and dictionary refinement, while
\citet{coote2026residual} learn dictionaries using spectral residuals and
conditioning penalties. These studies establish forecasting guarantees and
refinement methods for learned dictionaries. Our focus is a finite-sample
statistical decision about a fixed observed-output target: certify a fitted
forecast, exclude an entire class of decoders with a prescribed Lipschitz
budget, and preserve admissible forecasts when refining the representation.

\paragraph{Lower bounds and model invalidation.}
\citet{kumar2026limits} give lower confidence bounds for norm-bounded linear
prediction of soft successor coordinates. Their bounds compare within-class
successor variation with retained input variation. We extend the target and
predictor class and study a pairwise lower-bound statistic, called a
\emph{witness}, that avoids separately estimating and subtracting two
root-variance quantities. The prior paper also supplies upper risk bounds for
fitted models and evaluates them on independent trajectories. We inherit that
sampling principle. The extensions here are a pairwise witness for the observed
forecasting target, refinement geometry, and an implemented selection procedure.

More broadly, \citet{muller2025falsification} study bounds on the best risk in a
model class, and \citet{wu2026minimal} give non-asymptotic bounds in terms of
minimal empirical risk. Our contribution concerns the pairwise statistic and
its implications for a specified representation and sensitivity budget.
Pairwise differences also underlie the Gamma test for noise
variance in smooth regression \citep{evans2002gamma}. Lipschitz regularity has
long been used in set-membership identification \citep{milanese2004setmembership}
and in trajectory invalidation through data-driven abstractions
\citep{jin2020invalidation}. \citet{blum2026reliable} use pairwise Lipschitz
violations to compute prediction ranges robust to a bounded number of corrupted
observations. Those guarantees concern feasible functions or interpolation
under adversarial corruption. Our target is a lower confidence bound on
population mean squared forecasting error.

\paragraph{Risk calibration and temporal dependence.}
Our concentration arguments use classical bounded-mean inequalities
\citep{hoeffding1963} and empirical Bernstein bounds
\citep{maurer2009empirical}. Learn then Test formulates predictive risk control
as multiple testing \citep{angelopoulos2025ltt}. We use this principle to select
among models fixed before calibration and apply a union bound over the finite
candidate family. Non-asymptotic forecasting guarantees also exist for
standard statistical models \citep{mcdonald2017forecasting}.

Our principal experimental protocol uses independent episodes, while permitting
dependent observations within an episode. A calibration split from a single
dependent series does not itself restore independence. Concentration and
decoupling arguments then require additional assumptions, as illustrated by
\citet{oliveira2024conformal} in the distinct setting of conformal coverage.
Our confidence statements concern expected squared error. They do not provide
prediction intervals or conditional coverage for every observed history.

\paragraph{Scope of the extension.}
The contribution connects three elements: loss of predictive distinguishability
in a representation, a lower risk bound that can exclude a prediction class,
and refinement that preserves the previous forecasting class. Soft gating and
residual fitting have long histories
\citep{jordan1994experts,buhlmann2003boosting}. The block norm used here is also
an instance of the established group-norm and multiple-kernel-learning framework
\citep{bach2008group}. Its role is to maintain a comparable coefficient budget
as geometric blocks are added, including exact duplicates.

Our experiments compare representations under a common observed forecasting
target and calibration protocol, with feature count as the representation-size
measure. A lower bound above
the desired error tolerance rules out the specified prediction class. A zero
lower bound leaves its adequacy unresolved, and a positive lower bound alone
does not show that a proposed refinement will improve prediction.
