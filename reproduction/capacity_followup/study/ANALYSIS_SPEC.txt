KAHM GEOMETRY STUDY: PRESPECIFIED ANALYSIS
Version 1.1, prepared before confirmation data generation

STATUS AND SCOPE
This plan distinguishes an independent development pilot from a frozen
confirmation experiment. It is a prospective analysis plan for this study,
not an externally registered protocol. It does not guarantee publication,
power, distribution-free coverage, or superiority of KAHM as a family.

The principal objective is to identify whether the KAHM reconstruction
operator, scalar score construction, affinity normalization, or decoder
restriction accounts for prediction differences. The core geometric
comparison shares the training-fitted input scaler, training groups,
reference observations, score formula, association parameters, and head
selection procedure. Only the reconstruction operator differs between the
KAHM and matched affine-projection conditions. Any other geometric baseline
is a separately described comparator, not a same-component substitution.

The executable protocol records all generator parameters, feature formulas,
reference caps, dimensions, hyperparameter grids, noise laws, and seed rules.
This analysis plan must be frozen with that protocol and the source hashes
before any confirmation data are generated. Exact seed identifiers and the
pilot-derived numerical margins are stored in the freeze manifest.

1. EXPERIMENTAL UNITS AND DATA SEPARATION

There are two independent pilot replicates and 24 confirmation replicates.
Pilot and confirmation use disjoint random streams. The number 24 is fixed
before confirmation and is a resource decision; no prospective power claim
is made. Every planned replicate is retained, regardless of its results.

Within each replicate, training, validation, and test observations or complete
episodes use separate random streams. Inputs and targets from the test split
are never used to fit preprocessing, groups, reference models, feature
standardization, hyperparameters, or decoder coefficients. Validation may
select among the frozen candidate grid. Ties use a fixed deterministic rule.
The final test assesses the complete train-and-validation selection pipeline.

The cosine-branch study uses S uniform on {-1,+1}, T uniform on [-1,+1],
independent, and X=(T,c*cos(pi*T)+a*S), a>0. Its target is
Y=sqrt(1-lambda)*S + sqrt(3*lambda)*T + epsilon, with independent centered
bounded epsilon of fixed variance. The main comparison has no input noise.
At fixed c and training size, all lambda targets share the same input draws
and frozen unsupervised feature bank. Latent S and T are available only to
generate targets and conduct explicit diagnostic analyses; they do not
provide oracle group labels to the learned geometry. The same noise draws
may be reused across lambda for pairing, if recorded in the protocol.

Branch training sizes are 256 and 1024; validation size is 512 and test size
is 2048. The 1024 training size belongs to the primary family. The smaller
size is a prespecified secondary sample-size diagnostic.

The forecasting laws are nonlinear_ar, Van der Pol, and Duffing. Each
confirmation replicate has 128 independent training episodes, 128 independent
validation episodes, and 512 independent test episodes. Thirteen prescribed
origins per training episode give 1664 fitting rows. These rows are not 1664
independent episodes. Exact horizons, origins, episode lengths, initial-state
laws, and dynamical parameters are fixed by the executable protocol.

For forecasting, first average squared errors over the prescribed horizons,
output coordinates, and origins within each episode. Then average equally
over the independent test episodes to produce one method MSE per replicate.
The comparison estimand weights the declared horizons equally unless the
executable protocol explicitly supplies fixed alternative weights before
confirmation. Individual-horizon and individual-origin findings are secondary.

For the branch experiment, average squared errors over all test observations
to produce one method MSE per replicate. The primary endpoint uses noisy
observed Y. Errors against the analytically known conditional mean are a
secondary noise-separated diagnostic, not a replacement selected after results.

2. PILOT AND FREEZE BOUNDARY

The two pilot replicates may be used for implementation checks, runtime and
numerical diagnostics, and choosing a feasible common hyperparameter grid.
Pilot outcomes must be identified as development results and excluded from
confirmation means, uncertainty intervals, and success counts.

Before confirmation, freeze: all nine primary settings, all three primary
cellwise contrasts and two target-alignment interactions, all feature and head
formulas, the common grids and tie rules, the
24 confirmation seed identifiers, the full failure-handling rule, source
hashes, the input and data-generation specifications, and the numerical
practical-effect margin for every primary setting. Preserve the pilot records
used to derive those margins. A machine-readable freeze manifest should
contain these items or hashes of the files that contain them.

No method, setting, seed, metric, model-selection rule, or interval construction
may be altered in response to confirmation results while keeping the original
confirmation label. Any such follow-up must be separately identified as
exploratory, or use a new prerecording and fresh independent confirmation data.

3. PRIMARY FAMILY: 27 CELLWISE CONTRASTS AND TWO INTERACTIONS

Primary settings are:
  B1: branch, train=1024, c=0, lambda=0
  B2: branch, train=1024, c=0, lambda=0.5
  B3: branch, train=1024, c=0, lambda=1
  B4: branch, train=1024, c=1, lambda=0
  B5: branch, train=1024, c=1, lambda=0.5
  B6: branch, train=1024, c=1, lambda=1
  F1: nonlinear_ar, train=128 independent episodes
  F2: Van der Pol, train=128 independent episodes
  F3: Duffing, train=128 independent episodes

All three primary contrasts use the common validation-selected affine/ridge
output head, with its intercept and training-only feature standardization.
The flexible-head results are secondary. The compared encodings and any
finite budgets are precisely defined in the executable protocol.

For every setting and confirmation replicate r, define improvement as
control MSE minus candidate MSE, so a positive value favors the candidate.

  G: matched affine reconstruction + phi
       MINUS KAHM reconstruction + phi.
     This estimates the KAHM reconstruction-operator advantage under the
     otherwise shared pipeline. It does not establish universal superiority.

  N: KAHM + phi
       MINUS KAHM + phi augmented with the log normalizer.
     This tests the effect of retaining absolute score scale. At an affine
     head, it can reflect information and/or decoder accessibility; the
     score-vector and flexible-head diagnostics help distinguish these.

  L: KAHM + phi
       MINUS KAHM + the protocol-defined local-coordinate augmentation.
     This tests whether retaining within-group positional information improves
     the fitted forecast. The additional dimension and computation are reported.

Two additional paired target-alignment contrasts use the same branch bank
within each replicate. At each c in {0,1}, and train=1024, define

  I_c = G(c,lambda=0) - G(c,lambda=1).

A positive I_c means the KAHM reconstruction-operator advantage is larger
for the regime target than for the within-branch target. This directly tests
the difference of advantages; a significant result in one cell and an
inconclusive result in another would not establish this interaction. There
is no primary interaction test comparing the two curvature values.

There are exactly 9*3+2=29 primary paired contrasts. The simultaneous family
contains every one of them, including unfavorable and inconclusive outcomes.
No choice of the most favorable setting, decoder, or encoding defines a
primary comparison after seeing results.

4. ESTIMAND, UNCERTAINTY, AND MULTIPLICITY

For a primary contrast, let D_r be its paired test-MSE improvement in replicate
r, r=1,...,24. Report the mean Dbar, unbiased standard deviation s_D,
standard error s_D/sqrt(24), and interval

  Dbar +/- t_(23, 1 - 0.05/(2*29)) * s_D/sqrt(24).

These are two-sided Bonferroni-adjusted paired-replicate Student t intervals.
The intended family confidence level is 95% under the paired-normal model;
with nonnormal replicate differences their coverage is approximate. They are
not distribution-free risk certificates and are not applications of the
manuscript's bounded-loss certificate hierarchy. The report must state this
distinction prominently. Bonferroni does not require independent contrasts,
but each per-contrast t interval still relies on its own distributional model
or an adequate normal approximation. Twenty-four observations do not prove
that approximation. Inspect and display replicate variability and influential
replicates without removing them because of their outcome.

Different contrasts can share trained banks, test observations, and targets.
That dependence is intentional pairing and does not require treating their
rows as independent. The independent replication unit for these intervals
is the complete train/validation/test replicate, not an overlapping window,
horizon, test point, candidate fit, or reused target mixture.

Because synthetic data are regenerated independently in each replicate, the
reported mean includes training/validation randomness and finite test-sample
variation under the specified generator. It does not estimate a population of
real-world forecasting tasks. For any separate natural-data follow-up using
one fixed test cohort, variation over fitting seeds is conditional on that
cohort and must not be presented as independent population replication.

The frozen practical-effect threshold in each setting is

  delta_setting = 0.05 * mean(pilot raw-input ridge test MSEs),

where the mean uses exactly the two pilot replicates for that setting. Each
interaction's practical threshold is the arithmetic mean of its lambda=0
and lambda=1 cell thresholds; equivalently, 5% of the mean of the four
raw-ridge pilot MSEs at its two endpoints. These
pilot-derived numerical thresholds are fixed before confirmation. They are
study-design thresholds, not externally validated application requirements.
Conditional on the pilot, they are fixed comparison thresholds. Their
estimation uncertainty is not hidden or described as a known population scale.
The unrounded threshold and both pilot MSEs must be retained in the manifest.

Use the same simultaneous interval to classify each primary contrast:
  lower endpoint > delta_setting: evidence of a practical benefit under the
    declared margin and parametric interval assumptions;
  lower endpoint > 0, but not > delta_setting: evidence of a positive effect,
    with a practical benefit at the declared threshold unresolved;
  upper endpoint < 0: evidence of harm under the declared protocol;
  otherwise: direction unresolved.

An interval lying entirely inside [-delta_setting,+delta_setting] may be
described as supporting an effect within that prespecified practical band
under the same interval assumptions. A nonsignificant comparison by itself
does not establish equivalence, adequacy, or absence of an effect.

Report all absolute test MSEs and paired improvements. A descriptive relative
improvement may divide a mean difference by a clearly identified mean control
MSE, but a ratio of means is not automatically covered by the difference
interval. Do not attach an uncomputed ratio confidence interval to it.

5. PRESPECIFIED SECONDARY DIAGNOSTICS

The following are secondary unless a separate fresh-data protocol is frozen:
  - Branch training size 256 and comparisons of sample-size trends.
  - Flexible heads on the same frozen feature banks.
  - Full score vectors and score-vector/phi-plus-log-normalizer comparisons.
  - Raw-input ridge and flexible baselines, and other matched geometric baselines.
  - Individual forecasting horizons and episode distributions.
  - Branch conditional-mean MSE and the known noise variance.
  - Reflection-distance diagnostics and retention of S or T.
  - Reference/group balance, reconstruction residuals, feature conditioning,
    coordinate counts, fit/transform/head time, and numerical failures.

Secondary point estimates and uncertainty summaries are clearly labelled.
Unadjusted intervals do not authorize a claim that the best secondary result
is confirmed. Do not turn a selected favorable secondary contrast into a
primary conclusion. A paired bootstrap over whole confirmation replicates,
if computed with a fixed resampling seed, is only a sensitivity diagnostic;
percentile bootstrap intervals are not simultaneous and do not repair a
failed parametric assumption. There is no bootstrap requirement.

6. NUMERICAL FAILURES, EXCLUSIONS, AND REPRODUCIBILITY

Retain every prescribed attempt, its status, and its random identifiers.
Do not silently drop failed methods or replace difficult replicates.
Deterministic retries for technical interruptions may reuse the exact same
frozen inputs and configuration; document both attempts. A substantive code
bug requires an explicit audit record, preserved earlier outputs, amended
source hashes, and complete rerunning of every affected cell. If a correction
was informed by confirmation performance, report it as such and obtain new
independent confirmation streams for inferential claims.

No primary t interval is produced from a silently reduced replicate count.
If any of a contrast's planned 24 paired outcomes is unavailable, mark that
contrast incomplete. A separate descriptive incomplete-case summary may be
shown, with the missingness and its potential bias explained. Zero-variance
paired differences yield a degenerate t interval mechanically; report that
fact without claiming robustness to unobserved variability.

Archive per-replicate selected hyperparameters, validation scores, test MSEs,
episode summaries where feasible, feature dimensions, timings, diagnostic
values, configuration and source hashes, environment versions, and generator
seed identities. Aggregate reported tables directly from these records.
An independent analyzer checks pairing, completeness, primary-family size,
pilot/confirmation separation, margin provenance, and interval arithmetic.

7. CLAIM BOUNDARIES

This study measures specified implementations under specified synthetic laws.
It does not show that all KAHM models have an invariant-score bottleneck,
that every normalized affinity discards useful information, or that a null
contrast proves KAHM and affine geometries equivalent. Better local-coordinate
results do not establish a favorable accuracy-computation tradeoff unless
the added dimensions, memory, and timing are included. Stronger flexible-head
results diagnose the joint representation/head construction, not the Bayes
information loss without further argument.

Theoretical identities in THEORETICAL_NOTE.txt are mathematical explanations,
not new discoveries established by this experiment. Any approximate-symmetry
inequality has explicit population and decoder-budget assumptions. The
experiment does not claim novelty for the basic normalization identity or
the generic Lipschitz reconstruction-to-risk bound.
