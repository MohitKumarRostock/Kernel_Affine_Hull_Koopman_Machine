\begin{abstract}
Kernel Affine Hull Machines (KAHMs) reconstruct observations from local
reference sets. We study the conditional value of these reconstructions
as nonlinear features for finite, regularized forecasting decoders.
Normalized target transfer is a ratio of kernel-ridge predictions.
We give sufficient conditions for equality of KAHM and matched
radial-basis-function (RBF) augmented feature spans, and an irregular
reference construction for which the spans differ. Bounded features
yield uniform magnitude bounds for both a free-intercept residual head
and a centered correction around a fixed common forecast. These bounds
are generic ridge results and do not guarantee accuracy.
The principal experiment compares ten procedures on 192 simulated task
suites with 24 independent repetition aggregates. With separately
selected local dimensions, KAHM reduces nonlinear standardized mean
squared error by \DFGainCommon\% against common features and
\DFGainRawRBF\% against raw-input RBF, establishing the declared 3\%
margins. The margins against independently tuned RBF departures and
boosted trees are not established, and a non-KAHM policy has lower error.
A preprocessing follow-up does not confirm a general tanh benefit.
A computational reproduction of a separately specified residual study
holds a full-training common forecast and shared bias fixed.
Cross-fitted KAHM reduces baseline nonlinear loss by \CFRGainBias\%, but does not
establish the prescribed advantage over independent RBF or the expanded
policy. In secondary comparisons, in-sample KAHM improves its baseline
by \CFRISGainBias\% and independent in-sample RBF by \CFRISGainRBF\%,
while remaining above the policy's mean loss. The reproduction adds no
independent repetitions. The studies support geometry-dependent residual
features with conditional forecasting value; a general KAHM-specific
advantage and natural-series adoption remain unestablished.
\end{abstract}
