\section{A follow-up comparison of KAHM input scaling}
\label{sec:input-scaling}

\input{tables/input_scaling_numbers}

The preceding experiments standardize each history coordinate using its
training mean and standard deviation before fitting the geometric dictionaries.
The raw observations are already bounded by $[-1,1]$, but the standardized
histories need not lie in that interval. We therefore conducted a separate
follow-up to test whether KAHMs benefit from scaling the training histories to
$[-1,1]$. This is an empirical question about preprocessing: the reconstruction
regularization and folding score in the reused KAHM implementation depend on
input magnitudes and geometry. Bounds on the forecast target alone do not
specify the input scale seen by a KAHM.

\subsection{A fixed intervention and two types of replication}

For coordinate $j$ of a history vector $x$, let $x_j$ denote its value, and let
$a_j$ and $b_j$ be the minimum and maximum over all training histories. These
extrema are computed before subsampling histories for geometry fitting. The
primary alternative to standardization is the min--max transformation
\begin{equation}
 z_j(x)=
 \begin{cases}
  2\dfrac{x_j-a_j}{b_j-a_j}-1,&b_j>a_j,\\[4pt]
  0,&b_j=a_j.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:input-minmax}
\end{equation}
The training extrema are then held fixed for validation, calibration, and
testing. An unseen value outside the training range can therefore map outside
$[-1,1]$; the primary preprocessing variant, or \emph{arm}, does not clip it.
A diagnostic arm clips every transformed query coordinate to the interval,
using $\widetilde z_j(x)=\max\{-1,\min\{1,z_j(x)\}\}$. A second diagnostic
arm uses the bounded raw histories without standardization. Coordinates that
are constant in training map to zero in both min--max arms. No extrema are
estimated from validation data, test data, or a completed test trajectory.
Together with standardization, these alternatives give four preprocessing arms.

The intervention changes only the inputs to KAHM geometry and to models built
from it: retained-block, single-Frobenius-budget, duplicate-block,
hard-assignment, and feature-rollout models. The non-KAHM feature families,
forecast targets, output clipping, coefficient budgets, ridge coefficient,
affinity exponent, history lengths, origins, sample counts, local-point limits,
and random streams remain unchanged. Within each arm, each model family selects
its history length and dictionary stage by mean validation MSE across horizons.
Test error is not used to choose among preprocessing arms.

The first comparison reruns every original fitting seed and coefficient-budget
condition, pairing the four arms on identical data. Because the scaling
hypothesis was proposed after inspection of the original study, these reused
streams provide a matched follow-up comparison; they are not a new test set.
The primary outcome, alternatives, selection rule, and complete schedule were
recorded before the new scaling results were examined. All outcomes are
retained, including regressions. The exact-risk two-atom experiment and the
analytic duplicate-invariance illustration do not fit KAHM input geometry,
so neither admits this scaling intervention.

For confirmation, we fixed the same four arms and used fitting seeds
$4701$--$4703$ on all six synthetic systems. The original stream-offset rule
then generates new training, validation, calibration, and test episodes, with
the original sample counts and main-study budget $\kappa=4$. This adds 18
paired synthetic repetitions, without tuning between the replay and confirmation
stages. For PenDigits, the same three new seeds select new training subsamples
from the existing development writers. The validation writers and held-out
writer cohort remain unchanged. Thus the PenDigits confirmation assesses
variation across training subsamples on a fixed cohort, rather than variation
across newly sampled writers.

Duffing systems in both stages. The Duffing reduction is
\ScalingDuffingOriginalMagnitude\% on the original seeds and
\ScalingDuffingConfirmationMagnitude\% on the new seeds. The nonlinear

by \ScalingLogisticOriginalMagnitude\% and
\ScalingLogisticConfirmationMagnitude\%, and Van der Pol error increases by
\ScalingVDPOriginalMagnitude\% and \ScalingVDPConfirmationMagnitude\%.
An improvement averaged over horizons need not hold at every horizon. For

\input{tables/input_scaling_uncertainty}

A fitting repetition includes model construction and evaluation; a test episode
is only one observation used to evaluate its fitted model.
Table~\ref{tab:input-scaling-uncertainty} distinguishes these two sources of
variation. Here $\Delta$ is min--max MSE minus standardized-input MSE for a
repetition; $\overline\Delta$ and $s_\Delta$ are its mean and sample standard
deviation across complete fitting repetitions. The quantity
$\mathrm{SE}_{\rm ep}$ is the
standard error due to the finite number of independent test episodes,
conditional on the fitted, validation-selected models. To compute the latter,
we subtract the two models' losses on each common episode, average over horizons
within the episode, and estimate the variance of each repetition's mean paired
difference. We then combine those variances to obtain the standard error of
the mean across repetitions. Many test episodes can make this conditional
standard error small without providing many independent model fits.

The mean MSE change depends on the magnitude of each seed's effect and can
therefore have the opposite sign to the majority of individual effects. The
archive retains every seed's sign and effect size. Neither a small conditional
standard error nor a favorable mean establishes that scaling will improve a
newly fitted model.

\input{tables/input_scaling_pendigits}

The largest relative gain in the primary comparison occurs for the retained
soft KAHM predictor on PenDigits. Its cohort MSE, averaged over coordinates
and horizons, decreases by \ScalingPenOriginalMagnitude\% in the replay and
\ScalingPenConfirmationMagnitude\% with the new training subsamples.
Table~\ref{tab:input-scaling-pendigits} reports horizon-specific RMSE and its
variation across fitting seeds. All three horizons improve for each coordinate
and each fitting seed in both stages. Every held-out writer also has a smaller
mean loss, averaged over coordinates and horizons, in each seed. These
comparisons repeatedly evaluate the same 13 writers and do not establish a
claim about a population of independently sampled writers. Quadratic
regression and ridge AR remain more accurate than the scaled retained KAHM
predictor on this task; its one-step error also remains above persistence.

The gain does not extend to every predictor built from KAHM features. On
PenDigits, the hard-assignment model's all-horizon MSE increases by
\ScalingHardPenOriginalIncrease\% on the original seeds and
\ScalingHardPenConfirmationIncrease\% on the new seeds, with degradation in
every fitting repetition. The complete records also retain the other KAHM
ablations and rollouts. These results support a scaling benefit for particular
soft representations under the stated protocol, rather than for all KAHM
models.

As a separate secondary outcome, KAHM feature rollout has smaller mean
all-horizon MSE on \ScalingRolloutOriginalImprovingTasks\ of seven original
tasks and \ScalingRolloutConfirmationImprovingTasks\ of seven new-seed tasks.
The new-seed reductions include \ScalingRolloutVDPConfirmationMagnitude\%
on Van der Pol, \ScalingRolloutDuffingConfirmationMagnitude\% on Duffing,
and \ScalingRolloutPenConfirmationMagnitude\% on PenDigits. Logistic rollout
instead worsens by \ScalingRolloutLogisticConfirmationMagnitude\%.

\subsection{Diagnostic arms, coefficient budgets, and certification}

\input{tables/input_scaling_diagnostics}
\input{tables/input_scaling_sensitivity}

Clipped and unclipped min--max scaling give very similar results for the
retained KAHM family in this study. Using raw bounded histories can produce a
different change in accuracy (Table~\ref{tab:input-scaling-diagnostics}). Both
diagnostic arms retain the logistic and Van der Pol limitations. Whether a
history lies in $[-1,1]$ is therefore insufficient to predict an improvement.
The raw-history arm also removes centering and variance normalization, whereas
min--max scaling changes both location and coordinate scale. The study does
not isolate the contribution of each internal component of KAHM geometry.

Table~\ref{tab:input-scaling-budget} reports every original coefficient-budget
condition. At $\kappa=1$, the mean effects are small and the signs differ across
seeds. At $\kappa=4$ and 16, the quasiperiodic comparison improves in all three
repetitions, while the logistic comparison worsens in all three. The harmonic
mean change has different signs at different budgets. Neither the budget nor
the preprocessing arm is selected by test performance. These results limit
extrapolation of a gain from one coefficient class to another.

Improved empirical accuracy need not change an endpoint-based decision. At
each recorded RMSE tolerance, $0.20$, $0.35$, and $0.50$, standardization and
primary min--max scaling yield the same number of repetitions whose retained
KAHM upper endpoints meet the requirement at every horizon. This equality
holds for every original synthetic system and budget, and for every synthetic
confirmation system. PenDigits retains the same small number of writer units
and its uninformative nominal endpoints. The original failure allocations
account for multiplicity within each preprocessing arm, rather than selection
across all four arms. A confidence interpretation also requires the arm to
have been specified independently of its calibration outcomes. For the
original-seed replay, fixing the scaling protocol before rerunning previously
used streams does not by itself establish that independence; these replayed
endpoints are therefore reported as nominal calculations. The fresh-seed
synthetic confirmation uses new calibration episodes and supports the stated
within-arm guarantees. PenDigits still reuses the same
writers, so new fitting seeds alone do not establish independence from earlier
writer-level results. The cross-arm accuracy comparisons are therefore not
certified preprocessing improvements.

\paragraph{Reproducibility and interpretation.}
The standardization replay reproduces every retained non-timing numerical
result of the original campaigns exactly. Across preprocessing arms, all
non-KAHM numerical results and saved synthetic score arrays are unchanged;
writer-level control losses are also unchanged. Four truncated score archives,
two in the original replay and two in synthetic confirmation, were regenerated
using the same fixed source, configuration, and seeds. Their non-timing result
records and candidate descriptions agree exactly. The archive retains hashes
of the damaged and regenerated files and records of each replay. Reporting
followed archive-integrity checks and independent aggregation of the selected
models' test scores.

Standardization remains the reference preprocessing for the original
experiments. The repeated PenDigits and Duffing gains, together with the
logistic and Van der Pol regressions, show that the benefit of input scaling
depends on the task and prediction architecture.
