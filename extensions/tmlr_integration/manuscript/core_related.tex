\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Local geometry and reconstruction.}
Prediction from delay coordinates using local approximation predates
the present representations \citep{Farmer1987Prediction}. KAHMs were
introduced for geometrically informed classification, including a
differential-privacy application \citep{Kumar2024KAHM}, and developed
for collaborative learning \citep{Kumar2025Collaborative}. Those task
results do not establish forecasting utility. Local coordinate coding
connects localized representations with approximation of smooth
functions \citep{Yu2009LCC}. Local tangent coding adds weighted
principal-direction displacements \citep{Yu2010LTC}; these are the common
features retained in our matched comparisons. With linear readouts they
are also related to local affine experts \citep{Jacobs1991Experts}.
Our question is whether the additional KAHM reconstruction departures
change the available functions and improve a specified forecasting
procedure beyond these common features.

\paragraph{Kernel regression and predictive representations.}
The matched positive-kernel reconstruction is a vector-valued kernel
smoother that averages reference coordinates with normalized Gaussian
weights \citep{Nadaraya1964Regression}. The target-transfer identity in
Proposition~\ref{prop:mechanism-krr} relates KAHM to kernel ridge
regression, while Propositions~\ref{prop:mechanism-redundancy}
and~\ref{prop:mechanism-distinct} characterize equality and inequality of
the corresponding augmented raw feature spans. Reconstruction and
forecasting objectives also occur in learned dynamical representations:
\citet{Lusch2018Embeddings} combine reconstruction, latent linearity,
and multistep prediction, whereas \citet{Liu2023Koopa} optimize a
residual Koopman forecaster without the canonical reconstruction loss.
Here the principal experiment fixes the geometric bank and compares
its departures with a matched RBF reconstruction under identical
feature transformations and a restricted residual decoder.

\paragraph{Information, regularization, and residual fitting.}
Supervised dimension reduction studies preservation of the input--target
relationship \citep{Fukumizu2004SDR}, and predictive
$\mathcal V$-information distinguishes information accessible to a
restricted observer \citep{Xu2020Usable}. Our feature-space analysis
addresses a finite decoder and does not estimate unrestricted Bayes
risk on natural data. Residual fitting under squared loss is an
established construction \citep{Buhlmann2003L2}. The correction bound
in Proposition~\ref{prop:mechanism-correction-bound} follows from the
singular values of centered ridge regression and is not specific to
KAHM. Nystr\"om approximation changes the available coordinates and
computational cost \citep{Williams2001Nystrom,Rudi2015Nystrom}; the raw
RBF comparator is therefore evaluated as a complete fitted procedure,
not as a presumed equivalent function class.

\paragraph{Evaluation and relation to prediction-limit studies.}
Model-selection bias \citep{Cawley2010Selection} and forecast-evaluation
pitfalls \citep{Hewamalage2023Evaluation} motivate distinct fitting,
selection, calibration, and evaluation roles. The supporting natural-data
studies use the Monash archive \citep{Godahewa2021Monash} and are reported
separately from the principal simulated experiment. Related work by
\citet{KumarKargaran2026Limits} studies successor soft coordinates,
norm-bounded prediction certificates, and Koopman closure. The present
study shares KAHM source components and some generator definitions with
that work, but evaluates particular finite forecasting procedures against
matched alternatives. Its reconstruction-span results and correction
bound are established under their own assumptions. The paired-error
intervals and measured costs do not provide class-wide impossibility
results or minimum-cost certificates.
