INDEPENDENT SCIENTIFIC AND STATISTICAL REVIEW
Scope: manuscript and paper-asset construction from the completed KAHM study
Date: 2026-10-30

1. REVIEW CONCLUSION

The archived experiment supports a coherent, conditional research claim.
Its controlled contribution is a reconstruction/encoding/decoder separation:
near-exact affine reconstruction can induce an almost reflection-insensitive
score code, while less accurate KAHM reconstruction induces useful positional
variation. Affine locality controls and explicit local coordinates greatly
reduce that gap. The forecasting contribution is different: a particular
KAHM local-coordinate representation with a finite nonlinear head has a
fresh-sample Van der Pol advantage at two tested landmark budgets. It does
not win generally on the other laws. The paper should preserve both findings
and their distinction.

The title's word 'when' must be read as conditions observed in the declared
experiments, not a validated diagnostic for new tasks. The manuscript now
explicitly leaves such a predictive criterion open. The elementary supporting
propositions explain mechanisms and scope; they are not claimed to be new
KAHM approximation rates or unique properties of KAHM.

4. MATHEMATICAL AND STATISTICAL SCOPE

The methods appendix supplies proofs of the conditional-mean risk identity,
score/log-normalizer invertibility, exact and approximate symmetry statements,
the reconstruction-through-code upper bound, and common kernel-row scaling.
Exact score invariance requires equivariant reconstruction and preserved
group identities. The approximate-symmetry bound requires a declared metric,
a known Lipschitz budget, and a population reflection discrepancy. Empirical
diagnostics plus unconstrained fitted heads do not meet those requirements.

The reconstruction upper bound needs a reconstruction h(U) through the code
received by the predictor and a Lipschitz conditional mean on its domain.
Small raw-query reconstruction error alone does not supply that property.

The 29-family and 12-family intervals are separate. Under independent normal
paired differences, individual t intervals have exact nominal coverage and
Bonferroni gives family coverage at least95%. Otherwise coverage is approximate.
No joint95% statement across both stages is warranted. Margins are study-design
scales, not externally meaningful application thresholds. No prospective power
calculation is claimed. Significance at one budget and not the other does not
test a budget interaction. All target-mixture and method dependence is handled
by pairing; overlapping windows, horizons, or repeated fits do not supply
independent rows for primary inference. No primary pseudoreplication was found.

5. MATERIAL PUBLICATION LIMITS

A. The positive generalization evidence is for one specified numerical
   forecasting law, observation function, sample-size regime, horizon set,
   feature bank, and finite tuning grid. Natural forecasting evidence is absent.
   Three synthetic laws are not independent representatives of all ML tasks.

B. There is no validated pre-task geometric criterion predicting KAHM benefit.
   The branch mechanism does not by itself explain the Van der Pol advantage.
   A stronger follow-up would freeze such a predictor and test it on held-out
   tasks with deliberately varied dynamics, sensors, noise, and sample sizes.

C. The matched comparison is useful for component attribution, but not for a
   claim against all well-tuned affine/local learning or raw-input estimators.
   The native affine locality control itself shows the limitation. Stronger
   raw and local model comparisons would be needed for a broad performance claim.

D. The supporting mathematics is generic and elementary. Publication novelty
   rests on the controlled KAHM investigation, adverse cases, independent
   follow-up, and reproducible artifact; it should not be sold as a new
   general learning principle. This review does not assert an exhaustive
   novelty search or predict journal acceptance.

E. Numerical sensitivity removes one concrete underflow explanation, not every
   conditioning risk. Signed denominator cancellation remains a distinct issue.
   Changes to a future deployed feature map require consistent fitting and
   validation and should not silently replace historical results.

F. Timing shares intermediate computations and is not a standalone efficiency
   benchmark. Equal landmark counts do not establish compute fairness or a
   performance/compute frontier. The paper appropriately avoids these claims.

The manuscript is suitable as an internally reviewable research draft with
these limits explicit. Author review remains necessary for framing, attribution,
prior-work overlap, author metadata, and the intended submission venue. No
additional external action or submission is implied by this audit.
