\section{Introduction}
Forecasting requires a representation that preserves distinctions relevant to future observations and allows a predictive model to use them. Geometry-induced soft-state abstractions describe an input through graded relationships with reference regions. When these relationships are obtained from reconstruction scores, they have a direct geometric interpretation: a reference set receives greater affinity when it reconstructs the query more successfully. That interpretation alone does not explain whether the resulting coordinates support prediction of an observed future response.

This gap motivates the present study. Reducing a reconstruction vector to a score can discard position within a reference region, and normalizing scores into memberships removes their overall affinity scale. Conversely, an enriched encoding can preserve the input while requiring an inverse that a chosen linear or finite nonlinear decoder does not implement well. Information loss in the encoding and limited access through the fitted decoder are therefore distinct obstacles. Distinguishing them matters when deciding whether to enrich a representation, change the readout, or prefer a simpler construction.

We investigate these obstacles using Kernel Affine Hull Machine (KAHM) reconstruction scores as a concrete family of soft coordinates \citep{Kumar2024KAHM,Kumar2025Collaborative,KumarKargaran2026Limits}. The central question is which distinctions the coordinates retain, what enrichment restores, and under which specified representation--decoder procedures prediction improves. We compare memberships alone, memberships with partition log normalizers, memberships with gated local principal coordinates, and the full score vector. Normalizers retain absolute affinity information, whereas local coordinates retain position; these are parallel enrichments rather than successive stages of one hierarchy. Matched affine and positive-kernel reconstructions test whether an observed benefit is specific to KAHM, and raw histories provide a separate forecasting control. All procedures predict the same observed response.

The analysis characterizes score recovery, input recovery, and conditioning. It also gives a full-rank affine example in which reconstruction is exact but memberships carry no position information. The experiment tests complete representation--decoder procedures with matched reconstruction controls and validation-selected dimension, retaining all four laws and both decoders in one primary family. A separate raw-span comparison describes a limited implication for candidate Koopman observable spaces; it is not a closure theorem for the nonlinear score encodings.

The empirical findings separate enrichment from reconstruction-specific advantage. Local position improves KAHM memberships in six of eight law--decoder comparisons, but all four nonlinear-decoder comparisons with positive-kernel reconstruction remain unresolved. Unfavorable linear-autoregression and Duffing results are retained.

\paragraph{Relation to prior constructions.}
Local prediction from delay coordinates predates the present representation \citep{Farmer1987Prediction}. Local coordinate coding and local tangent coding motivate localized weighted displacements \citep{Yu2009LCC,Yu2010LTC}; the local-position block is an application of that established design. The distinction between information and access through a restricted predictor is also central to usable-information theory \citep{Xu2020Usable}. We do not claim a new general information principle or an optimal-decoder estimate. The positive-kernel reconstruction is a normalized vector-valued kernel smoother \citep{Nadaraya1964Regression}, while normalized inverse-kernel constructions have an antecedent in rescaled interpolation \citep{DeMarchi2017Rescaled}. The nonlinear decoder is a finite Nystr\"om approximation with an additional linear input, rather than unrestricted kernel regression \citep{Williams2001Nystrom}.

\paragraph{Distinction from fixed-abstraction certification and dynamical learning.}
\citet{KumarKargaran2026Limits} study lower prediction-error certificates for a fixed soft map and a prescribed norm-bounded linear predictor class, together with the conditional interpretation of exact coordinate propagation. Our target is instead a common future observation, and our comparison varies the representation, dimension selection, and fitted decoder. Those successor-coordinate certificates do not imply improved external-response prediction. Methods such as VAMPnets and learned Koopman embeddings explicitly model latent evolution \citep{Mardt2018VAMPnets,Lusch2018Embeddings}; our direct horizon-specific readouts do not. The present study concerns observable design and predictive access, not an empirical demonstration of autonomous state dynamics.
