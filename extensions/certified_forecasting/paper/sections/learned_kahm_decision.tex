\section{Certified decisions for learned KAHM representations}
\label{sec:learned-kahm-decision}

We ask whether class-level decisions can arise when the representation and
the pairing partitions are learned by the actual KAHM implementation.
Section~\ref{sec:decision-value} separately examines explicit feature maps.
This is a constructed mechanism experiment with continuous histories and fresh
independent episodes. It tests the connection between the theory and learned
geometry; it does not estimate how often the method succeeds on natural
forecasting tasks.

\paragraph{A smooth two-lag product and an irreducible control.}
Each history is $X=(X_1,X_2)$, where independently
$X_j=S_j(0.9+V_j)$, $S_j$ is uniform on $\{-1,1\}$, and
$V_j\sim\operatorname{Unif}[-0.1,0.1]$. The repairable one-step target is
\[
 Y=0.8X_1X_2+E,\qquad E\sim\operatorname{Unif}[-0.05,0.05],
\]
with independent noise. This is a smooth nonlinear second-order recurrence
at the declared forecast origin, with four modes in the initial-history law.
In the irreducible control, an independent history $\widetilde X$, unavailable
to the predictor, replaces $X$ in the target formula. In both regimes
$Y\in[-0.85,0.85]$. We fix the MSE tolerance at $0.10$ in these target units,
after five development-only pilot seeds and before any confirmatory
calibration data. It is a study-design tolerance, not an external application
requirement. The noise variance is $0.05^2/3$; the irreducible control has
Bayes risk $0.8^2(0.9^2+0.1^2/3)^2+0.05^2/3$. Neither reference value enters
model selection or a reported confidence endpoint.

\paragraph{Learned classes and frozen calibration.}
Each of 20 independently generated trials per regime uses 2,048 training,
1,024 validation, and 16,384 calibration episodes, with one origin and one
future observation per episode. Training-only coordinate min--max scaling,
as defined in Section~\ref{sec:input-scaling}, precedes geometry fitting;
frozen extrema transform later histories without clipping. KAHM blocks have
two and four clusters, association exponent $16$, retained subspace dimension
$2$, and at most 48 local references per cluster. The first class uses the
two-coordinate block; the second retains it and appends the four-coordinate
block. Thus their declared costs are $2$ and $6$. Both use the common
sum-of-block-norms budget $\kappa=2$ and the corresponding maximum-block
distance. The cost comparison concerns these two classes and counts retained
coordinates, not runtime or all possible forecasting methods.

For each class, validation selects among ridge penalties
$0$, $10^{-5}$, and $10^{-3}$. All 40 feature banks, 240 candidate fits, and
80 selected heads are recorded, and every selection is frozen before any
calibration episode is generated. Twenty-two candidate fits, including nine
selected fits, reach the 20,000-iteration limit before the strict $10^{-9}$
objective-gap criterion; their largest gaps are $5.35\times10^{-9}$ and
$2.21\times10^{-9}$, respectively. All coefficient budgets remain feasible.
The risk guarantees require fixed feasible forecasts, not exact optimization.

Two history-only partitions take the argmax of the frozen two-cluster or
four-cluster association block. No generated quadrant labels or future targets
define these partitions. We compute unconditional and both stratified lower
certificates, accounting for estimated stratum masses, at calibration sizes
256, 1,024, 4,096, and 16,384. A total failure allocation of $0.05$ covers
\emph{both regimes, all trials, all sample-size prefixes, both classes, and all
reported bounds jointly}: $0.01$ for upper endpoints, $0.01$ for unconditional
lower endpoints, and $0.03$ for the two stratified families. The latter split
each partition's allocation equally between mass estimation and conditional
mean bounds. All designs, sample sizes, tolerances, and partitions remain fixed
after calibration starts.

\paragraph{What the lower information changes.}
At 1,024 episodes, upper calibration accepts the six-coordinate forecast in
all 20 repairable trials. Stratified lower calibration additionally excludes
the cheaper class in 17 trials, certifying minimum feasible cost six; the
unconditional lower certificate does so in none. At 4,096 episodes both lower
constructions establish this conclusion in all 20 trials. In the irreducible
control, stratification excludes both classes in all 20 trials at 4,096 and
16,384 episodes, whereas unconditional lower calibration never establishes
joint infeasibility at the prescribed sample sizes. Every trial at 256
episodes remains unresolved. Table~\ref{tab:learned-kahm-decision} retains
these small-sample failures.

At 16,384 episodes, the repairable six-coordinate forecast has mean empirical
MSE $0.00431$ and mean upper endpoint $0.00906$ across trials. Its cheaper
class has stratified lower endpoints between $0.2513$ and $0.3905$. In the
irreducible control, the six-coordinate class has stratified lower endpoints
between $0.3441$ and $0.3657$, while its unconditional lower endpoints remain
between $0.0808$ and $0.0929$, below the tolerance. The choice of conditioning
still matters: the four-cluster partition yields zero lower endpoints for
the cheaper class in the repairable regime because it removes the relevant
target contrast. The prespecified two-cluster partition retains that contrast.
Thus stratification has decision value here, but finer conditioning is not
uniformly preferable. These outcomes supply a learned-KAHM example of the
class-exclusion mechanism; the natural-data results and their unresolved
lower certificates retain their separate scope.
