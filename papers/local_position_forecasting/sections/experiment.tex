
\section{Matched forecasting experiment}\label{sec:experiment}
\subsection{Simulation design and validation-selected dimension}
The design contains 32 independent repetitions of four observation laws at two added-noise levels, giving 256 task suites. The nonlinear laws are a bounded nonlinear autoregression and noisy observations of Van der Pol and Duffing oscillators. A linear autoregression is a separate control. The supplement specifies their equations, initialization, numerical integration, burn-in, and sensor noise. The laws were used in prior methodological studies; these are new repetitions under known laws, not previously unexamined domains.

Each law and repetition provides independent A/B/C/D sets with $64/32/32/128$ episodes of length 72. For both noise levels, the same base episodes are used. Added noise is $\eta s_A e_t$, where $\eta\in\{0,0.1\}$, the $e_t$ are independent standard normal variables, and $s_A$ is the population standard deviation of the base A observations. The same noise realization is paired across competitors and noise levels. The oscillator observations have sensor noise even at $\eta=0$.

Histories have width $d=12$, and $\mathcal H=\{1,4,12\}$. Zero-based origins are $11,15,\ldots,59$. Uniform sampling without replacement retains at most $512/256/256$ rows on A/B/C; D retains all 1,664 origins. Overlapping origins within an episode are dependent. They are not treated as independent inferential units.

Each candidate bank uses three $k$-means partitions requesting 2, 4, and 8 groups, with at most 24 reference histories per group. For a dimension cap $c$, the native local construction starts at $\min\{c,d,n_j-1\}$ and applies its positive-variance and projected-range reductions. There is no extra $d-1$ cap. Candidate caps are $1,2,4,8,12$; cap 20 is identical to cap 12 at $d=12$ and is not an additional search choice. The supplement provides the clustering, regularization, and fallback specifications.

After selecting the penalties for every candidate bank, P selects one cap by minimizing mean B loss across horizons, normalized by the corresponding A target variances. G, K, and R use this same bank with their own B-selected penalties. Exact ties choose the first value in ascending candidate order. This primary comparison evaluates enrichment of a P-selected representation. It is not symmetric between all four procedures. A secondary comparison allows each arm to select its own cap from the same fits; it assesses a different selection procedure and is reported descriptively.

The candidate set, 32-repetition budget, and four-comparison analysis were fixed before production data generation. Earlier outcomes informed the design, but no earlier observations enter these inference units. Fitted states were sealed before D generation, and predictions were sealed before terminal scoring. These records document execution order and source identity; they do not constitute external oversight, public preregistration, or cryptographic target isolation.

\subsection{Loss and primary inference}
Let $\tau$ index a task suite and $m\in\{G,P,K,R\}$ an arm. For horizon $h$, let
\[
 v_{\tau h}=\max\{\Var_{A,\tau}(Y_h),10^{-12}\},\qquad
 L_{\tau m}=\frac13\sum_{h\in\mathcal H}\frac{n_D^{-1}\sum_{i\in D}(y_{ih}-\widehat y_{m,ih})^2}{v_{\tau h}}.
\]
Here $\Var_{A,\tau}$ is the empirical population variance of the retained A targets, $n_D=1664$, and $\widehat y_{m,ih}$ is the direct prediction. We call $L_{\tau m}$ standardized mean squared error (SMSE). Within repetition $r$, $L^{\mathrm{NL}}_{r,m}$ averages the six nonlinear law/noise cells equally, while $L^{\mathrm L}_{r,m}$ averages the two linear cells. The sample size for each comparison is $n=32$ independent repetition aggregates.

The four primary comparisons are nonlinear P versus G, K versus P, and K versus R with a 3\% superiority margin, and linear K versus P with a 5\% noninferiority margin. For the relevant panel, candidate $a$, and reference $b$, define $d_r=L_{r,a}-cL_{r,b}$, where $c=0.97$ for superiority and $c=1.05$ for noninferiority. Define $\bar d=n^{-1}\sum_r d_r$ and $s_d^2=(n-1)^{-1}\sum_r(d_r-\bar d)^2$. The simultaneous one-sided upper endpoint is
\begin{equation}
 U=\bar d+t_{31,\,1-0.05/4}\frac{s_d}{\sqrt{32}},
 \label{eq:inference}
\end{equation}
where $t_{\nu,p}$ is the $p$ quantile of a Student distribution with $\nu$ degrees of freedom. A criterion is established only if $U<0$. Bonferroni adjustment defines one four-comparison family. Its nominal familywise coverage relies on the paired-$t$ approximation for repetition means and is not distribution-free. The margins are research thresholds, not deployment-derived tolerances.

The relative gain is $100(1-\overline L_a/\overline L_b)$, where bars denote mean repetition loss. This ratio of means is not the mean of repetitionwise relative gains. Law, noise, horizon, independently selected caps, and resource analyses are descriptive. Failure to establish a margin is not an equivalence finding.

\subsection{Primary results}
Table~\ref{tab:primary} contains the complete primary family. P reduces nonlinear mean SMSE from 0.0988703 to 0.0304507, a 69.20\% improvement over G. K reduces P's loss to 0.0272660, a further 10.46\% improvement. Both upper endpoints are negative, establishing their respective 3\% margins. Matched R has mean SMSE 0.0272740. K's 0.03\% point improvement over R does not establish its 3\% superiority margin.

On the linear control, P and K have mean SMSE 0.910921 and 0.921045. Thus K is 1.11\% worse at the point-estimate level, although the upper endpoint establishes noninferiority within the declared 5\% tolerance. Three of the four criteria are established. The failed K/R criterion directly limits a KAHM-specific interpretation of the positive K/P result.

\subsection{Heterogeneity and independent cap selection}
The mean improvement does not hold in every condition. K worsens P on nonlinear autoregression at both noise levels, while improving it on both oscillator laws. It improves R in three of the six nonlinear law/noise cells and worsens it in the other three. At horizon one, nonlinear K loss is 0.012639 versus P's 0.012255; at horizon four, the values are 0.019227 and 0.019254; at horizon twelve, they are 0.049932 and 0.059843. The aggregate K/P benefit is therefore concentrated at the longest tested horizon. These results concern separately fitted direct forecasts, not successive steps of a recursive rollout.

Independent cap selection reduces nonlinear K and R losses to 0.0267546 and 0.0267507, respectively (Table~\ref{tab:aggregate}). Their near-equal point losses slightly favor R and do not establish equivalence. G is unchanged because its gates are identical across caps under the shared reference construction; its independent selection of cap one is a tie-breaking outcome. P's independently selected cap is the primary shared cap by definition. The supplement retains all law/noise, horizon, cap-selection, and descriptive paired-comparison results.

\subsection{Dimensions and computational scope}
The selected input widths are 14 for G, 28--182 for P, and 196--350 for K and R, excluding the intercept. The experiment therefore does not demonstrate compression of a twelve-dimensional history. Equal K/R width also does not imply equal effective rank, conditioning, or statistical capacity.

The complete search across all four arms uses 931.88 summed worker wall seconds for fitting and selection, and 671.84 summed worker wall seconds for D prediction. The largest measured worker peak resident memory is 156.99 MiB. These are whole-search measurements, not isolated K/R deployment costs or elapsed parallel runtime. The retained computational audit reports reconstruction of features, independent refitting of the B penalty paths, forecast replay, and recomputation of the primary statistics. It does not independently reimplement the physical-law integrators or partition fitting, and is not an external scientific replication. Audit coverage and tolerances are stated in the supplement.
