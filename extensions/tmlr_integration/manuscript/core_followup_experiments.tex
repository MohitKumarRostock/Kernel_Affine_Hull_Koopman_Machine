\section{Follow-up experiments on preprocessing and residual targets}
\label{sec:followup-experiments}

Two later studies examine choices left open by the principal dimension
experiment: the preprocessing of the geometric histories, and the
construction of a correction around an identical fitted common forecast.
Their procedures, cohorts, and primary confidence families remain separate
from Section~\ref{sec:experiment}. Both use the same four numerical laws,
history width \PPHistory, horizons $1,4,12$, and
\PPTasks\ task suites per cohort. The preprocessing study has
\PPRepetitions\ fresh repetition units. The residual-target results below
come from a computational reproduction of that study's original prescribed
\CFRRepetitions\ units, adding \CFRAdditionalRepetitions\ independent
repetitions. Neither follow-up supplies a new natural-data application
cohort or replaces the principal experiment's six-criterion result.

\subsection{Preprocessing and dimension selection}

The complete-procedure follow-up jointly selects a history transformation
and native local cap on B. The transformations are standardization,
unclipped min--max scaling, and $\tanh$ of standardized histories. Five
common/departure pairs are retained: the three diagonal pairs and both
min--max/tanh hybrids. Caps are $1,2,4,8,12,20$; caps twelve and twenty
coincide at the tested ambient dimension. Each geometric procedure selects
independently, and raw-input RBF also receives a three-scaler search.
All transformations and coefficients are fitted on A; C chooses the
frozen procedure's blend toward raw ridge and the non-KAHM policy.

The bounded KAHM candidate remains a concatenated common-plus-departure
readout fitted to raw-ridge residuals. Sharing its common features with
another method does not force their fitted common coefficients or
intercepts to agree. This complete-procedure comparison therefore addresses
selection and prediction jointly. Table~\ref{tab:followup-pp-primary}
reports all four original primary claims. Selected KAHM has
\PPRBFPenalty\% higher nonlinear loss than independently selected RBF and
\PPPolicyPenalty\% higher loss than the non-KAHM policy. The
tanh/tanh candidate has \PPTanhPenalty\% higher loss than the
standard/standard candidate. Only linear-control noninferiority is
established: \PPPrimaryEstablished\ of \PPPrimaryCount\ criteria.

\paragraph{A separate fixed-common mechanism.}
A prespecified secondary construction divides the
\PPEpisodesA\ A episodes into disjoint groups P and R of thirty-two
episodes each. P fits and B selects a common predictor; R provides its
held-out residual targets. Within each common-scaler row, every correction
shares that exact predictor and the B residual-mean bias. B selects the
departure cap and penalties independently for KAHM and RBF, and C selects
only the incremental correction multiplier. The bounded departure readout
uses centered feature slopes and adds no response-mean intercept.
Table~\ref{tab:followup-pp-fixed} displays every prespecified scaler pair.
KAHM improves the identical bias baseline by
\PPFixedBiasGainMin--\PPFixedBiasGainMax\% and the independently selected
RBF correction by \PPFixedRBFGainMin--\PPFixedRBFGainMax\% across these
cells. These are descriptive mechanism comparisons under a different
training allocation; they do not reverse the complete-procedure primary
result or identify an externally validated best scaler.

\subsection{A full-training common predictor and residual-target ablation}

The next design retains a separately B-selected common forecast
$f_{\mathrm{com},h}$ fitted on all \CFREpisodesA\ A episodes and a shared
bias $\beta_h=\operatorname{mean}_B(Y_h-f_{\mathrm{com},h}(X))$.
Every new correction has the form
\begin{equation}
 \widehat f_{m,h}(x)=f_{\mathrm{base},h}(x)+\eta_{m,h}\delta_{m,h}(x),
 \qquad f_{\mathrm{base},h}=f_{\mathrm{com},h}+\beta_h,
 \quad\eta_{m,h}\in\{0,\tfrac14,\tfrac12,1\}.
 \label{eq:followup-fixed-common}
\end{equation}
Here $\delta_{m,h}$ is a centered, bounded departure-feature ridge slope
with no response-mean intercept. Zero correction recovers the same
$f_{\mathrm{base},h}$ for every method. This differs from the concatenated
complete heads in the preceding studies.

Four folds keep whole A episodes together. For out-of-fold (OOF) targets,
each held-out episode receives a prediction from a common model fitted
and independently B selected on the other forty-eight episodes. The
in-sample ablation instead uses residuals of the full-A common predictor.
Both target modes use the same full-A departure feature banks, feature
statistics, and \CFRRowsA\ correction rows. B independently selects a
departure scaler, cap, and penalties for each target mode and feature
kind; C selects $\eta$ by horizon. Thus the ablation changes the
residual-generation procedure, including its resulting B/C choices.
The OOF target excludes the row's episode from common-model fitting and
selection, but comes from a smaller fitted predictor than the deployed
common model. Cross-fitting alone does not remove that mismatch.

Table~\ref{tab:followup-cfr-primary} preserves the four original primary
comparisons. OOF KAHM improves its common-plus-bias baseline by
\CFRGainBias\%, establishing the 3\% margin. Its \CFRGainRBF\% gain over
independently selected OOF RBF does not establish that margin. Its loss
is \CFRPolicyPenalty\% higher than the expanded non-KAHM policy, which
can select legacy competitors and the new RBF corrections on C. Linear
noninferiority passes, giving \CFRPrimaryEstablished\ of
\CFRPrimaryCount\ established criteria. Passing the separate maximum-error
screen does not change these decisions.

\paragraph{Residual targets and matched controls.}
The in-sample KAHM correction achieves SMSE \CFRISLoss, improving the
shared bias baseline by \CFRISGainBias\% and independently selected
in-sample RBF by \CFRISGainRBF\%. OOF KAHM has
\CFROOFvsISPenalty\% higher loss, with in-sample KAHM lower in
\CFRISWinningRepetitions\ of \CFRRepetitions\ nonlinear repetition aggregates.
The paired difference and its descriptive interval appear in
Table~\ref{tab:followup-cfr-secondary}. The in-sample correction still
has \CFRISPolicyPenalty\% higher aggregate loss than the expanded
policy; this last comparison is a point description, without an added
hypothesis test. Neither the favorable in-sample result nor the
\CFRMatchedRBFGain\% gain over matched OOF RBF substitutes for a failed
primary claim. Matched RBF shares KAHM's selected scaler and cap and
retains its own penalty and calibration choices.

\paragraph{Computational status and scope.}
The original completed residual-study arrays, fitted states, and stage
records were unavailable. The reported reproduction regenerates its
declared seed streams with byte-identical scientific code and unchanged
scientific configuration. Earlier aggregate reports were known before
this execution. Its audit verifies newly retained records, including
all \CFRAuditArrays\ forecast arrays and \CFRAuditBases\ selected basis
occurrences; it does not authenticate the unavailable original execution.
The \CFRSVDRecoveries\ recorded SVD recoveries and
\CFRZeroRanks\ admissible singleton zero-rank bases belong to this
reproduction. The Supplementary Studies specify the complete grids,
allocations, inference, audit scopes, and numerical provenance. Taken
together, the follow-ups support studying KAHM as an optional, validated
correction to a competitive common forecast, while leaving its advantage
over a strong complete forecasting policy unestablished.
