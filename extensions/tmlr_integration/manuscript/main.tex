\begin{abstract}
Kernel Affine Hull Machines (KAHMs) reconstruct observations from local
reference sets. We study whether these reconstructions provide useful
features for a finite-dimensional forecasting decoder. Direct transfer of
normalized KAHM weights to reference targets is a ratio of kernel-ridge
predictions. We give sufficient conditions under which KAHM and matched
radial-basis-function (RBF) reconstructions generate the same augmented
feature span, and construct an irregular reference set for which the spans
differ. Separately, bounded features and a ridge residual decoder yield a
uniform bound on the forecast correction, including its free intercept.
A prospectively specified experiment compares ten procedures on 192 task
suites: three nonlinear simulation laws and a linear control, two
observation-noise levels, and 24 independent repetitions. The bounded
KAHM departure improves aggregate nonlinear normalized mean squared
error by \RMKvsCommonGain\% over common local-coordinate features.
Its gains over matched RBF reconstruction and raw-input RBF forecasting
are \RMKvsRBFDepartureGain\% and \RMKvsRawRBFGain\%; neither establishes
the prespecified 3\% margin under simultaneous inference. Boosted trees
and a validation-selected non-KAHM procedure attain lower aggregate
error. The correction bounds and linear-control noninferiority criterion
are satisfied. Supporting studies identify conditional gains, unfavorable
natural-data comparisons, and limitations of adaptation. Reconstruction
can therefore supply useful forecasting features, but algebraic
distinctness and bounded corrections do not establish a practical
advantage specific to KAHM.
\end{abstract}
