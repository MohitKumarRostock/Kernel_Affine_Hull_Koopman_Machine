\section{Interpretation and limitations}\label{sec:discussion}
The motivating problem is that geometric affinity alone does not establish predictive usefulness. The study separates information retained by an enrichment from the forecasting performance of a specified decoder and from a general preference for one reconstruction formula. Local coordinates can preserve position that a reconstruction score obscures. Their inverse relation explains available information, but the forecasts depend additionally on finite samples, feature scaling, selected dimensions, and regularization. Matched affine and positive-kernel controls are therefore essential: the large improvement over memberships alone is not a KAHM-specific comparison.

Earlier controlled studies examined related questions under different rank policies and decoders. A fixed-input branch intervention distinguishes reconstruction from target-relevant position. A separate 24-repetition capacity study reports favorable Van der Pol outcomes and unfavorable nonlinear-autoregression and Duffing comparisons. Supplement Section~S5 gives their complete comparison families and numerical conventions. These observations are not pooled with the present cohort.

Different downstream architectures also limit application claims. A dimension-selected residual forecaster did not establish superiority over a selected non-KAHM policy, and a fresh policy-augmentation study failed both specified nonlinear superiority margins. On a descriptive panel of 48 M4 training series, KAHM augmentation increased aggregate loss by 2.273\%. These procedures differ from the current score-based readouts. Their adverse findings remain in the supplement, but are not presented as tests of the same representation.

The main evaluation has four known laws, one history width, three horizons, and a single nonlinear landmark budget. There is no real-series evaluation of this exact procedure and no comparison against a comprehensive roster of modern forecasting systems. The goal is a controlled representation comparison, not state-of-the-art forecasting. Nominal paired-Student uncertainty, bounded candidate grids, numerical sensitivity of the inverse encoding, and the absence of an autonomous transition model further limit the conclusions. A larger future grid or a different target population would define a new procedure, not retroactively change these results.

\section{Conclusion}
KAHM reconstruction-score soft coordinates admit two distinct enrichments: retaining partition normalizers recovers absolute score information, while gated local position can preserve the input under explicit rank and positivity conditions. Neither fact alone determines fitted prediction accuracy. The matched evaluation finds useful local enrichment in several settings but conditional and sometimes unfavorable comparisons with affine, positive-kernel, and raw-input controls. Raw reconstruction-span analysis provides a limited connection to candidate Koopman observable spaces; it does not establish closure of the learned score coordinates. The resulting account identifies what the representation retains, how a specified decoder can use it, and where the evidence does not support a broader KAHM advantage.
