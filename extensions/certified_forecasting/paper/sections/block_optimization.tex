\subsection{Matrix-free optimization of scalar block scores}
\label{app:block-solver}

For a block of $b$ observations, orient each edge $e=(i,j)$ of the complete
graph with $i<j$. Let $\mathsf B$ have columns $e_i-e_j$, where $e_i$ is
coordinate vector $i$, and set $c_e=\kappa d(U_i,U_j)$. The constraints are
$|\mathsf B^\top v|\leq c$. Positive and negative parts of any signed edge
vector $\alpha$ give nonnegative multipliers for the two constraint directions.
Minimizing the resulting Lagrangian over $v$ yields the feasible dual value
\[
 D(\alpha)=\alpha^\top\mathsf B^\top Y
       -\sum_e c_e|\alpha_e|-\tfrac14\|\mathsf B\alpha\|_2^2
       \leq \min_{|\mathsf B^\top v|\leq c}\|Y-v\|_2^2.
\]
Thus no convergence assumption is required for a finite signed dual to supply
a lower value. The implementation minimizes $-D$ by accelerated proximal
gradient with componentwise soft thresholding \citep{beck2009fista}. Since
$\mathsf B\mathsf B^\top=bI-\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top$, the smooth negative-dual
term has gradient Lipschitz constant $b/2$, giving step size $2/b$. Incidence
products use indexed sums, avoiding a dense constraint matrix.

To obtain a primal upper value from an approximate vector $z$, evaluate
\[
 v_i^- =\min_j\{z_j+\kappa d(U_i,U_j)\},\qquad
 v_i^+ =\max_j\{z_j-\kappa d(U_i,U_j)\}.
\]
The triangle inequality makes both envelopes $\kappa$-Lipschitz on the sampled
support. Their average is feasible, as is a common translation by its mean
residual. Clipping to the observed target interval preserves feasibility and
cannot increase squared error. This construction remains valid for duplicate
or nearly coincident feature rows. Primal values quantify the numerical gap;
only guarded dual values enter lower calibration.

Each batched program follows its own stopping criterion with a fixed iteration
limit; another block's convergence does not alter its iterates. The recorded
dual and primal guards address floating-point roundoff numerically and are
not interval-arithmetic verification. Early-stopped programs and their gaps
are retained. The block score divides the lower squared-error sum by $b-1$;
the full-sample empirical-minimum comparator instead divides by its sample
size, as required by Appendix~\ref{app:erm-comparator}.
