\section{Exact-risk benchmark and centered comparator}
\label{app:known-risk}

This benchmark has an analytically known optimal risk and fitted-model risk,
so certificate coverage can be checked without using a test estimate as
population truth. Let $Z\in\{0,1\}$ be a Bernoulli state with
$\Pr(Z=1)=p\in(0,1)$, and let the scalar target be $Y=2Z-1$.
For a representation-separation parameter $a\in[0,1]$, use
\[
 \Phi_a(0)=\left((1+a)/2,(1-a)/2\right)^\top,\qquad
 \Phi_a(1)=\left((1-a)/2,(1+a)/2\right)^\top.
\]
Both vectors lie in the two-coordinate probability simplex. Their Euclidean
distance is $\sqrt2a$, whereas the target separation is $2$. For sensitivity
budget $\kappa\geq0$, define the unexplained separation
$c=(2-\kappa\sqrt2a)_+$. Proposition~\ref{prop:two-atom} then gives
\[
 R^*=p(1-p)c^2.
\]
Here $R^*$ denotes the minimum MSE over the benchmark's constrained
predictors. The two target values, representation family, and support are
specified in advance by the benchmark design.

An independent training sample gives the empirical frequency
$\widehat p$ of state $1$. We choose the constrained empirical-risk
minimizer whose predictions at the two states are
\begin{equation}
 \widehat f(0)=-1+\widehat p c,
 \qquad
 \widehat f(1)=1-(1-\widehat p)c.
 \label{eq:two-atom-fit}
\end{equation}
These values lie in $[-1,1]$ and have separation
$2-c=\min\{2,\kappa\sqrt2a\}$. The affine construction in
Proposition~\ref{prop:two-atom} therefore realizes them within the budget;
when $a=0$, the two values coincide. If one state is absent from training,
\eqref{eq:two-atom-fit} selects one feasible empirical minimizer using the
predeclared support. This convention specifies the prediction even at an
unobserved state.

Conditional on the training sample, state $0$ has prediction error
$-\widehat p c$, and state $1$ has error $(1-\widehat p)c$.
Consequently the exact fitted population risk is
\begin{equation}
 R(\widehat f)
 =c^2\{(1-p)\widehat p^2+p(1-\widehat p)^2\}
 =R^*+c^2(\widehat p-p)^2.
 \label{eq:two-atom-fitted-risk}
\end{equation}
For an independent pair of calibration states, the witness score equals
$c^2/2$ when the states differ and zero otherwise. The mismatch probability
is $2p(1-p)$, so the expected score is exactly $R^*$. With $n$ calibration
observations, the pair lower bound uses $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ disjoint
pairs. An odd final observation is excluded from pairing but remains in the
fitted-risk and centered statistics. The reported calibration sizes are all
even.

\paragraph{The centered comparator.}
For comparison, apply a centered certificate based on the target and
representation variances, with Euclidean distance and a single reference
class. Let $Z_1,\ldots,Z_n$
be independent calibration states, with $n\geq4$, and
$\bar z=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^nZ_i$ their empirical frequency. The output and
representation variances, computed with denominator $n$, are
\[
 \widehat s=4\bar z(1-\bar z),\qquad
 \widehat t=2a^2\bar z(1-\bar z).
\]
Their population counterparts are
$\sigma^2=4p(1-p)$ and $\tau^2=2a^2p(1-p)$.
The population centered bound is
$(\sigma-\kappa\tau)_+^2\leq R^*$, using nonnegative square roots.
In this two-state benchmark the inequality is an equality.

For a failure probability $\eta\in(0,1)$, define
\begin{align}
 r&=\log(2/\eta)/n,\nonumber\\
 V_\eta&=(\widehat s-4\sqrt{r/2})_+,\nonumber\\
 T_\eta&=\min\{1,\widehat t+\sqrt{2r}+1/n\},\nonumber\\
 L^{\rm ctr}_\eta&=(\sqrt{V_\eta}-\kappa\sqrt{T_\eta})_+^2.
 \label{eq:centered-comparator}
\end{align}
Here $V_\eta$ is a lower bound on $\sigma^2$, $T_\eta$ is an upper
bound on $\tau^2$, and the superscript ``ctr'' identifies the centered
risk bound. We show that $L^{\rm ctr}_\eta\leq R^*$ with probability
at least $1-\eta$.

First, $\mathbb E\widehat s=(1-1/n)\sigma^2\leq\sigma^2$, and
changing one calibration observation changes $\widehat s$ by at most
$4/n$. The bounded-differences inequality therefore gives
\[
 \widehat s-\mathbb E\widehat s\leq4\sqrt{r/2}
\]
with probability at least $1-\eta/2$. On this confidence event,
$V_\eta\leq\sigma^2$.
Second, changing one observation changes $\widehat t$ by at most $2/n$,
so the same inequality gives
\[
 \mathbb E\widehat t-\widehat t\leq\sqrt{2r}
\]
with failure probability at most $\eta/2$. Since
$\mathbb E\widehat t=(1-1/n)\tau^2$ and $\tau^2\leq1$,
\[
 \tau^2\leq\widehat t+\sqrt{2r}+\tau^2/n
          \leq\widehat t+\sqrt{2r}+1/n.
\]
Capping the right side at one remains valid because $\tau^2\leq1$.
A union bound gives $V_\eta\leq\sigma^2$ and
$T_\eta\geq\tau^2$ jointly with probability at least $1-\eta$.
The expression in \eqref{eq:centered-comparator} increases with its first
variance argument and decreases with its second. Hence it is no larger
than $(\sigma-\kappa\tau)_+^2\leq R^*$ on that event. The constant
one is the general simplex-variance bound; it is conservative for this
two-coordinate example.

In each Monte Carlo trial, the fitted-risk upper bound, pair-witness lower
bound, and centered lower bound each receive failure probability $\delta/3$.
For the centered bound, setting $\eta=\delta/3$ splits that allocation
between its two internal variance bounds. The three reported directions
therefore hold jointly with probability at least $1-\delta$ within a
trial. No joint $1-\delta$ event is asserted across all trials, grid
conditions, or separately reported experiments.
