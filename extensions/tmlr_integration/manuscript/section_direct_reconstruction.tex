\section{Training local reconstruction for the forecast}
\label{sec:direct-reconstruction}

The shared proxy metric in Section~\ref{sec:target-geometry} does not train
the deployed KAHM forecast. We next intervene on the reconstruction and
gate parameters themselves. This section specifies a forecasting family
and a matched evaluation, with historical pilot evidence kept separate.
Its public execution checkpoint freezes the numerical implementation,
analysis, exact task roster and prepared inputs before full evaluation.
This is a prospective checkpoint informed by previous results, not an
external registration or independent replication.

\subsection{A horizon-specific KAHM forecaster}

For a scalar series observed through time $t$, write its history as
$x_t=(y_{t-L+1},\ldots,y_t)^\top$ and its target at offset $h$ as $y_{t+h}$.
Training-only standardization gives $z_t$. A shared local bank supplies
reference columns $Q_c$, center $\mu_c$, principal basis $P_c$ and a fixed
positive kernel regularizer $\lambda_c$. Three blocks request 2, 4 and
8 local models, each with at most 24 references and rank at most two.
As in Equation~\eqref{c:eq:local}, the predictor receives the gate and
its gated principal coordinates. The complete forecast is
\begin{equation}
 \widehat y_{t+h}=f_{0,h}(x_t)+
 \gamma_h\{a_h+\beta_h^\top
       \widetilde\Gamma_{g,h}(z_t;\theta_h)\},
 \label{eq:direct-forecast}
\end{equation}
where $f_0$ is a retained raw-history ridge baseline,
$\widetilde\Gamma$ uses an A-fitted feature standardizer, and
$\gamma_h\in\{0,1/4,1/2,1\}$. The baseline penalty is selected inside
A, refitted on A, and held fixed. Every geometric arm therefore learns
the residuals of the same deployed baseline; neither donor residuals nor
a pretest refit is bundled into this intervention.

For each block $b$ and horizon $h$, a bounded log bandwidth
$\xi_{b,h}\in[-\log4,\log4]$ modifies both the reference and query
KAHM kernels:
\begin{align}
 K_c(\xi)_{ij}&=\exp\{-D_{c,ij}\exp(-2\xi)\},&
 k_c(z;\xi)_i&=\exp\{-d_{c,i}(z)\exp(-2\xi)\},\nonumber\\
 a_c(z;\xi)&=(K_c(\xi)+\lambda_c I)^{-1}k_c(z;\xi),&
 r_c(z;\xi)&=\frac{Q_ca_c(z;\xi)}{\boldsymbol1^\top a_c(z;\xi)}.
 \label{eq:direct-kahm}
\end{align}
Here $D$ and $d$ are the inherited local covariance-whitened projected
squared distances divided by twice the positive effective rank. A
degenerate rank-zero local model uses its fixed center instead of a kernel
solve. The regularizer,
reference membership, covariance and principal directions remain fixed.
A second bounded parameter $\eta_{b,h}$ multiplies each block's
folding-score logits by $\exp(\eta_{b,h})$ before softmax. Thus the
intervention changes the normalized signed KAHM reconstruction as well
as its use in the forecasting gate. It does not learn every KAHM component.

Both kernel matrices in Equation~\eqref{eq:direct-kahm} must change with
bandwidth: changing only a query similarity would define a different
procedure. For fixed reference columns, put $M=K+\lambda I$, $a=M^{-1}k$,
$s=\boldsymbol1^\top a$ and $r=Qa/s$. At points where $M$ is invertible
and $s\ne0$, ordinary matrix differentiation gives
\begin{equation}
 da=M^{-1}(dk-dM\,a),\qquad
 dr=\frac{(Q-r\boldsymbol1^\top)\,da}{s}.
 \label{eq:direct-derivative}
\end{equation}
This identity explains both how a bandwidth can alter reconstruction
and why signed-denominator cancellation matters. It is not a novelty
claim for differentiating through a linear solve. The present bounded
optimizer does not require analytic derivatives. Common log-kernel
shifting protects underflow; a relative denominator guard of $10^{-10}$
rejects unsafe A/B/C candidates. If a selected map is unsafe on D, its
entire affected horizon falls back to the frozen baseline, with paid work
and fallback recorded. Historical arithmetic and outcomes are unchanged.

\subsection{Actual forecast supervision and matched controls}

For each candidate $\theta_h$ and $\alpha\in\{0.01,0.1,1\}$, a ridge
head minimizes A residual sum of squares plus $\alpha\|\beta\|^2$,
with an unpenalized intercept. The geometry objective is actual B
forecast MSE for Equation~\eqref{eq:direct-forecast} at $\gamma_h=1$,
divided by a positive A-only target-variance scale. C then selects the
penalty and mixing weight, separately for each horizon. Zero correction
is the first incumbent; only strict C improvement replaces it. D labels
are never passed to the fitting API. A/B/C/D denote distinct roles,
not four opportunities to inspect the final evaluation.

The affine control trains a projection gain,
$r_c=\mu_c+\exp(\xi_b)P_cP_c^\top(z-\mu_c)$.
The locality control trains the balance between normal and center
distance, with logit
$-\exp(\xi_b)\|z-r_c^{\A}\|^2/v_c
-0.1\exp(-\xi_b)\|z-\mu_c\|^2/s_c$.
The RBF-local control trains tangent/normal anisotropy, with logit
\[
 -\frac{\exp(\xi_b)\|P_c^\top(z-\mu_c)\|^2+
 \exp(-\xi_b)\|(I-P_cP_c^\top)(z-\mu_c)\|^2}{2s_c}.
\]
The scales $s_c,v_c$ are fixed from A references. Each family uses the
same three gate-temperature parameters $\eta_{b,h}$ and three shape
parameters $\xi_{b,h}$. Identity parameters recover the fixed family;
fixing $\xi=0$ gives its gate-only intervention. Equal parameter counts
and data do not imply equal functional capacity.

Each gate-only and reconstruction/shape fit receives at most 256 actual
objective evaluations per horizon and penalty, with the same bounded
optimizer, zero initialization, stopping rule and finite-incumbent policy.
The fixed, gate-trained and reconstruction/shape-trained versions of the
four families give twelve geometric procedures. Raw ridge, raw history
plus Nystr\"om RBF, histogram gradient boosting, phase-correct seasonal
naive, and one C-selected non-KAHM policy complete the 17-method roster.
The policy chooses one complete non-KAHM method by aggregate C loss and
pays for all required candidates, counting shared nodes once. It is not
a D-selected oracle. Appendix~\ref{app:direct-protocol} records the
baseline grids, chronological rules, and cost scope.

\subsection{Development evidence and reserved evaluation}

The preceding exploratory pilot trained gate temperatures alone on
three fresh seeds per known law, with independently implemented
generators and 160 objective evaluations. It reduced mean KAHM MSE by
0.53\% on nonlinear AR, 30.21\% on Van der Pol and 11.29\% on Duffing
relative to that pilot's fixed KAHM. The best directly trained local
control remained more accurate on every law, and raw RBF was substantially
stronger on Van der Pol and Duffing. All 99 selected forecasts and
optimizer outcomes are preserved. These nine exploratory tasks do not
enter the new inference family or supply unseen source-transfer evidence.

The new simulation study uses the recovered inherited generators,
twelve fresh seeds per law, and independent A/B/C/D episodes numbering
64/32/32/128. Histories contain 12 observations and target offsets are
1, 4 and 12. The three training/selection roles are capped at 512/256/256
rows; D retains all 1,664 rows. Its 36 tasks are fresh repetitions of
known laws. The natural study adds ten series from four previously unused
Monash collections \citep{Godahewa2021Monash}: Dominick weekly retail,
rideshare hourly prices, Wikipedia daily web traffic, and one long
sunspot daily series. Series eligibility and deterministic hash selection
use A only. The initial rideshare context was shortened from 48 to 24
hours after an A-only availability check, before the public freeze and
any forecast evaluation; the daily seasonal lag and eligibility threshold
remain unchanged.

Natural A/B/C/D boundaries are chronological 50/20/15/15 percent, with
all targets inside their assigned segment and earlier observations
available as history. Missing windows are omitted without interpolation.
Thus the estimand concerns the retained available windows of these
single-vintage archives. It does not establish historical real-time data
release availability. Error scales use A-only seasonal differences,
with the prespecified first-difference fallback. The planned aggregate weights series equally within collections and
then weights four collections equally. A preparation audit after A-only
selection found no complete B windows in all three selected rideshare
series. These planned tasks remain feasibility failures, so the complete
four-collection accuracy aggregate is undefined. An availability-conditioned
description of the other seven series and three collections is reported
separately; it does not replace that estimand.

The synthetic primary family has 18 paired comparisons: on each law,
reconstruction-trained KAHM versus raw RBF, gate-trained KAHM, each
reconstruction-trained local control, and the non-KAHM policy. Two-sided
Bonferroni $t$ intervals use twelve repetition-level paired differences
and 11 degrees of freedom, under their normal approximation. Coverage
concerns this family only. Natural results are descriptive, including
leave-one-collection-out sensitivity; overlapping origins and ten series
do not turn four purposively selected collections into a large source
sample. All procedures, failures and fallback costs are retained.

\subsection{Audited results and predictive contribution}

All \DirectAvailableTasks{} feasible task suites complete, yielding
\DirectAvailableOutputs{} task--method prediction records from
\DirectPlannedOutputs{} planned outputs. The three preidentified
rideshare tasks account for the 51 unavailable outputs; there are no
additional task failures, invalid objective evaluations or numerical
fallback events. The frozen audit passes 543,017 checks, including
reconstruction of 144 generated episode batches and replay of all
731 selected states on both C and D. A separately written calculation
recomputes the scalar losses, A-only natural scales and all 18 paired
intervals without importing the model or analyzer. These are consistency
and replay checks, not independent experimental replications.

Table~\ref{tab:direct-methods} reports every declared procedure.
Relative to fixed KAHM, reconstruction training lowers mean synthetic
MSE by 0.42\%, 61.81\% and 12.76\% on nonlinear AR, Van der Pol and
Duffing, respectively. Relative to gate-only KAHM the reductions are
0.09\%, 41.43\% and 5.51\%. The latter Van der Pol increment is resolved
by the simultaneous analysis; the AR and Duffing gate increments are not.
The fixed-map comparisons are descriptive, outside that primary family.

The 18-contrast family yields \DirectPrimaryPositive{} positive,
\DirectPrimaryNegative{} negative and \DirectPrimaryUnresolved{}
unresolved intervals (Figure~\ref{fig:direct-primary}; complete numerical
intervals in Table~\ref{tab:direct-primary}). The positive comparisons

and KAHM versus gate-only KAHM on Van der Pol, with a 41.43\% reduction
and interval $[0.004349,0.007709]$. Differences are comparator minus KAHM,
so a positive interval favors KAHM. These intervals are for absolute
paired MSE differences, not percentages.

The adverse comparisons are equally material. On Van der Pol,
shape-trained affine and the C-selected policy beat reconstruction-trained
KAHM. The policy selects boosted trees in all twelve repetitions.
On Duffing, raw RBF, shape-trained affine, shape-trained locality and
the policy beat KAHM. No law resolves all six primary comparisons in
KAHM's favor, and no positive primary interval is against an equally
shape-trained non-KAHM local control. Unresolved differences do not
establish equivalence. The experiment therefore identifies useful direct
training within the KAHM family, especially on Van der Pol, without
establishing distinctive superiority of its reconstruction rule.

\paragraph{Available natural evidence.}
The complete four-collection comparison remains undefined. On the
explicitly conditional three-collection subset, KAHM has scaled MSE
$0.371935$, compared with ridge's $0.385625$ (3.55\% lower), fixed KAHM's
$0.394070$ and gate-only KAHM's $0.396192$. Raw RBF ($0.363007$), boosted
trees ($0.352201$) and shape-trained RBF-local ($0.363835$) remain more
accurate in aggregate. KAHM improves the C-selected policy's $0.382472$
by 2.75\%; that policy's validation selection is not a test-set oracle.
Source results are retained in Table~\ref{tab:direct-natural}.

The ridge improvement is concentrated in Dominick: KAHM has scaled MSE
$0.316351$, versus $0.357402$ for ridge. Excluding that collection leaves
only a 0.00229\% mean improvement over ridge across the other two
collections. C selects zero KAHM correction on twelve of the 21 available
natural horizons, including every Sunspot horizon. These observations
limit a broad transfer interpretation and show why an aggregate gain
cannot be equated with a uniformly useful new representation.

\paragraph{Optimization and measured resources.}
The run retains \DirectOptimizations{} geometry optimizations and
\DirectObjectiveEvaluations{} actual objective evaluations. There are
2,491 optimizer convergence exits, 604 evaluation-budget exits and one
abnormal optimizer stop; the best finite incumbent is retained in every
case. No invalid objective evaluation or numerical fallback is observed.
This accounting distinguishes a completed forecast from optimizer convergence.

Mean charged synthetic pipeline time is 10.725 seconds for
reconstruction-trained KAHM, versus 0.914 for gate-only KAHM, 0.0774 for
fixed KAHM, 0.0172 for raw RBF and 0.653 for boosted trees. Equally
shape-trained affine, locality and RBF-local take 6.973, 2.722 and
2.766 seconds, respectively. On the seven available natural tasks,
KAHM averages 7.929 seconds, versus 0.0130 for raw RBF and 0.655 for
boosted trees. Its mean selected serialized state is 104.2 KiB there,
versus 57.9 KiB for raw RBF and 383.3 KiB for boosted trees; zero-correction
choices can omit a geometric bank, so these are selected-state measurements,
not intrinsic storage lower bounds. Table~\ref{tab:direct-cost} and
Figure~\ref{fig:direct-natural-cost} give the complete resource comparison.
Costs include the recorded training, selection, query and state-packing
work. They do not measure update latency or minimal deployment cost.
The observed accuracy gains thus do not remove the need to justify
KAHM-specific complexity against the stronger alternatives.
