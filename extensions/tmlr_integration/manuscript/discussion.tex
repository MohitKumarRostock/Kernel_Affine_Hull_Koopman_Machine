\section{Interpretation and limitations}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Distinct features, bounded corrections, and predictive value}
The results distinguish three properties of a reconstruction-based
forecaster. First, KAHM reconstruction departures can contain functions
outside the span of matched affine and RBF features. The irregular-reference
construction proves the existence of this distinction; it does not imply
that every fitted reference set adds a new function. Second, bounded
features and ridge regularization control the magnitude of the fitted
correction. Third, the correction can improve prediction for a particular
target and decoder. None of these properties implies the next without
additional assumptions.

The principal experiment provides a useful example of this separation.
The bounded KAHM departure reduces nonlinear aggregate error by 12.58\%
relative to the shared gate-and-coordinate basis. This improvement meets
the prespecified 3\% margin and shows that the reconstruction features
contribute predictive value to the tested decoder. However, the matched
RBF departure recovers much of the same benefit. KAHM's 1.17\% point
advantage over that procedure does not meet the required margin. Its
3.92\% point advantage over raw-input RBF forecasting has an unresolved
simultaneous lower bound, and boosted trees and the selected non-KAHM
procedure attain lower aggregate error. The linear-control noninferiority
criterion and all stability checks are satisfied, but the collection of
required forecasting comparisons is not. These findings support the use
of matched reconstruction controls when evaluating the contribution of a
particular normalization or geometric construction.

The correction is selected with nonzero weight in all 432 nonlinear
KAHM task--horizon combinations. Thus the aggregate result does not arise
from always returning the raw ridge baseline. Nevertheless, activation
is only a validation decision. It does not establish that the correction
reduces held-out loss in each task or horizon. Likewise, the raw-span
analysis describes representable functions before block normalization
and penalized fitting. A nonorthogonal change of coordinates can alter
ridge predictions even when the underlying spans coincide, and separate
radial transformations can change the spans themselves. The experiments
therefore compare complete, specified forecasting procedures.

\subsection{What the complementary experiments establish}
The controlled representation experiments show that reconstruction
accuracy and predictive encoding can differ. A residual score can discard
position along a reconstructed set, whereas explicit local coordinates
or a locality-sensitive score can retain it. Fresh simulations then show
that the representation and finite decoder must be evaluated together:
KAHM has favorable comparisons on one declared law while simpler controls
perform better on others. These conditional gains do not justify a
recommendation across unrelated time-series sources.

The natural-data study illustrates that restriction. Its KAHM augmentation
has higher aggregate error and cost than the raw-input RBF comparator.
An additional comparison using an unclipped min--max scaler preserves
the aggregate ordering on those same observations, although individual
controlled losses change. This supports the ordering under the two tested
preprocessing choices. It does not establish invariance to scaling or
transfer to a new population of series.

Retaining a raw forecast and adding a geometric correction makes zero
correction available after a successful model fit. The additive study
shows why this architectural property is weaker than a guarantee of
usefulness. Validation can choose an unhelpful correction, and the shared
geometric fit can fail before the correction weight is selected. Two
natural-data configurations have such failures, so their full-roster
geometric aggregate is undefined. The post-hoc analysis of the 22
successful configurations is conditional on fitting success. It cannot
replace the planned evaluation. On that subset, ridge is both more
accurate and cheaper than the geometric alternatives.

The supervised-geometry study finds that KAHM features can correct ridge
on the three tested nonlinear laws and modestly improve the natural-data
aggregate. The comparisons do not establish an incremental benefit from
the learned diagonal proxy metric or from fitting donor-excluded
residuals. Simpler matched geometries have favorable comparisons, and
fixed-metric KAHM is more accurate and cheaper than learned-metric,
donor-excluded KAHM on the archive. Donor exclusion prevents a model from
fitting its own query response, but it does not make residuals from donor
models identically distributed with residuals from the final model.
The transport identity in Equation~\eqref{eq:target-donor-transport}
explains this distinction under fixed maps and a common evaluation law;
chronological distribution changes require further assumptions.

Direct training of reconstruction bandwidths and gate temperatures also
produces mixed evidence. The synthetic analysis has two favorable, six
adverse, and ten unresolved comparisons. None of the favorable primary
intervals is against an equally trained non-KAHM local control. Three
planned natural-data tasks have no eligible training-query windows,
leaving the four-collection aggregate undefined. The descriptive result
on the available seven series concerns a feasible subset, not the
complete planned population. Absent query windows and a failed geometric
fit are different limitations and require different methodological
responses.

\subsection{Scope of the stability guarantee}
The bounded-feature construction limits one source of extreme residual
corrections. The largest observed taskwise ratio of KAHM maximum absolute
error to ridge maximum absolute error is 1.553, below the prespecified
screen of five. No feature-, coefficient-, or correction-bound violation
occurs. These implementation checks are consistent with the deterministic
bound, but they do not convert it into a forecast-risk guarantee. A
bounded correction can still be systematically inaccurate, and the raw
ridge forecast is not bounded by the correction result.

The radial map is nonexpansive as a function of its feature input. This
property does not bound sensitivity to the original history when the
preceding reconstruction is highly sensitive. In particular, replacing
a reconstruction by an affine fallback near a small signed denominator
need not preserve continuity. Nor does the fixed-feature bound control
changes caused by updating the geometry online. The adaptive experiment
retains its extreme forecasts and unfavorable accuracy comparisons after
the numerical solver correction. The fixed-geometry result therefore
establishes a guarantee for a different, explicitly bounded procedure.

\subsection{Statistical and external validity}
The final experiment uses independent repetitions of four known laws.
The nonlinear laws were already available during method development.
The two additional-noise levels share underlying episodes and use a
noise amplitude estimated from the fitting split. This pairing supports
controlled comparisons but does not supply independent tasks for
inference. The primary sample size is 24 repetition aggregates, regardless
of the number of overlapping origins, horizons, or forecast cells.
The paired-mean $t$ bounds rely on their stated approximation and are not
distribution-free. The fixed repetition count is an experimental budget,
not a demonstrated power calculation.

The six primary contrasts form one simultaneous family. Earlier studies
have their own contrast families and sampling units; the paper does not
claim joint 95\% coverage across all reported analyses. Failure to
establish a 3\% improvement margin has several possible interpretations.
For the matched RBF departure, the estimated gain is below the margin.
For raw-input RBF forecasting, the estimate exceeds the margin but the
lower bound remains below zero. Neither result establishes equality,
and the latter does not establish that raw-input RBF is superior.
Condition-specific summaries describe heterogeneity without selecting a
new favorable subpopulation for confirmatory inference.

The natural collections were selected for diversity and feasibility,
not sampled randomly from future forecasting applications. Four reserved
families in the original study provide four source-level observations;
multiple windows, configurations, and fitting seeds do not enlarge that
number. Several series yield few training or test windows. Excluding
windows with unavailable values makes the evaluation conditional on
window availability. The FRED-MD input is a retrospective snapshot whose
release-time revisions and upstream transformations are not fully
characterized here, and Solar Weekly has simulation provenance.
Consequently, the evidence does not establish performance for arbitrary
missing-data patterns, real-time macroeconomic vintages, or all natural
forecasting domains.

The tested models are local univariate forecasters with finite training
caps, reference counts, decoder dimensions, and tuning grids. Equal
requested landmark counts do not imply equal realized rank or effective
capacity. The studies do not constitute a comprehensive comparison with
neural or pretrained forecasting models, long-context multivariate
methods, or all adaptive kernel algorithms. A failed procedure within
these settings constrains that procedure; it is not an impossibility
result for every predictor based on the representation.

\subsection{Computational interpretation and practical use}
Reported costs include the fitting and selection operations required by
each procedure. Shared operations are charged once within a procedure,
and selection policies pay for the candidates they evaluate. These
measurements describe the supplied implementations on the recorded host.
They do not establish minimum algorithmic costs, and small timing
differences should not be interpreted with more precision than the
measurement design supports. Serialized model size, owned array storage,
process high-water memory, and transient fitting workspace measure
different resources.

The target-geometry study illustrates why both absolute and relative costs
matter. Its learned-metric KAHM procedure takes about 0.162 seconds per
task, compared with 0.00130 seconds for ridge: a large ratio at a small
absolute scale. The final reconstruction experiment shows a different
tradeoff: KAHM fits faster than boosted trees but predicts more slowly,
and raw-input RBF is cheaper on both measures. An application would need
to specify acceptable error, latency, memory, and updating costs before
these tradeoffs could justify deployment. The experiments report the
observed loss--cost pairs without imposing an unspecified exchange rate
between accuracy and computation.

\section{Conclusion}
KAHM reconstruction can supply useful features to a finite forecasting
decoder, but its value depends on the reference geometry, the accessible
feature span, regularization, and the prediction target. Exact identities
identify normalized target transfer as a kernel-ridge ratio and show
when matched reconstruction spans are redundant. An irregular reference
set establishes that the spans can also differ. Separately, a bounded
feature map and ridge residual decoder yield a uniform correction bound.

The matched forecasting experiment establishes improvement over the
common local-coordinate basis, but it does not establish the required
practical advantage over the full comparison family. Complementary
experiments identify conditional gains, unfavorable natural-data
comparisons, incomplete task coverage, and limitations of online
adaptation. Together, the results clarify what reconstruction contributes
to prediction and why algebraic distinctness, numerical stability, and
forecasting accuracy require separate evaluation.
