\section{Reconstruction algebra and bounded residual decoding}
\label{app:reconstruction-mechanism}

The results here concern fixed reference maps and a specified decoder class.
They establish identities, conditional distinctness and correction bounds;
none is an accuracy theorem for an arbitrary time-series law. The executable
companion \path{extensions/reconstruction_mechanism/algebra.py} evaluates only
fabricated reference banks and matrices, independently of forecast outcomes.

\subsection{Normalized kernel identities}
Let the rows of $Q\in\R^{M\times p}$ be reference histories, $P$ a fixed
principal-coordinate basis of rank $r$, and $W$ the positive projected
covariance precision. For row-vector histories the inherited Gaussian kernel is
\[
 k_i(x)=\exp\!\left[-\frac{((x-q_i)P)W((x-q_i)P)^\top}{2r}\right].
\]
Write $k(x)$ as a row vector, $K$ for the reference Gram matrix and
$C=(K+\lambda I)^{-1}$. The KAHM and matched RBF reconstructions are
\begin{equation}
 A_K(x)=\frac{k(x)CQ}{k(x)C\mathbf1},\qquad
 A_R(x)=\frac{k(x)Q}{k(x)\mathbf1}.
 \label{eq:mechanism-normalized-maps}
\end{equation}
Both use the same references, projected distance and bandwidth. Multiplying
$k(x)$ by a common positive scalar cancels from either ratio; this justifies
the implemented maximum-log-kernel subtraction.

\begin{proposition}[Normalization and target transfer]
\label{prop:mechanism-krr}
Whenever $s(x)=k(x)C\mathbf1\ne0$, the weights
$\alpha(x)=k(x)C/s(x)$ sum to one. For any reference target vector $y$,
\[
 \alpha(x)y=\frac{k(x)(K+\lambda I)^{-1}y}
 {k(x)(K+\lambda I)^{-1}\mathbf1}.
\]
This is exactly the ratio of two dual kernel-ridge predictions, one trained
on $y$ and the other on the constant vector, under the displayed
no-additional-intercept convention. Moreover, for an affine scalar function
$f(q)=qb+c$,
\[
 \alpha(x)f(Q)-f(x)=\{A_K(x)-x\}b.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The first statement follows by summing the weights; the remaining identities
follow by substitution and $\alpha(x)\mathbf1=1$.
\end{proof}
Directly transferring these weights to future targets is therefore not an
independent forecasting mechanism. Likewise, $A_K(x)b$ is normalized kernel
ridge on the restricted pseudo-target $Qb$. The identity
$A(x)+\{x-A(x)\}=x$ holds for every reconstruction and creates a linear
redundancy if all three are appended without restriction. These observations
do not rule out useful basis functions for a restricted decoder.

\subsection{When reconstruction departures are redundant}
Define the affine reconstruction in row-vector notation by
$A_A(x)=\mu+(x-\mu)PP^\top$. With a common locality gate $g(x)$, let
$d_K=g(A_K-A_A)$ and $d_R=g(A_R-A_A)$.

\begin{proposition}[A sufficient raw-span equivalence]
\label{prop:mechanism-redundancy}
Suppose $K\mathbf1=\kappa\mathbf1$ and the augmented reference-coordinate
span is invariant under $C$: $C\mathbf1=a\mathbf1$,
$CQ=QB+\mathbf1 c^\top$, where $a=(\kappa+\lambda)^{-1}$.
Then
\[
 A_K=(A_R B+c^\top)/a,
 \qquad
 d_K=d_R B/a+gA_A(B/a-I)+gc^\top/a.
\]
After redundant coordinate columns are removed, the augmented raw feature
spans agree when both include the common gate and gated principal coordinates.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Substitute the two assumed actions of $C$ into
Equation~\eqref{eq:mechanism-normalized-maps}. The last two departure terms
belong to the common feature span because
$gA_A=g\mu+g(x-\mu)PP^\top$. The restriction of an invertible operator to
an invariant finite-dimensional subspace is invertible, giving equality of
the active augmented spans.
\end{proof}

For example, take one scalar bank $q=(-1,1)^\top$, $P=1$, $g=1$ and
$A_A(x)=x$. If the off-diagonal Gram entry is $\rho$, then
\begin{equation}
 A_K=cA_R,\qquad d_K=cd_R+(c-1)x,\qquad
 c=\frac{1+\rho+\lambda}{1-\rho+\lambda}.
 \label{eq:mechanism-two-reference}
\end{equation}
Thus $\operatorname{span}(1,x,d_K)=\operatorname{span}(1,x,d_R)$ exactly.
The source-compatible sample-variance precision is $1/2$, so $\rho=e^{-1}$;
the inherited regularization iteration gives $\lambda=2.6519665429866075$
and $c=1.224037566462905$. The saved numerical identity errors on the
fabricated grid are below $6\times10^{-16}$.

These are statements about raw spans. An isotropic ridge penalty is not
invariant under a general nonorthogonal coordinate change. Separate nonlinear
radial transforms can also destroy the displayed linear relation. The
empirical comparison therefore concerns the complete reconstruction,
normalization and decoder procedure; it cannot automatically attribute a
forecast difference to additional raw function-space capacity.

\subsection{An explicit distinct reconstruction basis}
\begin{proposition}[Irregular scalar references]
\label{prop:mechanism-distinct}
Let $q_1<q_2<q_3$, $q_1+q_3\ne2q_2$, and use a Gaussian kernel with
positive precision $t$. Suppose $v=C\mathbf1$ is componentwise positive.
For one bank with gate one and affine reconstruction $x$,
\[
 A_K\notin\operatorname{span}(1,x,A_R),\qquad
 d_K\notin\operatorname{span}(1,x,d_R).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Set $u=Cq$. The six unordered sums $q_i+q_j$, $i\le j$, are distinct.
Products of the Gaussian translates satisfy
\[
 k_i(x)k_j(x)=e^{-tx^2}e^{-t(q_i^2+q_j^2)/2}e^{t(q_i+q_j)x}.
\]
The six real exponentials remaining after the common factor is removed are
linearly independent: differentiating a zero linear combination at zero
gives a nonsingular Vandermonde system.

Suppose $A_K=cA_R+d$. Cross multiplication and coefficient matching give,
with $w=u-dv$, $w_i=cq_i v_i$ and
$w_i+w_j=c(q_i v_j+q_j v_i)$. If $c=0$, invertibility of $C$ implies
$q=d\mathbf1$, a contradiction. Otherwise
$(q_i-q_j)(v_i-v_j)=0$ forces $v$ to be constant, hence $K$ must have
constant row sums. Its middle row instead exceeds either endpoint row,
since the Gaussian decreases strictly with distance. This is again a
contradiction.

Both reconstructions are bounded on the real line: $A_R$ is a convex
combination of the references, while
$A_K=(\sum_i k_i v_i (u_i/v_i))/(\sum_i k_i v_i)$ is a convex combination
of the fixed values $u_i/v_i$. Any putative additional linear term in $x$
must consequently have zero coefficient. The statement for departures
follows by adding the common coordinate $x$.
\end{proof}

The explicit fixture $q=(-1,0,2)^\top$ has inherited sample-variance
precision $3/7$ and $\lambda=4.498274743339644$. Positivity of $C\mathbf1$
can be certified without a query grid. Write $K=I+E$ and $a=1+\lambda$.
If $a>2\|E\|_\infty$, the Neumann series gives
\[
 \|aC\mathbf1-\mathbf1\|_\infty
 \le \frac{\|E\|_\infty/a}{1-\|E\|_\infty/a}<1.
\]
Here $\|E\|_\infty=1.2314905926823394$, so the condition holds.
On 81 fabricated queries in $[-3,4]$, the maximum least-squares residual
of $d_K$ outside the sampled $(1,x,d_R)$ span is $0.05355670997078987$.
This numerical witness supplements the analytic proof; it does not prove
nonredundancy for every high-dimensional bank fitted in the forecasting study.

\subsection{A bounded correction with a free intercept}
For a feature block of width $p_b$, use the radial map
\begin{equation}
 T_{R_b}(v)=\frac{v}{\sqrt{1+\|v\|_2^2/R_b^2}},\qquad R_b=\sqrt{p_b}.
 \label{eq:mechanism-radial}
\end{equation}
It has norm at most $R_b$. Its Jacobian has tangential eigenvalue
$(1+\|v\|^2/R_b^2)^{-1/2}$ and radial eigenvalue
$(1+\|v\|^2/R_b^2)^{-3/2}$, both in $(0,1]$, so the map is
nonexpansive. Concatenating separately bounded blocks gives
$\|\psi(x)\|\le R=(\sum_bR_b^2)^{1/2}$. This does not establish a
Lipschitz constant for the preceding history-to-feature map, particularly
across the signed-denominator fallback boundary.

\begin{proposition}[Centered ridge correction bound]
\label{prop:mechanism-correction-bound}
For residual targets $e_i$ and bounded feature vectors $\psi_i$, minimize
\[
 n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n(e_i-b-\psi_i^\top w)^2+\alpha\|w\|^2,
 \qquad \alpha>0,
\]
with a free intercept. Let $\bar e$, $\bar\psi$ be the training means,
$e_c=e-\bar e\mathbf1$, and $\sigma_e=\|e_c\|/\sqrt n$. Then
\begin{equation}
 \|w\|\le\frac{\sigma_e}{2\sqrt\alpha},\qquad
 |\gamma q(x)|\le |\gamma|\left[
 |\bar e|+(R+\|\bar\psi\|)\frac{\sigma_e}{2\sqrt\alpha}\right],
 \quad q(x)=\bar e+(\psi(x)-\bar\psi)^\top w.
 \label{eq:mechanism-correction-bound}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Centering eliminates the intercept. If the centered design has singular
values $s$, its coefficient operator has singular values
$s/(s^2+n\alpha)\le1/(2\sqrt{n\alpha})$. Multiplying by $\|e_c\|$
gives the first inequality. The second follows from Cauchy--Schwarz and
$\|\psi(x)-\bar\psi\|\le R+\|\bar\psi\|$.
\end{proof}
The numerical penalty therefore corresponds to sum-SSE penalty $n\alpha$;
using $\alpha$ unchanged in a sum-SSE solve would implement a different
procedure. In multiple horizons the bound applies separately to each
selected $\alpha_h$ and $\gamma_h$. It is generic ridge algebra, not a
KAHM-specific performance theorem. It bounds only the correction: the
retained raw ridge baseline and the total forecast error are not globally
bounded by it. The reserved study checks the feature, coefficient and
correction inequalities on every saved task in addition to its separate
error screen.
