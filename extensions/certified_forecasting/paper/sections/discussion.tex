\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What is certified about a representation.}
Lower and upper risk bounds can establish both that a fitted forecast meets a
tolerance and that every cheaper declared class fails it. This identifies
minimum feasible representation cost even when upper-only selection returns
the same predictor. The conclusion concerns a representation, metric,
sensitivity budget, and evaluation law jointly. As
\eqref{eq:risk-decomposition} shows, it does not identify information loss
separately from irreducible noise or sensitivity restrictions. The learned-KAHM
study demonstrates these decisions on a recoverable signal and an independent
future control; their prevalence on naturally acquired data remains open.
Costs count declared features, not globally optimal forecasting complexity
or runtime.

\paragraph{Population slack and calibration precision.}
Pairwise slack reflects conditional residual means and loose geometric
inequalities (Proposition~\ref{prop:witness-slack}). Our consistency result assumes
unconditional iid draws, bounded scalar targets, and compact representation
support; conditional blocks can approach a smaller average of conditional
optima. Larger blocks leave fewer independent scores, so finite-sample
endpoints need not improve.

Fresh calibration conditional on 40 frozen KAHM banks connects the hierarchy
to learned geometry (Section~\ref{sec:learned-kahm-hierarchy}). In the
independent-future control, $b=8,16$ exclude both classes at $n=1024$ for every
control bank while pairs do not. All lower methods assess the same scalar Lipschitz
class. At that size stratification gives larger mean endpoints; full-sample empirical
minimization resolves every control at $n=256$, its only tested sample size.
Larger blocks add no cost decision in the repairable regime. The new study
uses KL lower means, whereas the earlier pair study uses empirical Bernstein;
cross-study differences do not isolate block size. These are conditional
evaluations of fixed banks, not new training repetitions or natural-data
evidence. Stratification can also remove useful contrasts, and a sharp
Lipschitz bound can remain loose for the affine readout. Zero or unresolved
lower bounds establish neither class adequacy nor predictive sufficiency.

\paragraph{What refinement guarantees.}
Retaining old blocks preserves earlier feasible forecasts, and exact
duplication leaves the block-norm class unchanged. A ridge-regularized fit may
still change. Neither nesting nor a reduced obstruction guarantees improvement
of a fitted predictor. Residual correlation gives a sufficient population
condition when budget remains, while the distance-expansion result is
necessary. Neither guarantees that unsupervised KAHM clustering discovers a
sufficient state. The experiments freeze candidates before calibration;
sequential refinement with fresh evaluation blocks remains untested.

\paragraph{Sampling populations and adaptation.}
Synthetic episodes are independently generated. PenDigits supplies only 13
eligible held-out writer identifiers, inferred from source fields; its
generic endpoints are uninformative. Speech supplies documented recording
identifiers and 978 calibration groups, permitting informative upper bounds
under the explicit iid group-record assumption. Source identifiers and
disjoint splits do not establish random population sampling. Eligibility
rules based on completed recordings and source quality screening further
restrict both real-data populations. Repeated fitting seeds on the same
cohort measure fitting variation, not independent population replications.

Target ranges follow from the synthetic laws, the PenDigits acquisition box,
or the fixed speech-energy transformation. Clipping predictions cannot bound
loss for an unbounded target. The certificates do not transfer across
changes in dynamics, sensors, noise, or origin laws. Original-seed scaling
follow-ups reuse previously examined calibration, and their endpoints are
nominal. Fresh synthetic studies use the stated independent streams;
PenDigits and speech scaling follow-ups on examined cohorts remain
descriptive. Confidence allocations cover each declared study, not the
entire research campaign.

\paragraph{Accuracy and preprocessing.}
The matched comparisons support KAHM gains on nonlinear autoregression and
against RBF geometry on the fixed PenDigits cohort. Affine-hull alternatives
are stronger on the oscillators, while autoregressive, quadratic, and kernel
regressors outperform KAHM on speech. Scaling to $[-1,1]$ improves some tasks
and worsens others. It must be treated as a development-selected model choice,
with training extrema frozen before calibration; clipping future inputs is
a distinct representation. These experiments assess the stated certificate
framework and forecasting implementations. They establish neither universal
KAHM superiority nor an accuracy frontier against broadly tuned neural
forecasters. Execution times depend on hardware and implementation, and
feature count alone does not determine computational cost.

\section{Conclusion}

Assessing a forecasting representation requires distinguishing the performance
of a fitted decoder from the risk of its entire declared class. The normalized
block hierarchy connects a pairwise obstruction to optimal scalar Lipschitz
risk under the stated conditions. Calibration, stratification, and retained
feature blocks turn this lower information into accuracy and minimum-cost
decisions. Learned KAHM dictionaries provide a concrete implementation;
the guarantees are general. Their usefulness depends on population slack,
independent sample size, and computational cost, while the experiments retain
task-dependent accuracy and unresolved decisions as part of the evidence.
