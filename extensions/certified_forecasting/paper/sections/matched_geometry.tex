\section{A matched comparison of KAHM and alternative geometries}
\label{sec:matched-geometry}

\input{tables/matched_geometry_numbers}

The preceding experiments establish a KAHM forecasting procedure and several
of its failure modes. They do not fully separate the geometric construction
from the decoder: the original KAHM Frobenius and rollout families have no
corresponding RBF or affine-hull controls. We therefore compare all three
geometries with the same decoder choices and validation opportunities across
the complete seven-task panel. The protocol was internally frozen before the
full follow-up fits; it was motivated by the earlier results and was not an
external preregistration.

\subsection{Design and validation selection}

Each geometry is paired with three heads: direct prediction under the sum of
block Frobenius norms, direct prediction under a single Frobenius norm, and
affine feature-transition rollout. Every geometry/head pair has the four
retained dimensions $2,6,14,30$, both history lengths, and all four preprocessing
options from Section~\ref{sec:input-scaling}. In this study preprocessing is
varied equally for KAHM, RBF, and affine-hull geometry. Training data, horizons,
origins, regularization, geometry subsampling, and target units follow the
original task configurations. The direct heads use a common numerical
coefficient budget; it does not make different feature maps statistically
equivalent. Rollout sensitivity is computed from its fitted transition and
readout. Feature count likewise does not equate runtime or kernel costs.

The five fitting seeds $5701$--$5705$ generate new training, validation,
calibration, and test episodes for every synthetic task. On PenDigits they
select new training-stroke subsamples and geometry randomness on the same
development and held-out writer cohorts. The latter comparison measures
fitting variation on a fixed cohort, not variation over new test writers.

For each task, seed, geometry, and head, the primary analysis selects
preprocessing, history, and retained dimension by mean validation MSE over the
horizons. Ties are resolved by feature count, history, preprocessing order
(standardization, min--max, clipped min--max, raw), and candidate name.
There are six primary comparisons per task and seed: KAHM versus each
alternative within each head. The selected dimensions, histories, and
preprocessing can differ between geometries; their available choices are the
same. Test all-horizon MSE is the main descriptive
outcome; percentages compare means of MSE, and every paired seed effect is
retained. Physical MSE values are not pooled across tasks. Selection at each
fixed dimension additionally gives descriptive curves, with only history and
preprocessing selected by validation. A secondary analysis also selects the
head by validation and is reported separately.

PenDigits selection is coordinate-specific and weights validation writers
equally. After selection, the two coordinate
losses and horizons are averaged within each held-out writer, and the 13
writers receive equal weight. Repeated fitting seeds do not supply additional
independent writers.

\subsection{Confidence accounting and interpretation}

For each synthetic task and seed, each preprocessing arm receives failure
budget $0.01$ for its complete frozen risk/refinement family, with the inherited
$80/20$ split. The union over four arms therefore costs $0.04$. The remaining
$0.01$ is split equally over the six primary geometry comparisons. For each,
we average alternative-minus-KAHM squared loss over horizons within an
independent calibration episode and apply the paired empirical Bernstein
bound. A positive lower endpoint certifies improvement of these fitted
predictors. The loss difference lies in $[-4,4]$; its observations are complete
episode averages. Validation selection precedes calibration and uses
independent development data.

This gives a joint $95\%$ event within each complete synthetic task/seed,
without a simultaneous claim across tasks, seeds, or earlier campaigns.
Fixed-dimension and head-selected comparisons are descriptive. An unresolved
improvement certificate does not establish equal risk. PenDigits retains its
writer-level upper bounds with failure budget $0.0125$ per preprocessing arm,
joint over candidates, coordinates, fitting seeds, and horizons. Their union
costs $0.05$, conditional on the original unverified iid-writer assumption.
There is no additional independent calibration cohort for its geometry
comparisons, so paired writer differences remain descriptive.

\subsection{Geometry, prediction head, and task interact}

\input{tables/matched_geometry_primary}

All scheduled fits completed, retaining \MatchedCandidates\ candidates.
Table~\ref{tab:matched-geometry-primary} gives all \MatchedComparisons\ primary
task/head/alternative contrasts: KAHM has lower mean test MSE in
\MatchedImproved\ and higher mean test MSE in \MatchedDegraded. These counts
describe dependent contrasts on a fixed task panel; they are not independent
votes for a universally preferred geometry. Absolute errors for all nine
geometry/head families appear in Table~\ref{tab:matched-geometry-mse}.

On nonlinear AR, KAHM improves on both geometric alternatives under every head
in all five fitting seeds. Its block-direct mean MSE is $0.00456$, compared
with $0.00511$ for ridge AR and $0.00523$ for quadratic regression. On PenDigits,
the KAHM reductions relative to RBF are $15.6\%$, $17.2\%$, and $17.9\%$ for
block direct, Frobenius direct, and rollout, respectively, with lower loss in
all five seeds. The corresponding mean reductions relative to affine-hull
features are $12.7\%$, $1.9\%$, and $1.4\%$, with four, four, and three favorable
seeds. This is a benefit within the geometric comparison, not overall
forecasting superiority: PenDigits Frobenius-direct KAHM MSE is $0.00504$,
while ridge AR and quadratic regression achieve $0.00184$ and $0.00102$.

The oscillator controls expose an important attribution limit. On Van der Pol,
KAHM mean MSE is $14.4$--$17.1\%$ lower than RBF across the three heads, yet
it exceeds affine-hull MSE by $9.5\%$, $102.7\%$, and $92.8\%$, respectively.
On Duffing, the corresponding excesses over affine-hull MSE are $13.1\%$,
$70.6\%$, and $79.9\%$. The affine-hull advantage with Frobenius and rollout
heads occurs in every fitting seed on both oscillators. Consequently, a gain
from changing a KAHM head or its preprocessing cannot by itself establish a
benefit specific to KAHM geometry. Mean effects can also disagree with the
majority of seed signs: the favorable quasiperiodic block-direct means against
both alternatives occur with lower KAHM loss in only two of five seeds.

Figure~\ref{fig:matched-geometry-dimensions} retains every fixed-dimension
comparison. On PenDigits, KAHM has lower mean MSE than RBF at all four
dimensions under each head, so the advantage is not explained solely by a
larger selected feature count. Its ranking against affine-hull geometry
changes with dimension. On nonlinear AR, all three KAHM heads improve on both
alternatives at dimensions 14 and 30, while the smaller dictionaries have
mixed effects. Because history and preprocessing are reselected at each size,
these curves do not assert monotone fitted improvement under the nesting
theorem.

Only \MatchedCalCertified\ of the \MatchedCalComparisons\ planned synthetic
calibration comparisons has a positive improvement lower endpoint: Van der
Pol, seed 5703, KAHM rollout versus RBF rollout, with a lower bound of
$0.00107$ on the all-horizon MSE reduction. The other 179 comparisons remain
unresolved. The positive statement concerns those fitted predictors within
the declared per-repetition joint event. It is separate from the descriptive
test-effect table and does not supply a population comparison for PenDigits.
