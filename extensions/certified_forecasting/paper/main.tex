\begin{abstract}
We study whether a fixed forecasting representation can meet an expected
squared-error tolerance under a decoder sensitivity budget. For bounded scalar
targets, we construct normalized minimum-risk scores on blocks of observations.
The size-two score is a pairwise obstruction; expected scores increase with
block size and converge to optimal Lipschitz risk under unconditional iid
sampling and compact metric representation support. Exact rare-conflict and
noisy-support laws show that no fixed block size is uniformly sufficient.
Independent-block calibration and estimated-mass stratification turn these
population bounds into finite-sample lower certificates. Combined with upper
bounds for fitted forecasts, they can certify minimum feasible cost among
declared representation classes. Learned Kernel Affine Hull Machine (KAHM)
dictionaries provide an expandable implementation. Fresh calibration of forty
frozen banks shows larger blocks excluding classes when pairs are inconclusive,
while stratification and full-sample empirical minimization can give stronger
decisions. Broader forecasting studies retain task-dependent accuracy and
unresolved cases. The certificates assess a representation, metric, sensitivity
budget, target, and evaluation law jointly; they do not identify every positive
lower bound as removable information loss. Informative natural-data class
exclusion remains unestablished.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Code, data, and research transparency}
The \href{https://github.com/MohitKumarRostock/Kernel_Affine_Hull_Koopman_Machine/tree/d6d74d1953a4310b73065a556df816e5ab5e3ebc/extensions/certified_forecasting}{archived research repository}
at revision \texttt{d6d74d1} contains the reference forecasting,
input-scaling, matched-geometry, lower-bound comparison, block-hierarchy,
learned-KAHM decision, learned-geometry hierarchy, controlled-decision,
speech, and speech-scaling studies in
\path{extensions/certified_forecasting}. Each study has its own protocol,
source hashes, result records, and reproduction commands.
The separate dimension-policy study of Section~\ref{sec:dimension-policy}
is supplied in the accompanying research extension
\path{extensions/dimension_certificates}; it is not part of that historical
revision. Its frozen schedule, source hashes, development/calibration/test
seals, complete analysis tables, and independent audit identify the evaluated
family and the order of data access. The accompanying numerical-records
archive provides the production records and checksums. The manuscript source
package includes the verified small analysis inputs and a generator for all
dimension-study tables, so compilation does not require that numerical archive.
Appendix~\ref{app:protocols} specifies the forecasting configurations and
independent sampling units. The separately compiled supplement retains
complete secondary experimental tables and the local KAHM implementation
details; every proof of the main results remains in this manuscript.
The reference studies' reproduction guide is
\path{extensions/certified_forecasting/docs/REPRODUCIBILITY.md}.
Public data are identified by source and checksum; download scripts preserve
attribution and license information. Appendix~\ref{app:speech-protocol} describes the speech study's evaluation
order and its implications for interpretation.
