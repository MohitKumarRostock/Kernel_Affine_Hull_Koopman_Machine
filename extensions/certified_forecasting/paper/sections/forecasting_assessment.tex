\section{Representation behavior on broader forecasting tasks}
\label{sec:experiments}

The controlled studies separate population obstruction, calibration uncertainty,
and decisions between declared classes. The following experiments examine
whether the implemented representations also give useful forecasts on broader
tasks. They do not establish universal KAHM superiority or the prevalence of
informative natural-data class exclusion. All prescribed configurations and
adverse outcomes are retained. The accompanying supplement gives the complete
forecasting, preprocessing, matched-geometry, and speech tables and protocols;
Appendix~\ref{app:protocols} defines the generators and comparison models.

\subsection{Synthetic forecasting and retained-block refinement}
\label{sec:oscillators}
\label{sec:budget-sensitivity}

The main study uses four bounded length-72 episode laws: harmonic,
quasiperiodic, nonlinear autoregressive, and logistic. Five fits per law use
128 training, 512 validation, 4096 calibration, and 2048 test episodes;
histories have lengths 4 or 12 and horizons are 1, 4, and 12. Forty candidates
per fit are frozen before calibration. Van der Pol and Duffing supply three
additional fits each. Calibration averages over origins within independent
episodes. Its failure allocation is per declared repetition, rather than
joint over the whole research campaign.

KAHM improves on long-horizon persistence for periodic signals, but quadratic
and autoregressive regressors are stronger. For nonlinear AR at horizon 12,
KAHM RMSE is $0.0743$, versus $0.0802$ for ridge AR and $0.0813$ for
quadratic regression; those alternatives are better at one step. At all-horizon
RMSE tolerance $0.35$, the main study's 800 candidates give 301 acceptances,
24 exclusions, and 475 unresolved decisions. For the 240 oscillator candidates,
the corresponding counts are 55, 30, and 155. Van der Pol horizon-12 KAHM
RMSE is $0.3814$, versus $0.2990$ for affine-hull blocks; on Duffing, KAHM
has $0.2499$, versus $0.1369$ for quadratic regression. Direct fitting also
does not uniformly improve on rollout: iterated KAHM gives $0.3408$ on
Van der Pol.

Among 480 main retained-KAHM candidate--horizon statements, 119 lower
endpoints are positive, but only two exclude tolerance $0.35$ and none
excludes $0.50$. Paired fitted-loss comparisons certify 72 of 360 main,
43 of 54 Van der Pol, and 10 of 54 Duffing adjacent-prefix improvements.
These fitted improvements differ from class exclusion. Across all 62 main,
oscillator, and budget-sensitivity fits, all 186 lower endpoints of the
complete-family validation winners are zero; 184 of 186 are zero when
selection is restricted to KAHM. Fresh budget comparisons at
$\kappa\in\{1,4,16\}$ show benefits from increasing one to four on several
tasks and little further gain at sixteen. Zero-ridge duplicate-block checks
agree within solver gaps; ridge-regularized fits need not be invariant.

geometry--decoder pair has the same prefix dimensions $2,6,14,30$, two

All \MatchedCandidates\ fits completed. KAHM has lower mean MSE in
\MatchedImproved\ of \MatchedComparisons\ contrasts and higher MSE in
\MatchedDegraded. These are descriptive, dependent contrasts. Nonlinear AR
favors KAHM over both geometric alternatives under every head and seed;
its block-direct MSE $0.00456$ also improves on ridge AR $0.00511$ and

\subsection{Preprocessing is part of the representation}
\label{sec:input-scaling}

Training-standardized histories are the original default. The primary
alternative fits coordinate minima $a_j$ and maxima $b_j$ on training data
and freezes the map
\begin{equation}
 z_j(x)=
 \begin{cases}2(x_j-a_j)/(b_j-a_j)-1,&b_j>a_j,\\
 0,&b_j=a_j.\end{cases}
 \label{eq:input-minmax}
\end{equation}
Future extrema may fall outside $[-1,1]$; clipping is a distinct representation.
Targets and their units are unchanged. Section~\ref{sec:method} explains why
the full reconstruction-and-affinity map is not invariant to this scaling.

The original-seed follow-up is descriptive; a frozen confirmation protocol
uses three new streams on each of six synthetic tasks. Duffing MSE decreases
by $7.07\%$ and $7.29\%$ in the original and confirmation stages, whereas
logistic MSE increases by $2.65\%$ and $6.97\%$ and Van der Pol MSE increases
by $3.82\%$ and $2.26\%$. The nonlinear-AR effect changes sign. On the fixed
PenDigits cohort, soft-KAHM MSE decreases by $19.79\%$ and $19.65\%$, but
hard assignments worsen by $3.96\%$ and $4.14\%$. Conventional regressors
remain stronger. These outcomes support selecting preprocessing on development
data, rather than prescribing one transformation for every task. The matched
study above makes that selection before fresh calibration.

\subsection{Natural-data calibration and its limits}
\label{sec:pendigits}
\label{sec:speech}

PenDigits uses original unnormalized trajectories with a fixed acquisition-box
transformation. Its 1751 eligible held-out digits belong to 13 inferred writer
identifiers. Losses are averaged within writers. Even under an iid eligible-writer
assumption, generic upper-RMSE endpoints near $1.56$ certify none of the
declared tolerances $0.2,0.35,0.5$. Completed-stroke eligibility defines a
retrospective population, and repeated fitting seeds do not supply additional
independent writers. The accuracy comparisons therefore remain descriptive.

The official Mini Speech Commands subset supplies 7178 eligible one-second
clips in 1630 documented recording identifiers
\citep{warden2018speech,tensorflow2026mini}. A fixed split assigns
244/163/978/245 groups to training/validation/calibration/test. The causal
observable is frame energy in decibels relative to full scale, floored at
$-60$ dB and mapped to $[0,1]$; multiplying normalized RMSE by 60 returns
dB error for that floored observable. Histories contain 4 or 12 frames and
horizons are 10, 40, and 120 ms. Losses weight identifiers equally after
averaging their origins and clips. The confidence statement assumes iid
group-record bundles; identifiers and disjoint splits do not establish that
population assumption.

Three fitting seeds compare 486 candidates per fit across eight families;
all 24 validation-selected models are frozen before held-out evaluation.
A joint $0.05$ allocation covers the 72 selected-model/horizon upper bounds.
All six learned families pass the all-horizon 18 dB criterion in every fit;
persistence and the constant model do not. No family passes the 6 or 12 dB
criterion. At 120 ms, mean test RMSE is 12.73 dB for KAHM, 12.51 for ridge
AR, 12.47 for quadratic regression, and 12.39 for Gaussian-kernel regression,
versus 14.70 for persistence. The conventional regressors also outperform
KAHM at shorter horizons. The tolerances are declared resolution levels,
not established audio-application requirements.

A later scaling analysis reuses this cohort and makes no new confidence
claim. Min--max scaling lowers KAHM all-horizon MSE by $1.41\%$, but raw
energy histories perform better still; the conventional regressors remain
stronger than every KAHM scaling arm. Thus speech demonstrates informative
upper calibration under its sampling model, while natural-data class exclusion
and a general KAHM forecasting advantage remain unestablished. The complete
observable, model grid, scoring-order record, and scaling results accompany
the supplement and Appendix~\ref{app:speech-protocol}.
