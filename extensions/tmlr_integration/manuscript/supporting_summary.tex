\section{Supporting evidence and limits of transfer}
\label{sec:supporting-evidence}

Complementary studies examine the information retained by geometric
features, access by finite decoders, transfer to natural series, and
learning or updating the geometry. They use different representations,
baselines, splits, loss scales, and sampling units from the bounded
reconstruction-departure experiment. Their outcomes therefore describe
separate fitted procedures; they are not pooled into its confidence
family. Table~\ref{tab:supporting-evidence} summarizes the evidence.
The companion \emph{Supplementary Studies} document provides the complete
definitions, proofs, protocols, comparison tables, and failure accounting.

\paragraph{Information and finite-decoder access.}
In a controlled branch construction, the same observations and noise
support targets that vary from branch identity to signed position.
An affine reconstruction can be nearly exact while its discrepancy-based
code loses the position required for prediction. Affine local coordinates
recover accurate prediction in that example, so the benefit is not
exclusive to nonlinear reconstruction. In a separate finite-decoder
comparison, KAHM local features outperform raw and affine local features
on Van der Pol at both 64 and 256 Gaussian landmarks. Raw histories
perform better on nonlinear autoregression, and both controls perform
better on Duffing. The inferential units are 24 independent full
repetitions per setting, with separate multiplicity adjustments for the
original and follow-up studies.

\paragraph{Natural-data transfer and model selection.}
The reserved-source comparison uses Traffic, Hospital, Saugeen River
flow, and US births. Raw-history augmentation with KAHM features has
4.26\% higher source-balanced scaled MSE than raw RBF and costs 2.30
times as much in the measured warm-library workflow. Raw ridge is more
accurate than both. The favorable development mean is strongly
influenced by FRED-MD, and it does not establish a KAHM-specific benefit.
Adding geometric descriptors to metadata and a paid validation probe
changes none of the 80 confirmation decisions. The post-hoc min--max
scaling analysis reuses these sources and simulation draws; it changes
individual errors but does not reverse the principal mean-loss orderings.

\paragraph{Residual correction and learned geometry.}
An additive correction preserves the fitted raw baseline as a candidate,
but validation can select a correction that worsens test error. The
synthetic factorial resolves gains over raw RBF in five of twelve
comparisons, while a simpler geometric correction has lower mean error
in every Van der Pol and Duffing cell. On the archive roster, the two
capacities of one Car Parts series produce eight failed geometric fits
and four unavailable policy outputs. The planned full-roster geometric
and policy losses are consequently undefined. A post-hoc comparison on
22 successful configurations gives additive KAHM only a 0.31\% gain
over raw RBF at 13.29 times its measured model-pipeline cost; ridge is
more accurate and cheaper on that subset.

The donor-residual study completes all 48 tasks. Its learned-metric,
out-of-fold KAHM procedure improves the ridge reference on the three
synthetic laws and has 2.18\% lower descriptive archive loss. However,
the metric-versus-identity and donor-versus-in-sample intervals include
zero. No adjusted comparison with an equally trained geometric control
favors KAHM. These results support residual correction in the evaluated
settings without identifying the proposed metric or KAHM reconstruction
as the necessary source of that improvement.

\paragraph{Training and adaptation.}
Direct reconstruction training produces two favorable, six unfavorable,
and ten unresolved primary contrasts. Neither favorable contrast is
against an equally shape-trained non-KAHM local control. Three selected
rideshare series have no eligible training-query windows, leaving the
planned four-collection natural aggregate undefined; results on the
seven available series form a separate descriptive comparison. A
nine-task gate-training pilot uses a separate generator and budget and
does not contribute to those intervals.

The online study meets its declared 99th-percentile service-time screen,
but both capacity settings fail the recurring-stream and natural-data
accuracy criteria. A corrected ridge solver removes computational
exceptions while retaining an extreme forecast that a fresh solve also
reproduces. Thus a nonvanishing reconstruction denominator and an accurate
linear solve do not by themselves control feature extrapolation. The
bounded, fixed-geometry decoder analyzed in the main experiment is a
different procedure; its correction bound does not validate a repair of
the online method.
