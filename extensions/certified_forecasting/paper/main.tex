\begin{abstract}
Kernel Affine Hull Machines (KAHMs) represent data through local geometric
reconstruction. We use their soft associations to forecast observed signals
and assess accuracy against a specified error tolerance. Retaining dictionary
blocks under a bound on the sum of block Frobenius norms preserves earlier predictors and
prevents duplication from enlarging the feasible class. We derive a population
risk lower bound from pairs whose target separation exceeds that allowed by
the decoder's Lipschitz budget. This pairwise obstruction is exact for a
distribution supported on two points and dominates a conditional root-variance
bound under the same pairing law. Calibration on independent episodes combines
this obstruction with fitted-risk upper bounds and paired improvement
comparisons, allowing unresolved accuracy decisions. The theory extends to
other fixed representations. Experiments cover a known-risk benchmark, six
synthetic dynamics, and writer-separated pen trajectories. Matched controls
show KAHM gains on nonlinear autoregression and pen trajectories, while
affine-hull alternatives perform better on oscillators. Preprocessing produces
both gains and regressions. Follow-ups on the same writer cohort are
descriptive. The guarantees concern expected squared error under a specified
episode and forecast-origin law; few independent evaluation units can leave
the bounds uninformative.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Code, data, and research transparency}
The \href{https://github.com/MohitKumarRostock/Kernel_Affine_Hull_Koopman_Machine/tree/research/certified-soft-state-forecasting/extensions/certified_forecasting}{accompanying repository} provides the implementation, frozen configurations,
result summaries, and figure generators in
\path{extensions/certified_forecasting}; editable manuscript files are in its
\path{paper} directory. The original experiments, input-scaling follow-up, and
matched geometry--decoder comparison are archived separately. Their records
include configurations, source hashes, and documented exact replays of damaged
score archives in the scaling study. The evidence packages identify retained
score arrays, and fixed-seed runners regenerate the full episode-level
archives. Appendix~\ref{app:protocols} specifies the experimental
configurations and independent sampling units.
