\section{Experiments with known sampling laws}
\label{sec:experiments}

The experiments ask whether the confidence statements cover known risks,
whether KAHM soft associations support accurate forecasts relative to hard
assignments and alternative representations, whether retained blocks yield
certified improvements, and whether the resulting bounds support useful
accuracy decisions. The original campaigns establish the tasks and baseline
protocols. Separate follow-ups examine KAHM input scaling
(Section~\ref{sec:input-scaling}) and compare geometric families with equal
preprocessing, decoder, and dictionary-size options
(Section~\ref{sec:matched-geometry}). Every candidate and completed fitting
seed is retained, together with the configurations, random streams, raw
certificate scores, and machine-readable results. The aggregates include
adverse and inconclusive outcomes.

\subsection{Exact-risk calibration and small retained variation}

The controlled experiment uses two input atoms with probabilities $p$ and $1-p$,
targets $-1$ and $1$, and soft coordinates
$((1-a)/2,(1+a)/2)$ and their reversal. Their distance is $\sqrt2a$.
Proposition~\ref{prop:two-atom} gives the exact optimum
$p(1-p)(2-\kappa\sqrt2a)_+^2$. We also fit the constrained two-atom predictor
using 256 independent training observations, and compute its true population
risk analytically rather than substituting a test estimate. The comparison
uses the pair lower bound, the centered raw-target version of the earlier
variance certificate, and the fitted-model upper bound. Each receives a
failure allocation of $0.05/3$; details of the centered correction and the
fitted risk appear in Appendix~\ref{app:known-risk}.

The parameter grid is
\[
p\in\{.2,.5\},\qquad a\in\{0,.2,.6,1\},\qquad
\kappa\in\{.5,1,2\}.
\]
Calibration sizes are $n\in\{64,256,1024,4096\}$, with 300 independent repetitions per condition:
28,800 trials in total. One pair lower bound exceeds its exact oracle risk;
the centered lower and fitted upper bounds have no violations in this finite
campaign. Thus 28,799 of the 28,800 trials satisfy all three reported directions.
This is a descriptive Monte Carlo check of these configurations, not an
empirical proof of the coverage theorem or a global confidence statement over
the entire campaign. The single violation is retained in the released records.

\centering\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/known_risk.pdf}
\caption{Controlled two-atom experiment, $p=.5$ and $\kappa=1$. Lines average
300 trials at each calibration size. The exact optimum is known analytically.
Both methods use the same constant reference class; no comparison across
different conditioning laws is implied. The fitted upper bound concerns the
empirically fitted predictor, whose population risk can exceed the oracle.}
\label{fig:known-risk}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:known-risk} shows the effect of directly estimating the pair
discrepancy. For the collapsed map $a=0$, the exact minimum MSE is one.
At $n=4096$, the mean pair lower endpoint is $0.9177$, compared with
$0.5327$ for the centered endpoint; the mean fitted upper endpoint is $1.0200$.
The centered input-variance estimate is exactly zero here, but its additive
confidence correction is subsequently square-rooted, producing an
$n^{-1/4}$-order input penalty. The pair statistic avoids that particular
operation. For $a=.6$ and the same $n$, the exact optimum is $0.3315$,
the mean pair and centered lower endpoints are $0.2980$ and $0.2230$,
and the mean fitted upper endpoint is $0.3451$. At small samples, both lower
methods frequently return zero. We do not infer a new general minimax rate or
uniform finite-sample dominance from these controlled comparisons.

\subsection{Bounded time-series forecasting}

The main learning experiment uses four independently sampled finite-episode
generators: a noisy harmonic oscillator with random frequency and phase, a
quasiperiodic signal with two components, a nonlinear autoregression with
bounded innovations, and a logistic map with an episode-specific parameter.
Their definitions guarantee observed values in $[-1,1]$. The precise equations,
initialization, noise ranges, and burn-in conventions are given in
Appendix~\ref{app:protocols}. Independence is between generated episodes, not
between their overlapping histories.

For each generator, five complete learning repetitions use 128 training,
512 validation, 4096 calibration, and 2048 independent test episodes of
length 72. Histories have length four or twelve. Horizons are $1,4,12$ and
the common origins are $11,15,\ldots,59$, giving 13 origins per episode.
All geometric maps are relearned in each repetition. The main decoder budget
is $\kappa=4$, ridge coefficient $10^{-4}$, and KAHM sharpness $\omega=16$.
The four retained dictionaries have $2,4,8,16$ coordinates. Up to 1600 training
histories enter geometry construction and up to 24 local training points enter
each KAHM model. The decoder uses all available training rows.

There are 40 frozen candidates per repetition. They include retained KAHM,
radial-basis-function (RBF), and local affine-hull features; hard KAHM state
assignments; ordinary Frobenius-budget and exact-duplicate ablations;
ridge autoregression; direct quadratic regression; affine feature-transition
rollouts on KAHM and polynomial dictionaries; persistence; and a constant model.
The RBF and affine-hull alternatives are separate constructions, not relabeled
KAHMs. A common numerical norm budget across different feature maps does not
make their statistical capacities identical. These fixed configurations test
the procedure and its failure modes, rather than establishing a tuned
state-of-the-art accuracy frontier.

The risk endpoints allocate $0.04$ over the $2\times40\times3$ selected tails,
giving $\alpha=1/6000$ per tail. The remaining $0.01$ is allocated over
18 adjacent-prefix improvement comparisons, giving $1/1800$ each. This yields
one joint 95\% event per complete learning repetition. Confidence is not
claimed jointly across all 20 independent experimental repetitions. The upper
sample size is 4096 and the lower sample size is 2048 disjoint episode pairs;
the 13 within-episode origins do not multiply these sample sizes.

\input{tables/forecast_results}

Table~\ref{tab:forecast} reports the independent-test behavior of a
validation-selected candidate from each family. KAHM soft states improve
substantially over persistence at long horizons in the harmonic and
quasiperiodic tasks, but quadratic and autoregressive models are stronger on
these particular generators. In the nonlinear autoregression, the KAHM model
has mean test RMSE $0.0743$ at horizon 12, compared with $0.0802$ for ridge AR
and $0.0813$ for quadratic regression; the simple autoregressive models are
better at horizon one. These results support task-dependent behavior rather
than a universal benefit from geometric soft coordinates. Direct KAHM fitting
also improves mean horizon-12 error relative to its iterated feature-transition
counterpart on the two periodic tasks. That difference is empirical and does
not establish that direct fitting dominates rollout in general.

\subsection{Useful certificates, class exclusions, and refinement decisions}

\input{tables/certified_selection}

Table~\ref{tab:selection} reports the actual tolerance decisions. For the
retained KAHM family, every nonlinear-AR repetition certifies RMSE at most
$0.20$ at all three horizons. Every quasiperiodic repetition certifies $0.35$,
and every harmonic repetition certifies $0.50$ but none certifies $0.35$.
When all comparison models are eligible, the harmonic task also certifies
$0.35$ in every repetition. No candidate certifies the $0.50$ all-horizon
requirement for the logistic task. This last result is an unresolved decision;
it does not establish that the true risk of every candidate exceeds $0.50$.

The upper bounds can be conservative even with thousands of episodes.
For example, the selected harmonic quadratic model has mean horizon-one
test RMSE $0.0311$ but mean upper endpoint $0.1497$. The bound is useful for
some requested tolerances and uninformative for others. Reporting only its
coverage or positivity would obscure this practical distinction.

Across the 480 KAHM candidate--horizon statements, 119 have a positive lower
endpoint. Such an endpoint identifies a restriction on the stated class,
whereas a zero endpoint leaves its adequacy unresolved. Figure~\ref{fig:refinement}
shows how increasing retained resolution changes the obstruction and the
fitted error. The block metric and common budget are held fixed in meaning
throughout this comparison. The certificate neither labels every geometric
change as useful nor assumes that an observed improvement of a training
objective generalizes.

The paired comparison certifies positive risk reduction in 72 of the 360
adjacent KAHM refinement statements, under the per-repetition joint event.
Table~\ref{tab:improvements} retains the inconclusive comparisons. The nonlinear
AR generator has no certified adjacent-prefix gain in this study, even though
its fitted models can meet a useful overall accuracy tolerance. Its coarse
representation already captures much of the persistent regime structure.
This illustrates why a fitted-risk certificate and a refinement-improvement
certificate answer different questions.

Figure~\ref{fig:duplication} isolates the replication issue without confounding
training noise. Numerical zero-ridge tests independently check that fitting a
single block and exact copies gives matching optimum training risk within the
recorded solver gaps. The ordinary main-study duplicate candidate uses zero
ridge while the primary models use $10^{-4}$; differences between those fitted
candidates are not used as proof of replication invariance.

\subsection{Numerical and reproducibility verification}

All 20 scheduled main runs completed and all 800 fitted candidates are retained.
The largest recorded block-solver first-order gap is below $10^{-7}$.
An independent audit reconstructs all 4800 selected one-sided risk endpoints
and all 360 improvement bounds from the saved double-precision episode scores,
with maximum discrepancy zero. A fresh replay of the first harmonic run
matches every non-timing result and all 120 retained score arrays exactly,
including 983,040 scalar entries. These are numerical reproducibility checks;
they do not replace the probability arguments or constitute formal verification
of floating-point arithmetic. The full source and configuration hashes, failure
accounting, and verification output are archived.
