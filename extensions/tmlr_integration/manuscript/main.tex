\begin{abstract}
We investigate when Kernel Affine Hull Machine (KAHM) reconstruction
supplies useful functions for a finite time-series forecast decoder.
Exact identities show that direct normalized target transfer is a
kernel-ridge ratio and that some symmetric banks give redundant raw
features. An irregular-reference construction proves that KAHM
reconstruction departures can instead extend the raw feature span of the
common affine coordinates and matched RBF reconstruction. A separately bounded feature map and centered
ridge readout give a uniform correction-magnitude bound; this generic
stability result does not guarantee forecast accuracy.
We then complete a locally frozen, matched experiment with ten procedures,
192 task suites and 24 independent repetitions across three known
nonlinear laws and a linear base-law control, at two added-noise levels.
The bounded KAHM departure reduces nonlinear aggregate normalized MSE by
\RMKvsCommonGain\% against the shared gate-and-coordinate basis, meeting
the fixed 3\% useful-effect criterion. Its point gains against matched
RBF departure and raw RBF are \RMKvsRBFDepartureGain\% and
\RMKvsRawRBFGain\%; neither establishes the required 3\% margin under
the six-comparison simultaneous analysis. KAHM error is 1.77\% higher
than boosted trees and 6.04\% higher than the validation-selected
non-KAHM policy. All declared stability screens and the linear-control
noninferiority condition pass. Thus two of six required comparisons
pass and the fixed decision stops this forecasting-performance branch.
The earlier direct-training and amended online-development results
remain separate, including unfavorable comparisons and failures.
The completed evidence establishes conditional basis distinctness,
bounded corrections and a useful reconstruction contribution beyond the
common basis, while leaving a distinctive practical KAHM forecasting
advantage unestablished. The conclusion is restricted to the declared
procedures and known laws; it is not an impossibility theorem for all
geometric predictors.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
A useful forecasting representation must make target-relevant structure
accessible to the decoder that is actually fitted. Kernel Affine Hull
Machines (KAHMs) construct nonlinear reconstruction maps from local
reference observations \citep{Kumar2024KAHM}. Accurate reconstruction alone
does not show that these maps help predict a future observation. Nor does
algebraic nonlinearity establish a useful statistical contribution at a
specified sample size and computation budget. This paper studies those
questions separately: whether a reconstruction supplies a distinct basis,
whether its correction can be controlled, and whether the complete fitted
procedure improves reserved forecasts beyond matched alternatives.

The distinction begins with the decoder's inputs. A local reconstructor can
recover every point on an affine line while its zero residual discards
position. Existing local KAHM forecasters already expose gated principal
coordinates as well as reconstruction scores, so a new experiment must
account for those coordinates. We therefore examine full-coordinate
\emph{reconstruction departures}: the difference between a nonlinear
reconstruction and its matched local affine projection, multiplied by the
same locality gate. The common gate and principal-coordinate block is
identical in the KAHM and RBF arms. An affine departure is identically zero,
providing the nested common-feature control. Deterministic reconstruction
cannot add information beyond the observed history; it can change the
functions available to a restricted, regularized decoder.

The algebra makes this candidate testable without assuming it is novel or
useful. Directly transferring normalized KAHM weights to reference targets
is exactly a normalized kernel-ridge ratio. Some symmetric reference banks
also yield identical augmented raw KAHM and RBF feature spans. An explicit
irregular three-reference construction, by contrast, proves that raw
KAHM departures need not lie in the common-plus-RBF span. These results
establish conditional basis distinctness, not a new general regression
principle. Ridge penalties and the separately bounded feature transforms
are not invariant under arbitrary reparameterizations, so empirical
attribution concerns the complete declared procedure
(Appendix~\ref{app:reconstruction-mechanism}).

Stability is a second requirement. A preceding online development study
completed 144 amended runs and 387,900 scored forecasts. A documented
solver repair removed numerical exceptions, yet extreme corrections
remained and were reproduced by a fresh solve in an examined state
(Section~\ref{sec:adaptive-development}). A correct linear solve does not
control extrapolation of its input features. The final fixed-geometry
experiment therefore uses A-fitted blockwise normalization, an explicit
radial bound, and centered ridge residual heads. We prove a uniform bound
on the correction's magnitude, with the free intercept and mean-SSE
penalty convention stated explicitly. It is generic ridge algebra; it
neither bounds the raw baseline nor guarantees accuracy, and it does not
retroactively repair the earlier adaptive procedure.

The final experiment compares ten complete forecasting procedures across
three previously studied nonlinear laws and a linear Gaussian base-law
control, at two added observation-noise levels. Twenty-four independent
repetitions give 192 task suites. Separate episode splits A, B, C and D, generated from independent base
episodes and unit-noise streams with a shared A-estimated noise amplitude,
respectively fit the maps, select horizon-specific hyperparameters,
calibrate correction strengths and a non-KAHM policy, and evaluate the
frozen choices. Prepared inputs, scientific source and the six-contrast
analysis were frozen locally before reserved forecasts were computed.
This is a prospective local checkpoint, not public preregistration.
The five nonlinear contrasts require a useful improvement of at least
3\% over the common block, matched RBF departure, raw RBF, boosted trees
and the C-selected non-KAHM policy. A sixth contrast requires
noninferiority within 5\% of ridge on the linear control. These are
prespecified research-allocation margins, not application-derived
utility thresholds. Section~\ref{sec:reconstruction-mechanism} reports
the complete result and its fixed decision.

The cumulative evidence remains separated by study
(Table~\ref{tab:evidence-status}). Historical controlled interventions
identify information lost by reconstruction scores and show conditional
finite-decoder gains. Natural-source and scaling studies delimit transfer.
An additive correction study preserves failed fits and an explicitly
amended successful-subset analysis. Shared proxy-metric learning does not
establish its added value. Direct training of KAHM bandwidths and gate
temperatures against forecast error yields two favorable, six unfavorable
and ten unresolved synthetic comparisons; none of its favorable primary
intervals is against an equally trained non-KAHM local control. Three
planned natural tasks lack training-query windows, leaving that full
aggregate undefined. The adaptive development study fails its accuracy
criteria at both capacity tiers. These findings motivate the final
mechanism test; they are not pooled with it as additional independent
observations.

The contribution is a completed investigation of three separable
properties. First, exact identities, a redundancy condition and an
irregular-bank construction identify what the normalized KAHM map can
add to the chosen raw feature span. Second, an explicit bounded-feature
readout gives a reproducible correction guarantee under its stated
assumptions. Third, a matched, fixed-budget experiment tests whether this
procedure supplies useful forecasting value, with complete selection,
failure and cost accounting. The earlier empirical record supplies
context and counterexamples, while retaining its original inferential
scope. Local coordinates, learned forecasting representations,
differentiable ridge solves \citep{Bertinetto2019Solvers} and residual
correction are established ideas. The research claim concerns these
specific geometric constructions and their observed predictive limits.

A new realization of a known simulation law is not a new application.
Likewise, a failed finite procedure is not an impossibility theorem for
all decoders on a representation. The final experiment's stopping rule
makes those boundaries operational: an adverse or unresolved result at
the fixed budget ends this forecasting-performance branch without
selecting a favorable subset or expanding the same experiment. The
stronger general question is which representation and decoder conditions
permit prediction, a question that requires its own formal assumptions
and evidence rather than an unsupported performance claim.

\section*{Reproducibility and research transparency}
The public repository preserves the original scientific source code,
execution freezes, and archived outcomes separately from the
\path{extensions/forecasting_utility} and
\path{extensions/target_geometry} studies. Historical bundles retain
their original scope: the natural forecast arrays reconstruct 2,496
task--method losses, and the separate scaling analysis contains 1,664
natural method runs and 1,440 controlled head fits. Its compact controlled
episode losses and recorded full-prediction audit are distinguished from
recreating the omitted full controlled prediction arrays. Those archives
are not silently replaced by the present extension.

The earlier forecasting-utility extension has its own protocol and dependency freeze, published
before full evaluation, and a reproducibility bundle retaining all 1,336
successful standalone forecasts from 1,344 attempted fits in 168
configurations. Eight geometric fits and four dependent policy evaluations
fail on two Car Parts configurations; their records remain in the bundle.
Successful policy forecasts reuse their selected standalone outputs.
A dated reporting amendment records the post-hoc matched-success archive
diagnostic, while preserving the undefined full-roster geometric losses.
The bundle also retains targets,
split/origin hashes, complete validation candidates, selected settings,
warning/failure accounting, dataset provenance, measured computation nodes,
and analysis tables. It covers this extension; it does not claim to embed
the original studies' complete bundles or the separate certificate paper.
A distinct manuscript-source bundle contains the editable \LaTeX{} and
generated displays for this revised research draft.

The independent forecasting-utility audit reconstructs final losses from saved
predictions, checks the fixed selector decisions and their cost
dependencies, and verifies exact baseline recovery at zero correction.
The manuscript asset builder independently reconstructs the residual
alignment identity and checks the reported hierarchical aggregation.
Generated tables, figures, and numerical macros carry input/output hashes.
These computational checks establish consistency of supplied evidence;
they do not create independent replications, establish a universal
complexity criterion, or replace scientific review.

The target-geometry study has a distinct public protocol checkpoint
\texttt{0d7ee3c17515eb1d922cdd302facd2b7874dc9a9}, created before its
confirmatory execution. Its 55-file scientific freeze includes the
protocol, numerical source, input roster, prepared arrays and independent
analysis. The complete 48-task run retains all 768 final forecasts,
validation candidates, donor boundaries, metric objectives, warnings,
input hashes and computation nodes. Its independent audit reconstructs
inputs and final losses, verifies donor exclusions and the metric proxy,
and checks selection against recorded grids without independently
refitting every validation candidate. No reporting amendment or task
replacement was needed for this run. That study's reproduction bundle covers
this target-geometry study and its frozen numerical dependencies; historical
archives retain their stated scope. The editable manuscript bundle includes
all generated displays needed to compile the integrated draft.

The direct-reconstruction extension has its own public checkpoint
\texttt{39174d06dffc7e28fed73dbbf866e539560dd021}. It preserves 32
scientific files and all 46 prepared input sets before model evaluation.
The three anticipated rideshare availability failures remain in its roster;
its planned four-collection efficacy aggregate is undefined. This freeze
is separate from the exploratory gate pilot and from all previous
execution checkpoints. The separate direct-reconstruction reproduction bundle includes that frozen
extension, exact inherited numerical dependencies, selected fitted states,
optimizer trials, predictions and failure records, as well as the
separately preserved pilot. Repository task archives losslessly contain
original execution files; unpacking verifies their identities before the
frozen audit consumes them. These post-execution containers are packaging,
not revisions of the scientific protocol or recorded outcomes.

The final reconstruction-mechanism study has a separate local checkpoint
\texttt{6d666b0fa3d8e0ee90a357ea7970def94e9fdda7}. Its freeze records
239 source, configuration and input files, including the complete
192-task prepared roster, before the first reserved forecast. Publication
of this local record after execution does not make it public
preregistration. All ten procedures finish on every task, giving 1,920
saved task--procedure prediction arrays and 9,584,640 scored forecast
values. The inferential units remain 24 independent repetition
aggregates. The frozen analyzer imports no forecasting implementation
and reconstructs every loss and all six fixed contrasts from saved
predictions. A separate internal audit uses independently written
feature evaluation, ridge forward passes and tree traversal; it checks
selected normal equations, window chronology, B/C selections and the
six primary statistics without refitting the forecasters. Recorded
scores for discarded B candidates can be checked for selection
consistency, but their unsaved coefficients are not independently
replayed. This is computational verification, not external replication.

Selected-state replay reproduces all 1,920 prediction arrays exactly on
the recorded environment. Per-method resource charges, query norm and
signed-denominator diagnostics, all selected multipliers and complete
condition/horizon tables accompany the data. Post-execution lossless
archives may replace bulky loose evidence files in the public tree;
their inventories and restoration helper verify every original byte
before the frozen checks run. Packaging changes neither source hashes
nor scientific outcomes. The manuscript asset builders reconstruct the
adaptive and final-mechanism displays from their separate saved records.
The current source ZIP compiles independently of the forecasting code,
and the new reproduction ZIP covers the completed mechanism study and
its exact inherited dependencies. Historical studies retain their own
reproduction packages and their documented numerical sensitivities.
