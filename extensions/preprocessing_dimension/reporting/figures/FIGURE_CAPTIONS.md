# Figure captions

## Figure 1

Preprocessing and native local-dimension cap in the primary complete-head procedure. Curves show the three diagonal scaling arms. Shaded bands and selected-cap error bars are descriptive unadjusted 95% intervals of mean SMSE over 24 independent repetition aggregates. The B-selected marker aggregates task-specific choices and is therefore not positioned at a single numerical cap. Caps 12 and 20 are numerically equivalent at the fixed ambient history dimension 12. All forecasts are C-calibrated.

## Figure 2

Primary selected-context comparison of ten complete forecasting procedures on the nonlinear panel. Dots and error bars are mean SMSE and descriptive unadjusted 95% intervals across 24 independent repetition aggregates. These mean intervals are not paired tests of procedure differences. The paired primary comparisons use the separate declared four-member family.

## Figure 3

Secondary fixed-common experiment: incremental forecasting gain beyond the identical common-plus-shared-bias baseline within each row. KAHM and RBF use independently B-selected departure caps; matched RBF uses KAHM's selected cap with its own penalty and C multiplier. The five rows use the common/departure preprocessing pairs shown. Intervals are descriptive unadjusted 95% paired-t intervals for SMSE improvement, linearly rescaled by the observed mean baseline SMSE. They are not confidence intervals for an unknown risk ratio. The P/R sample allocation differs from the primary complete-head experiment; the rankings must not be pooled.

## Figure 4

Four prespecified claims with their mean margin gaps and simultaneous one-sided lower bounds. For nonlinear comparisons the gap is 0.97 times comparator loss minus candidate loss; for linear noninferiority it is 1.05 times ridge loss minus KAHM loss. The paired-t bounds use 24 independent repetition aggregates and Bonferroni family allocation 0.05/4. The plotted values divide absolute SMSE margin gaps by the observed mean comparator SMSE and multiply by 100 for legibility. This display rescaling does not change the sign or the claim decision and is not a risk-ratio confidence interval. No upper confidence bound is shown.
