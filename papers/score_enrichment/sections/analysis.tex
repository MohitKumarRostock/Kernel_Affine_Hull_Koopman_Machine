\section{Information retention and candidate observable spaces}\label{sec:analysis}
The statements in this section condition on the fitted reference geometry. Information recovery concerns exact encodings; it is distinct from a guarantee for a finite fitted decoder. Complete proofs of the propositions are in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.

\subsection{What normalization retains}
\begin{proposition}[Recovery of the score vector]\label{prop:normalizer}
Fix $\tau>0$, $\omega>0$, and a partition. The pair $(\phi^m,\zeta^m)$ determines its score vector through
\begin{equation}
 T_j^m(z)=1+\tau-\exp\left(\frac{\log\phi_j^m(z)+\zeta_{b(j)}^m(z)}{\omega}\right),
\label{eq:inverse}
\end{equation}
where $b(j)$ is the partition containing $j$. Conversely, the scores determine both memberships and normalizers. For a partition with $q$ models,
\[
 \phi_j^m(z)\geq\frac{\tau^\omega}{\tau^\omega+(q-1)(1+\tau)^\omega}>0.
\]
\end{proposition}
Thus normalizers recover the full scores, not information already lost when reconstruction vectors were reduced to scores. Memberships alone retain only relative affinities. For example, two distinct constant score vectors give the same uniform memberships but different normalizers. This is an elementary property of the normalization, not a new inverse-learning algorithm.

The exact lower bound is not a practical conditioning guarantee. With $\tau=10^{-6}$, $\omega=16$, and four models, it is about $3.33\times10^{-97}$. Stable logarithmic evaluation preserves the normalization without a small-sum override; it cannot make division by a very small membership well conditioned. Also, an information-equivalent nonlinear reparameterization generally changes linear predictors, Gaussian distances, and isotropic penalties. Equal information does not imply equal fitted forecasts.

\subsection{What local coordinates retain}
Let $v_j(z)=\phi_j^m(z)P_j^\top(z-\mu_j)$ and let $\mathcal P=\col_j P_j^\top\in\R^{(\sum_jr_j)\times d}$.
\begin{proposition}[Input recovery and conditioning]\label{prop:local}
If every membership is positive and $\mathcal P$ has column rank $d$, then $\Gamma^m_{\Loc}$ is injective. Define
\[
 q(z)=\col_j\left(\frac{v_j(z)}{\phi_j^m(z)}+P_j^\top\mu_j\right).
\]
The input is then recovered by $z=(\mathcal P^\top\mathcal P)^{-1}\mathcal P^\top q(z)$.
Moreover, suppose two inputs $z,z'$ satisfy $\phi_j^m(z),\phi_j^m(z')\geq\varepsilon>0$ and $\|P_j^\top(z'-\mu_j)\|_2\leq M$ for all $j$. Writing $v=\col_jv_j$ and letting $\sigma_{\min}(\mathcal P)>0$ be the smallest singular value,
\begin{equation}
 \|z-z'\|_2\leq
 \frac{\|v(z)-v(z')\|_2+M\|\phi^m(z)-\phi^m(z')\|_2}
 {\varepsilon\sigma_{\min}(\mathcal P)}.
 \label{eq:conditioning}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
The condition is sufficient, not necessary: a rank-deficient stack does not establish information loss because the gates can themselves distinguish inputs. Conversely, full rank does not guarantee a stable inverse, dimensionality reduction, or low fitted error. The local construction can be wider than its input, and the finite decoder need not implement the inverse above.

\paragraph{Exact reconstruction can yield constant memberships.}
If all affine bases in one partition have rank $d$, then $\mathcal A^A_j(z)=z$ for every model. At a nonzero query all scores are zero; at zero all share the zero-norm score convention. Equation~\eqref{eq:memberships} therefore gives uniform memberships at every input. Exact reconstruction by these affine models supplies no distinction through their memberships alone. Their gated full local coordinates, however, retain the input. The experiment retains full-rank affine candidates rather than imposing a rank restriction to avoid this behavior.

\subsection{Raw reconstruction spans and the Koopman connection}\label{sec:rawspan}
For this comparison only, fix a single local geometry and one \emph{common} scalar gate $g(z)$. Suppress the local index, put $n=n_j$, and write $Q,G,C=(G+\lambda I_n)^{-1},P,\mu$ for its references, Gram matrix, inverse, basis, and mean, with $\lambda>0$. Let $\mathcal S_0$ be the real linear span of $g$ and the coordinates of $g(z)P^\top(z-\mu)$. Define $d_m=g(\mathcal A^m-\mathcal A^A)$ for $m=K,R$. Adjoining a vector to a span means adjoining all its scalar coordinate functions.

\begin{proposition}[Equal augmented raw spans]\label{prop:equal}
Suppose $G\mathbf1_n=\kappa\mathbf1_n$ and $C\mathcal V\subseteq\mathcal V$, where $\mathcal V=\Span\{\mathbf1_n,Q_{:1},\ldots,Q_{:d}\}\subseteq\R^n$ and $Q_{:i}$ is column $i$ of $Q$. Then
\[
 \Span(\mathcal S_0,d_K)=\Span(\mathcal S_0,d_R).
\]
Adjoining a common intercept preserves the equality.
\end{proposition}
The assumption concerns a subspace of reference-coordinate vectors, not a state-transition law. It includes symmetric two-reference examples for which the reconstruction formulas differ but their augmented spans agree. It does not hold for every bank.

\begin{proposition}[An irregular scalar geometry]\label{prop:distinct}
Let $q_1<q_2<q_3$ with $q_1+q_3\ne2q_2$, define $k_i(z)=\exp(-\beta(z-q_i)^2/2)$ for $\beta>0$, and let $G_{ij}=\exp(-\beta(q_i-q_j)^2/2)$. Set $C=(G+\lambda I_3)^{-1}$ for $\lambda>0$ and suppose $C\mathbf1_3$ is entrywise positive. The reconstruction maps use $Q=(q_1,q_2,q_3)^\top$ in Equation~\eqref{eq:reconstructions}. For $g=1$ and $\mathcal A^A(z)=z$, as scalar functions on $\R$,
\[
 \mathcal A^K\notin\Span(1,z,\mathcal A^R),\qquad
 d_K\notin\Span(1,z,d_R).
\]
\end{proposition}
The appendix gives an explicit admissible example. These propositions delimit raw expressivity; neither orders regularized risks. In particular, they do not compare $\Gamma^K$ and $\Gamma^R$, whose gates generally differ and whose scores are nonlinear functions of reconstruction.

For a specified deterministic evolution $F:\mathcal Z\to\mathcal Z$, the Koopman operator acts on scalar functions as $\mathcal U_F f=f\circ F$. Exact linear coordinate evolution requires invariance of their coordinate-function span, as discussed by \citet{KumarKargaran2026Limits}. Equal raw spaces have the same invariance status under this fixed operator. Proposition~\ref{prop:distinct} permits different candidate spaces but establishes invariance of neither. Moreover, an injective encoding admits a conjugated evolution on its image, but that evolution need not be linear. The observed-history forecasting experiment below does not assume a deterministic Markov state or fit a transition matrix. These qualifications prevent a direct-output gain from being read as evidence of autonomous closure.
