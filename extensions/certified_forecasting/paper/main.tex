\begin{abstract}
We study when a forecasting representation can meet an accuracy requirement
under a prescribed decoder sensitivity. Kernel Affine Hull Machine (KAHM)
dictionaries provide a learned, expandable setting for this general question.
For bounded scalar targets, we construct normalized minimum-risk problems on
blocks of observations. Their size-two member is a pairwise obstruction;
their expectations increase to the optimal Lipschitz risk under compactness
and unconditional iid sampling. Exact rare-conflict and noisy-support laws
show why no fixed block size is universally sufficient. Independent-block
calibration and estimated-mass stratification give lower risk certificates.
Combined with fitted-risk upper bounds and retained representation classes,
these can certify minimum feasible cost among declared alternatives.
Experiments distinguish population slack from finite-sample uncertainty.
Fresh calibration on forty frozen learned KAHM banks demonstrates class
exclusion with larger blocks where pairs are inconclusive, while retaining
cases favoring stratification and full-sample empirical minimization.
Broader synthetic, pen-trajectory, and speech studies show task-dependent
accuracy and preprocessing effects. The guarantees concern expected squared
error for a fixed target and evaluation law; they distinguish class-level
limitations from fitted accuracy without identifying all limitations as
removable representation loss. Informative natural-data class exclusion
remains limited.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Code, data, and research transparency}
The \href{https://github.com/MohitKumarRostock/Kernel_Affine_Hull_Koopman_Machine/tree/research/certified-soft-state-forecasting/extensions/certified_forecasting}{accompanying repository} provides the implementation, frozen configurations,
result summaries, and figure generators in
\path{extensions/certified_forecasting}; editable manuscript files are in its
\path{paper} directory. The forecasting, input-scaling, matched-geometry,
lower-bound comparison, block-hierarchy, learned-KAHM decision, learned-geometry hierarchy, controlled
decision, speech, and speech-scaling studies
have separate
protocols and result directories. These provide configurations, source hashes,
per-episode scores, and outcomes for every prescribed configuration.
Appendix~\ref{app:protocols} specifies the forecasting configurations and
independent sampling units. The separately compiled supplement retains
complete secondary experimental tables and the inherited local KAHM
implementation details; every proof of the main results remains in this
manuscript. The reproduction guide is
\path{extensions/certified_forecasting/docs/REPRODUCIBILITY.md}.
Public data are identified by source and checksum; download scripts preserve
attribution and license information. Appendix~\ref{app:speech-protocol} describes the speech study's evaluation
order and its implications for interpretation.
