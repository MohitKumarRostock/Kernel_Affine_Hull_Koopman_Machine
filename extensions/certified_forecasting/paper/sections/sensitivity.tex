\subsection{Sensitivity to the coefficient budget}
\label{sec:budget-sensitivity}

The coefficient budget $\kappa$ defines the prediction class being evaluated.
Its numerical value is not directly comparable across different feature maps,
and an unfavorable result at one budget does not establish unavoidable
representation error at every larger budget. We therefore conducted a follow-up
after inspecting the main experiment, using new development, calibration, and
test random streams. This study was not specified before the main results
were seen.

The follow-up fixes history length $m=12$ and compares
$\kappa\in\{1,4,16\}$ on all four main systems, using three new seeds,
2701--2703. All other simulation, fitting, geometry, and sample-count settings
are unchanged: each repetition uses 128 training, 512 validation, 4,096
calibration, and 2,048 test episodes. Restricting the study to one history
length leaves 21 frozen candidates per budget. Within each system and seed,
all budgets use the same random streams, data, and geometric feature blocks;
only the coefficient constraint changes. The study contains 36 runs and
756 fitted models.

For each system, seed, and budget, we select the retained KAHM prefix by mean
validation MSE over the three horizons. No budget is selected using test error.
We allocate failure probability $0.05/3$ to each budget's complete family of
risk bounds and paired comparisons. A union bound gives joint confidence at
least 0.95 across the three budgets within each system--seed repetition.
This confidence statement does not extend jointly across all twelve
system--seed repetitions. Table~\ref{tab:budget} reports test RMSE for the
validation-selected KAHM predictor and counts repetitions in which its upper
bounds certify RMSE at most 0.35 at all three horizons.

Increasing the budget from 1 to 4 materially improves fitted KAHM prediction.
On the harmonic system, mean one-step RMSE decreases from 0.3737 to 0.1414;
on the nonlinear autoregression, it decreases from 0.1059 to 0.0670. On the
quasiperiodic system, the number of validation-selected models certified at
tolerance 0.35 increases from zero to three out of three. The budget therefore
affects both predictive accuracy and the certification decision.

Increasing the budget from 4 to 16 produces much smaller changes. Harmonic
horizon-12 RMSE changes from 0.3900 to 0.3875, and none of those KAHM models
passes the all-horizon 0.35 criterion. Quasiperiodic and nonlinear-AR results
are unchanged at the displayed precision. Logistic horizon-12 RMSE remains
approximately 0.5044, with no successful all-horizon certification. These
outcomes do not establish impossibility for richer geometries or larger
budgets. They show that increasing the budget alone to 16 does not resolve
the remaining limitations of this configuration.

The autoregressive and quadratic baselines fitted at the same numerical budgets
are retained in the released results. On the harmonic system at $\kappa=16$,
their mean horizon-12 RMSEs are 0.2377 and 0.2375, respectively, and both certify
all three repetitions at tolerance 0.35. On the nonlinear autoregression at
$\kappa=4$, KAHM has smaller horizon-12 RMSE (0.0746) than the autoregressive
(0.0800) and quadratic (0.0809) baselines, while its one-step error is larger.
The comparison depends on the task and horizon. Equal numerical budgets do
not imply equal effective capacity when the feature metrics differ.

Every run retains its candidate descriptions, calibration losses, pair scores,
and test losses. An independent reconstruction audit passed for all 36 runs,
recomputing 2,268 candidate--horizon certificates and 324 paired improvement
bounds. All recorded constrained optimizations converged with reported gaps
below $10^{-7}$. The use of common random streams across budgets was also
verified. These checks support the reported numerical calculations and confidence
allocation. The follow-up design remains conditional on the earlier findings.
