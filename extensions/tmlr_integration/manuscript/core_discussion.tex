\section{Discussion and conclusion}\label{sec:discussion}

The central results distinguish three properties of a reconstruction
representation. Algebraic distinctness means that its coordinates permit
functions unavailable to a specified comparison span. A bounded correction
means that a fitted residual head cannot exceed a stated magnitude under
the feature-norm assumption. Predictive usefulness means that the complete
procedure achieves lower loss under a specified evaluation distribution.
None of these properties implies the next one without additional
assumptions or evidence.

The principal experiment supports a contribution from reconstruction
departures beyond the shared locality and principal-coordinate features.
It establishes the specified margins over independently selected common
features and raw RBF, but not the complete practical-advantage criterion
across the comparison family. RBF reconstruction achieves much of the
same benefit. KAHM's point improvement over boosting is below the
specified margin, and the selected non-KAHM procedure has lower
aggregate loss. Failure to establish
a margin does not establish equality of two methods. Likewise, the
irregular-bank example demonstrates existence of distinct raw spans,
not distinctness for every bank fitted in the experiment.

Local dimension affects several parts of the predictor simultaneously.
It changes the common-coordinate basis, projected covariance and kernel,
and reconstruction departure. In these experiments, selecting the cap
improves the complete KAHM procedure relative to cap two, whereas the
native default cap twenty increases aggregate error. The matched-bank
decomposition shows that stronger common features account for the selected
procedure's gain despite a smaller incremental benefit from departures.
Full ambient local rank remains a valid native construction, but it is not
an empirical guarantee of better forecasting. Since the history dimension
is twelve, the observed cap-twenty procedure permits full ambient rank,
not evidence about a binding twenty-dimensional cap on longer histories.

The correction bound provides a precise magnitude guarantee for the
specified decoder. It does not establish robustness of the full
history-to-feature map: signed normalization and the affine fallback may
still be sensitive to the input. The baseline and targets are not
uniformly bounded, so the theorem gives no uniform forecast-error bound.
Its applicability to other bounded features also limits a claim of
methodological novelty based on the bound alone.

The empirical scope is finite. Twenty-four independent repetitions
quantify variability under four fixed simulation laws; they are not
24 independent application domains. The observation maps, finite burn-in,
history length, horizons, model grids, and resource settings are part of
the tested procedures. The simultaneous intervals use a paired-mean
$t$ approximation, and the 3\% improvement and 5\% noninferiority margins
express research criteria rather than application-specific requirements.
The supporting natural-data and adaptive studies impose further limits
on extrapolation of the simulated gains. The experiments are not a
comprehensive comparison with neural, pretrained, long-context
multivariate, or modern adaptive forecasting methods.

Computation must also be interpreted at the level measured. Fitting
costs include the candidates evaluated during selection. Prediction time,
serialized state size, owned array storage, and whole-process peak memory
measure different resources and cannot be substituted for one another.
The dimension-selected procedure pays for every evaluated cap; charging
only its winning bank would understate its selection cost.
The measurements characterize the supplied implementations on the
recorded environment; they do not establish minimum algorithmic costs
or satisfy an unspecified deployment budget.

KAHM reconstruction can therefore supply useful features to a finite
forecasting decoder, with a value that depends on geometry, feature
parameterization, regularization, and target structure. The contribution
of the present study is to characterize these dependencies and evaluate
them against matched alternatives. A general forecasting advantage
specific to KAHM remains unestablished by the reported evidence.

\section*{Reproducibility and research transparency}

The accompanying computational materials retain the protocol, source
freeze, fitted states, predictions, input and target arrays, selection
records, and analysis for all 192 task suites under
\path{extensions/dimension_forecasting}. The analysis program verifies the
source and stage inventories before reconstructing the 27 numerical
tables and six primary contrasts. Every cap, all ten procedures, and all
declared conditions are retained regardless of predictive performance.
The complete prediction inventory contains 31,104 arrays and 155,271,168
forecast values, including fixed-cap, selected, matched-bank, calibrated,
and unit-calibration views. The ten calibrated primary procedures alone
account for 1,920 arrays and 9,584,640 forecast values.

An independent audit regenerates episodes from the pinned generators and
independently checks seeds, noise, windows, and target construction,
the selection and calibration calculations, all 18,816 stored local
bases, and all 261,450 rows of the released analysis tables. Numerical
parity checks against the original full comparison suite use manufactured
inputs before the source freeze. These are computational consistency
checks, not an independent scientific replication. Reconstructing scores
from saved forecasts, replaying fitted states, and refitting candidates
remain distinct operations. The archived arrays define the reported
scores; reproducing floating-point states requires compatible numerical
libraries and arithmetic.

The standalone manuscript source includes its generated displays and
compiles without the numerical archives. Its asset builder reads the
sealed study tables and records their checksums. The companion
\emph{Supplementary Studies} gives the complete methods and results for
the supporting investigations in Section~\ref{sec:supporting-evidence}.
The complete principal fitting specification is in
Appendix~\ref{app:reconstruction-results}. The prospective specification
was local, informed by earlier studies, and not publicly preregistered.
