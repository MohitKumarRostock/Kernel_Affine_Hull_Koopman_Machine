\section{Reconstruction departures as predictive bases}
\label{sec:reconstruction-mechanism}

The earlier interventions change how local reconstructions control a
forecasting representation. We now test whether the reconstruction itself
supplies useful functions to a restricted decoder once a shared local
affine basis is retained. This section evaluates one specified construction against
a fixed comparison family within a bounded experimental budget.
Every feature is a deterministic function of the observed history: additional
basis functions do not add statistical information beyond that history.

\subsection{A distinct mechanism, with explicit cases of redundancy}

Let $z$ be an A-standardized history, and let a fixed local bank supply
reference rows $Q_j$, principal directions $P_j$, center $\mu_j$ and a positive
regularizer $\lambda_j$. The KAHM and matched positive-kernel reconstructions are
\begin{equation}
 A_{K,j}(z)=\frac{k_j(z)(K_j+\lambda_jI)^{-1}Q_j}
 {k_j(z)(K_j+\lambda_jI)^{-1}\mathbf1},\qquad
 A_{R,j}(z)=\frac{k_j(z)Q_j}{k_j(z)\mathbf1}.
 \label{eq:mechanism-main-maps}
\end{equation}
Both maps use the same reference histories and covariance-whitened projected
Gaussian kernel. The affine reconstruction is
$A_{A,j}(z)=\mu_j+(z-\mu_j)P_jP_j^\top$ in row-vector notation.
Shared gates $g_j(z)$ use a blockwise softmax of squared center-distance logits;
the gate does not depend on which reconstruction is being compared.

The common basis $\psi_0$ contains all $g_j$ and all gated principal
coordinates $g_j(z)(z-\mu_j)P_j$. The reconstruction departures are
\begin{equation}
 d_K(z)=\operatorname{concat}_j
       g_j(z)\{A_{K,j}(z)-A_{A,j}(z)\},\qquad
 d_R(z)=\operatorname{concat}_j
       g_j(z)\{A_{R,j}(z)-A_{A,j}(z)\}.
 \label{eq:mechanism-main-departures}
\end{equation}
The full history-coordinate departures enter the decoder, including their
components outside the local principal subspaces. Substituting the affine
reconstruction makes the departure identically zero and recovers the common
basis. Thus the common-only arm is an ablation of the added basis; the RBF arm
is the comparison with matched reference, kernel, gate and feature dimensions.

The reconstruction identity alone does not establish a new predictive function
class. Transferring normalized KAHM weights directly to reference targets is
exactly a ratio of two kernel-ridge predictions, one for the target and one for
the constant vector (Proposition~\ref{prop:mechanism-krr}). Symmetric two-point
reference banks can also make $d_K$ and $d_R$ linearly interchangeable after
the common coordinates are included
(Proposition~\ref{prop:mechanism-redundancy}). Conversely,
Proposition~\ref{prop:mechanism-distinct} proves a strict raw-span distinction
for an irregular three-point scalar bank. The inherited-compatible example
$Q=(-1,0,2)^\top$ supplies such a certificate. This proves existence of a
distinct reconstruction basis, not predictive superiority for every fitted
bank. Feature transformations and isotropic ridge penalties can change fitted
forecasts even when two raw spans coincide; the empirical estimand is the
complete specified procedure.

\subsection{A bounded residual decoder}

Each feature block is centered and scaled using A alone. It then passes through
the radial map
\begin{equation}
 T_R(v)=\frac{v}{\sqrt{1+\|v\|_2^2/R^2}},\qquad
 R=\sqrt{\text{block width}}.
 \label{eq:mechanism-main-radial}
\end{equation}
The map is nonexpansive and has norm at most $R$. The common and departure
blocks are transformed separately, with no subsequent standardization:
\[
 \psi_K=[T_{R_0}(\widetilde\psi_0),T_{R_d}(\widetilde d_K)],\qquad
 \psi_R=[T_{R_0}(\widetilde\psi_0),T_{R_d}(\widetilde d_R)].
\]
Consequently, appending a departure cannot rescale the common block. Three
bank partitions with 2, 4 and 8 local models, rank two and at most 24 references
per local model give 42 common and 168 departure coordinates. The bank is
unsupervised and fixed from A; this experiment does not further optimize its
references, directions or kernels against B targets.

For each horizon, a raw-history ridge baseline $f_{0,h}$ is retained and
\begin{equation}
 \widehat y_{t+h}=f_{0,h}(x_t)+\gamma_h q_h(x_t),\qquad
 q_h(x)=b_h+\psi(x)^\top w_h.
 \label{eq:mechanism-main-forecast}
\end{equation}
The correction fits A baseline residuals using mean squared error plus
$\alpha_h\|w_h\|^2$, with a free intercept. B selects
$\alpha_h\in\{10^{-4},10^{-3},10^{-2},10^{-1},1\}$ separately by horizon.
C then selects $\gamma_h\in\{0,1/4,1/2,1\}$, again separately by horizon.
There is no refit on B or C, and D targets never enter either selection API.

The free intercept matters to stability. If $\bar e_h$ and
$\sigma_{e,h}$ are the A residual mean and centered RMS, and $\bar\psi$ is
the A feature mean after bounding, Proposition~\ref{prop:mechanism-correction-bound}
gives
\begin{equation}
 \|w_h\|\le\frac{\sigma_{e,h}}{2\sqrt{\alpha_h}},\qquad
 |\gamma_h q_h(x)|\le |\gamma_h|\left[
 |\bar e_h|+(R+\|\bar\psi\|)
 \frac{\sigma_{e,h}}{2\sqrt{\alpha_h}}\right].
 \label{eq:mechanism-main-correction-bound}
\end{equation}
Here $R$ is the radius of the concatenated bounded features. This generic
ridge bound limits the correction, not the raw baseline or total forecast
error. It does not establish a global input Lipschitz constant through a
signed-denominator fallback boundary. For the unnormalized signed
reconstruction weights, define
\begin{equation}
 a_j(z)=k_j(z)(K_j+\lambda_jI)^{-1},\qquad
 \rho_j(z)=\frac{|a_j(z)\mathbf1|}{\|a_j(z)\|_1}
 \quad\text{when }\|a_j(z)\|_1>0.
 \label{eq:mechanism-relative-denominator}
\end{equation}
This dimensionless ratio measures signed cancellation and is invariant to
common positive rescaling of the query kernel. If $\rho_j\le10^{-10}$,
or required quantities are undefined or nonfinite, the local reconstruction
is replaced by its affine map, making that local departure zero. Every
affected row remains recorded.

\subsection{A fixed test of useful basis value}

The primary candidate is the bounded KAHM departure. Its five primary
comparators are the common basis, bounded RBF departure, raw-history-plus-RBF,
histogram gradient boosting, and a C-selected non-KAHM policy. Raw ridge,
persistence, and KAHM/RBF ablations that omit only the departure bound complete
the ten-method roster. All non-ridge arms use C-selected mixing with the common
baseline. The policy selects one complete calibrated non-KAHM procedure by C
normalized error; neither KAHM arm is eligible. It is not a D oracle.
Raw RBF and boosting receive the same A/B/C roles and horizon-specific model
selection. Their exact grids and the accounting of their costs are recorded
in Appendix~\ref{app:reconstruction-results}.

The roster contains four laws, two added-noise levels and 24 fresh repetition
seeds: 192 task suites. The nonlinear AR, Van der Pol and Duffing laws are the
inherited numerical observation laws already investigated earlier. A stable
linear Gaussian AR(2) base law is the negative control. Added Gaussian
observation noise has standard deviation $\eta$ times the standard deviation
of all unaugmented A episode values, with $\eta\in\{0,0.1\}$; it affects
both histories and observed future targets. The underlying episodes, unit-noise
arrays, retained rows and fitting seed are paired across noise levels. The
oscillator observations already contain inherited sensor noise at $\eta=0$.
Fresh realizations of these known laws are not new application evidence.

A/B/C/D contain 64/32/32/128 length-72 episodes, generated from independent
base-episode and unit-noise streams. The augmented splits share the
A-estimated noise amplitude.
History length is 12, horizons are $(1,4,12)$, and origin stride is four.
The first three roles retain 512/256/256 rows; D retains all 1,664 origins.
The protocol, analysis, executable sources and prepared inputs were frozen
locally before reserved forecasts, at commit \texttt{\RMFreezeShort{}}.
This was not public preregistration or independent replication. Historical
studies retain their own estimands and are not pooled with this experiment.

For task $t$, method $m$ and horizon $h$, the loss scale is
$v_{th}=\max\{\Var_A(Y_h),10^{-12}\}$, using the retained A target rows
and population variance convention. The task loss is
\[
 L_{tm}=\frac13\sum_{h\in\{1,4,12\}}
 \frac{\MSE_{D,t,m,h}}{v_{th}}.
\]
Each repetition averages its six nonlinear law/noise conditions equally.
For each comparator $m$, the primary useful-effect contrast is
$d_{rm}=0.97L_{rm}-L_{rK}$. The linear-control aggregate instead averages
its two noise levels and uses
$d_{r,\mathrm{lin}}=1.05L_{r,\mathrm{ridge}}-L_{rK}$.
Every one-sided lower bound
\[
 \bar d-t_{1-0.05/6,23}\,s_d/\sqrt{24}
\]
must be strictly positive. These are six Bonferroni-adjusted paired-mean
$t$ comparisons, with independent repetitions as the sampling units.
Their finite-sample interpretation relies on the paired-mean approximation;
overlapping windows and paired noise levels do not increase the sample size.
The 3\% improvement margin and 5\% linear tolerance are chosen research
allocation thresholds, not application-derived requirements or a definition
of universal effectiveness. Twenty-four repetitions is a fixed budget, not
a demonstrated power calculation.

The complete gate also requires finite, technically complete outputs, the
feature/coefficient/correction bounds, and a taskwise maximum absolute KAHM
error no more than five times the ridge maximum. The last requirement is a
coarse engineering screen. No activation-rate or runtime-ratio threshold is
used. A wholly failed method or any final prediction-cell recovery blocks an
efficacy conclusion. Once technically complete, failure to satisfy every
condition stops this forecasting-performance branch; a pass supports separate
reserved application testing. Neither a favorable subset nor extra repetitions
may be selected after D to change this decision.

The positive common-basis comparison and the failed overall decision are
compatible. A bounded reconstruction departure supplies useful functions to
this decoder, while the matched positive-RBF reconstruction supplies much of
the same predictive value. KAHM's point improvement over that matched arm is
below the prespecified 3\% threshold. Its point improvement over raw RBF
exceeds 3\%, but the simultaneous lower bound remains below zero, so that
useful-effect claim is unresolved. Boosting and the selected policy have
smaller aggregate point losses. The evidence therefore supports useful
reconstruction features without establishing the required KAHM-specific
practical advantage.

The maximum taskwise KAHM-to-ridge maximum-error ratio is
\RMMaxErrorRatio{}, against the declared limit of five. The saved checks
record \RMFeatureViolations{} feature-bound, \RMCoefficientViolations{}
coefficient-bound and \RMCorrectionViolations{} correction-bound violations.
C activates the bounded KAHM correction on \RMKActiveNonlinear{} of
432 nonlinear task/horizon cells and \RMKActiveLinear{} of 144 linear-control
cells. Activation means a nonzero selected mixing coefficient; it is not
evidence that the correction improves D loss. Mean charged fit-and-selection
time is \RMKFitSeconds{} seconds for bounded KAHM departure and
\RMRFitSeconds{} seconds for its matched bounded RBF departure; detailed
costs and accounting boundaries appear in Appendix~\ref{app:reconstruction-results}.

The functional certificate, deterministic correction bound and empirical
decision answer different questions. Distinct functions may still be
unhelpful at the observed sample size and decoder penalty. A bounded correction
may still be inaccurate. Conversely, an unresolved useful-effect contrast does
not show that the two representations are identical. The complete evidence
therefore supports conclusions about this specified reconstruction-to-prediction
mechanism, with the declared law, noise, data and decoder restrictions.
