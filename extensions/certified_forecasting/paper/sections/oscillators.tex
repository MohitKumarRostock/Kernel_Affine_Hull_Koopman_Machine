\subsection{Independently initialized Van der Pol and Duffing episodes}
\label{sec:oscillators}

We apply the same frozen procedure to independently initialized Van der Pol
and forced Duffing episodes, connecting the experiment to
\citet{kumar2026limits}.
Let $x(t)$ denote physical position, $v(t)$ velocity, and $t$ continuous time;
a dot denotes differentiation with respect to $t$. The dynamics are
\begin{align*}
 \dot x&=v,&
 \dot v&=(1-x^2)v-x
 &&\text{(Van der Pol)},\\
 \dot x&=v,&
 \dot v&=-0.2v+x-x^3+0.3\cos(1.2t+\varphi)
 &&\text{(Duffing)}.
\end{align*}
Initial position and velocity are independent and uniform on $[-2,2]$.
For Duffing, the forcing phase $\varphi$ is independently uniform on
$[0,2\pi]$ in each episode. We integrate with classical fourth-order
Runge--Kutta at step size $0.025$, discard ten time units, and sample 72
observations at interval $0.1$. The observed scalar signal is
\[
 y_j=0.95\tanh(x(10+0.1j))+\eta_j,\qquad
 \eta_j\stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}\operatorname{Unif}[-0.02,0.02],
 \qquad j=0,\ldots,71.
\]
Here $j$ is the discrete observation index, $\tanh$ is the hyperbolic tangent,
and $\operatorname{Unif}[a,b]$ denotes the uniform distribution on $[a,b]$.
The noises $\eta_j$ are independent and identically distributed (iid).
Certificates use the conservative range $[-1,1]$ for the sensor, whose range
lies inside $[-0.97,0.97]$. They concern sensor-output error, not physical
position or velocity, and require neither stationarity nor independent
within-episode windows.

Each system has three new complete fitting repetitions, using 128 training,
512 validation, 4096 calibration, and 2048 test episodes. Histories, the
40-candidate family, coefficient budget, and per-repetition joint confidence
allocation are unchanged from the main experiment. Horizons $1,4,12$ now
correspond to $0.1,0.4,1.2$ time units. Selection within each family minimizes
validation MSE averaged over horizons before calibration or testing. The
unchanged forecasting procedure receives the new generator; all candidates,
episode scores, and implementation hashes are retained.

The prespecified half-step check compares 16 fixed initializations with
identical phases and observation noise at step sizes $0.025$ and $0.0125$.
Maximum sensor differences, $1.69\times10^{-6}$ for Van der Pol and
$3.49\times10^{-6}$ for Duffing, are below the fixed $10^{-3}$ threshold.
This assesses numerical consistency, without rigorously bounding global
continuous-time integration error.

Table~\ref{tab:oscillators} shows substantial long-horizon improvement over
persistence for retained KAHM models. Mean horizon-12 RMSE is $0.3814$ versus
$0.8677$ on Van der Pol, and $0.2499$ versus $0.3185$ on Duffing. Other models
perform better on these tasks: local affine-hull blocks achieve $0.2990$ on
Van der Pol, and direct quadratic regression achieves $0.1369$ on Duffing.
Iterated KAHM has lower mean horizon-12 error than direct KAHM on Van der Pol
($0.3408$ versus $0.3814$), whereas direct KAHM performs better on Duffing
($0.2499$ versus $0.2686$). The earlier advantage of direct fitting on the
periodic generators therefore does not extend to every nonlinear oscillator.

The retained KAHM family certifies the all-horizon RMSE requirement $0.35$ in
all three Duffing repetitions. On Van der Pol, it certifies $0.50$ in all three
repetitions but does not certify $0.35$. When all comparison families are
eligible, the $0.35$ requirement is certified in every repetition of both
systems. No candidate certifies the all-horizon $0.20$ requirement in this
follow-up. Adjacent KAHM refinements have strictly positive certified
improvement in 43 of 54 Van der Pol comparisons and ten of 54 Duffing
comparisons, with the same per-repetition confidence allocation.

All six scheduled runs completed, retaining 240 fitted candidates. An
independent score audit reconstructs their 1440 one-sided risk endpoints and
108 paired improvement bounds with zero numerical discrepancy. The probability
guarantees apply to the specified bounded sensor and independent-episode
numerical law. They do not transfer directly to errors in physical state
measured under another observation protocol.
