\begin{abstract}
Kernel Affine Hull Machines (KAHMs) represent data through local geometric
reconstruction models. We turn their soft associations into forecasts of the
observed signal and assess whether those forecasts meet a specified error
tolerance. The learner retains dictionary blocks and fits constrained
multihorizon decoders. Bounding the sum of block coefficient norms preserves
earlier predictors when a block is added and prevents exact duplication from
enlarging the feasible class. We derive a population risk lower bound from
pairs whose target separation exceeds the separation permitted by the decoder's
Lipschitz budget. This pairwise obstruction is exact for a distribution supported
on two points and is at least as large as a conditional root-variance bound
under the same pairing law. Calibration on independent episodes combines the
obstruction with fitted-risk upper bounds and paired improvement comparisons,
and reports an unresolved decision when accuracy cannot be certified. The
theory also applies to other fixed representations. Experiments include a
known-risk benchmark, six synthetic dynamics, and pen trajectories separated
by writer. Matched RBF and affine-hull controls show KAHM gains on nonlinear
autoregression and pen trajectories, while affine-hull alternatives perform
better on the oscillators. Preprocessing comparisons retain both gains and
regressions across repeated fits. Follow-up comparisons on the same writer
cohort are descriptive. Few independent evaluation units can leave
the bounds uninformative. The guarantees concern expected squared error
under a specified episode and forecast-origin law.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Code, data, and research transparency}
The \href{https://github.com/MohitKumarRostock/Kernel_Affine_Hull_Koopman_Machine/tree/research/certified-soft-state-forecasting/extensions/certified_forecasting}{accompanying repository} provides the implementation, frozen configurations,
result summaries, and figure generators in
\path{extensions/certified_forecasting}; editable manuscript files are in its
\path{paper} directory. The original experiments, input-scaling follow-up, and
matched geometry--decoder comparison are archived separately. Their records
include configurations, source hashes, and documented exact replays of damaged
score archives in the scaling study. The evidence packages identify retained
score arrays, and fixed-seed runners regenerate the full episode-level
archives. Appendix~\ref{app:protocols} specifies the experimental
configurations and independent sampling units.
