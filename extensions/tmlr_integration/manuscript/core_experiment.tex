\section{Dimension selection and forecasting comparisons}\label{sec:experiment}

\subsection{Comparison procedures}


The primary candidate is the bounded KAHM departure. Its five primary
comparators are the common features, bounded RBF departure, raw-history
RBF forecasting, histogram gradient boosting, and a validation-selected
non-KAHM procedure. Raw ridge, persistence, and KAHM and RBF variants that
omit only the departure bound complete the ten-method comparison.
The principal experiment uses the bounded features and candidate geometries
defined in Sections~\ref{sec:reconstruction-method}
and~\ref{sec:bounded-decoder}. All non-ridge candidates are calibrated on C by blending their forecasts
with the same ridge baseline. The selection policy chooses one complete,
calibrated non-KAHM procedure using horizon-averaged normalized C error;
neither KAHM variant is eligible. Raw RBF and boosting have the same
A/B/C roles and horizon-specific hyperparameter selection.
Appendix~\ref{app:reconstruction-results} specifies their grids and costs.

The primary comparison uses each geometric procedure's independently
B-selected cap from Equation~\eqref{eq:dimension-cap-selection}.
Three secondary designs isolate the role of that choice. First, a fixed
cap of two uses the explicit restriction
$\min\{2,d-1,n_j-1\}$. Second, the native default cap twenty uses
$\min\{20,d,n_j-1\}$. Third, all reconstruction controls use the
bounded KAHM procedure's selected bank, while retaining their own
penalty and calibration choices. Only the third design, and comparisons
within a fixed-cap bank, match references, projected kernels, gates,
feature dimensions, transformation rules, and decoder grids. Primary
independently selected procedures need not share a bank.

All six native caps and the fixed-cap-two bank are fitted separately.
At history dimension twelve, native caps twelve and twenty produce
identical banks and forecasts; ascending tie resolution therefore never
selects twenty. The fixed-cap-two and native-cap-two results are also
identical in this experiment. These identities are verified before final
evaluation and retained as internal controls. The stored common-feature
width ranges from 28 to 182 across candidates; either departure adds 168
coordinates. Thus dimension selection changes the common feature block
as well as the projected kernel and reconstruction departure.
Raw RBF forecasting instead uses 12 history coordinates and at most
64 Nystr\"om coordinates. Its comparison, and those with boosting and
the selection policy, assess complete procedures with different
representations and search spaces. They are not feature- or
computation-matched ablations.

\subsection{Observation laws and independent evaluation roles}

The experiment comprises four laws, two additional-noise levels, and
24 independent repetitions, giving 192 task suites. The nonlinear laws
are an autoregression, Van der Pol dynamics, and Duffing dynamics. A
stable linear Gaussian AR(2) process provides a negative control.
Additional Gaussian observation noise has standard deviation $\eta s_A$,
where $s_A$ is the standard deviation of all original A episode values
and $\eta\in\{0,0.1\}$. The added noise affects histories and targets.
Underlying episodes, standardized noise draws, retained rows, and fitting
seeds are paired between the two noise levels. The oscillator observations
already contain sensor noise when $\eta=0$.

Sets A, B, C, and D contain 64, 32, 32, and 128 episodes, respectively,
each of length 72. Their base episodes and standardized noise draws use
independent random streams; the additional-noise amplitude is estimated
on A and shared across sets. Histories have length 12, horizons are
$\mathcal H=\{1,4,12\}$, and consecutive forecast origins are four
steps apart. A, B, and C retain at most 512, 256, and 256 histories,
respectively; D retains all 1,664 origins. The protocol, executable source,
and analysis were frozen before generating these data. B choices are
saved before C calibration; all fitted states are sealed before generating
D. D inputs and targets are stored separately, and all forecasts are
sealed before target release. Fresh random streams are disjoint from
the earlier studies' streams. This is a prospective local specification
informed by those studies, rather than public preregistration or external
replication. Their losses are not pooled with this experiment.

\subsection{Loss aggregation and simultaneous inference}

For task $\tau$, method $m$, and horizon $h$, let
$\MSE_{D,\tau,m,h}$ be the mean squared error over D origins. Define
$v_{\tau h}=\max\{\Var_{A,\tau}(Y_h),10^{-12}\}$, where $Y_h$
denotes the horizon-$h$ target and the
variance uses the retained A target rows with divisor $n_A$. The task loss is
\[
 L_{\tau m}=\frac1{|\mathcal H|}\sum_{h\in\mathcal H}
       \frac{\MSE_{D,\tau,m,h}}{v_{\tau h}}.
\]
For repetition $r$, let $L^{\mathrm{NL}}_{rm}$ average the six nonlinear
law--noise task losses equally, and let $L^{\mathrm{lin}}_{rm}$ average
the two linear-control task losses. For each of the five nonlinear
comparators, define the useful-improvement contrast
\[
 d_{rm}=0.97L^{\mathrm{NL}}_{rm}-L^{\mathrm{NL}}_{rK}.
\]
A positive population mean of this contrast corresponds to an improvement
in expected normalized loss exceeding the specified 3\% margin.
The linear-control contrast is
\[
 d_{r,\mathrm{lin}}=1.05L^{\mathrm{lin}}_{r,\mathrm{ridge}}
                          -L^{\mathrm{lin}}_{rK},
\]
which tests noninferiority with a 5\% relative tolerance. For each
contrast, $\bar d=24^{-1}\sum_r d_r$ and
$s_d^2=23^{-1}\sum_r(d_r-\bar d)^2$ denote the sample mean and variance.
The one-sided lower confidence bound is
\[
 \bar d-t_{23,1-0.05/6}\,\frac{s_d}{\sqrt{24}},
\]
where $t_{\nu,q}$ is quantile $q$ of the Student $t$ distribution with
$\nu$ degrees of freedom. Bonferroni adjustment covers the six contrasts
simultaneously at nominal level 95\%, subject to the paired-mean
$t$ approximation. The sampling units are independent repetition
aggregates; overlapping windows and paired noise levels do not increase
the inferential sample size.

The prespecified criterion requires all six lower bounds to exceed zero.
It also requires complete finite predictions, satisfaction of the
feature, coefficient, and correction bounds, and a taskwise maximum
absolute KAHM error no greater than five times the corresponding ridge
maximum. The last requirement is an engineering diagnostic rather than
a population risk guarantee. A wholly failed method or a final
prediction-cell recovery prevents an efficacy conclusion. The 3\%
improvement margin, 5\% tolerance, and fixed 24-repetition budget express
the study's research criteria; they do not derive from a deployment loss
function or a power calculation. Failure to satisfy the complete criterion
concludes the planned performance evaluation without adding repetitions
or selecting favorable conditions after inspecting D.

\subsection{Dimension changes the complete feature representation}

Table~\ref{tab:dimension-sensitivity} compares the specified cap rules on
the same episodes. B-selected KAHM improves on fixed cap two by
\DFDimensionGain\%, with lower repetition-average loss in 21 of 24
repetitions. Its mean paired difference is $-0.000973$ with a secondary,
unadjusted 95\% paired-$t$ interval $[-0.001229,-0.000717]$.
Native cap twenty instead increases loss by 8.39\%, with higher loss in
all 24 repetition aggregates; its corresponding difference is 0.002507
with interval $[0.002024,0.002991]$. These intervals are descriptive
secondary analyses outside the six-comparison primary family.

The bounded KAHM cap counts across the 144 nonlinear tasks are
15, 22, 68, 31, and 8 for caps 1, 2, 4, 8, and 12, respectively.
Cap four is the most frequent selection, but the variation does not support
a universal fixed-cap recommendation. No experiment here measures a
binding cap of twenty in an ambient dimension greater than twenty.

To interpret the selected procedure, let $L_K^{(c)}$ and $L_0^{(c)}$
denote KAHM and common-feature loss using the same bank rule $c$, each
with its own fitted head and C calibration. Relative to the fixed cap-two
bank, the exact loss decomposition is
\[
 L_K^{(c)}-L_K^{(2)}
 =\{L_0^{(c)}-L_0^{(2)}\}
  +\{(L_K^{(c)}-L_0^{(c)})-(L_K^{(2)}-L_0^{(2)})\}.
\]
For the KAHM-selected bank, the complete change $-0.000973$ comprises
a common-feature improvement of $-0.002150$ and a weakening of the
incremental departure benefit by $+0.001177$. The departure still reduces
loss relative to this matched common control: its increment changes from
$-0.004967$ to $-0.003790$. Native cap twenty similarly strengthens the
common predictor but loses more of the departure's incremental benefit,
producing a net deterioration. This decomposition concerns the complete
fitted predictors; it is not an orthogonal variance decomposition or a
causal attribution to individual coordinates.

Using the KAHM-selected bank, bounded KAHM improves over matched bounded
RBF by 0.68\%, compared with 0.72\% for independently selected RBF.
The matched difference is $-0.000198$, with secondary unadjusted interval
$[-0.000325,-0.000072]$. Its sign does not establish the primary 3\%
margin. Bounded KAHM also has 2.28\% lower loss than its unbounded
counterpart on the same bank, versus 1.71\% when those procedures select
their caps independently. Setting only the calibration weights to one
retains a 3.39\% selected-versus-fixed-cap-two improvement; the direction
of the dimension result therefore does not depend on C shrinkage alone.

The heterogeneity is substantial. Selection improves fixed-cap-two KAHM
in five of the six nonlinear law--noise cells, but slightly worsens Duffing
with added noise. At Van der Pol with no additional Gaussian noise,
KAHM has loss 0.009347 versus 0.003801 for boosting; the non-KAHM policy
selects boosting in all 24 repetitions of this condition. Native cap
twenty improves the first two horizon averages relative to cap two but
worsens horizon twelve. Appendix~\ref{app:reconstruction-results}
reports every method, condition, and horizon aggregate.

\subsection{Numerical checks and measured resources}

The maximum taskwise KAHM-to-ridge maximum-error ratio is
\DFMaxErrorRatio{}, below the limit of five. There are no feature-bound,
coefficient-bound, or bounded-correction violations. All 192 tasks complete
without a failed method or recovered final forecast. Across 18,816 local
models, no model requires an additional positive-eigenvalue or projected-range
rank reduction, a rank-zero fallback, or a numerical query guard. Native
cap twenty stores rank twelve in 2,685 of its 2,688 local models; three
two-reference models have rank one. The stacked local bases have ambient
rank twelve for every bank, including the small-cap banks. C selects a
nonzero KAHM correction weight in
\DFKActiveNonlinear{} of 432 nonlinear task--horizon combinations and
\DFKActiveLinear{} of 144 linear-control combinations. This activation
frequency describes calibration choices, not a guarantee of lower D loss.
Mean charged fitting and selection time is \DFKFitSeconds{} seconds
for selected bounded KAHM departure and \DFRFitSeconds{} seconds for
selected bounded RBF departure. These costs include all six native cap
candidates. The fixed-cap-two and native-cap-twenty KAHM costs are 0.085
and 0.101 seconds. Appendix~\ref{app:reconstruction-results} reports
retained state sizes, full-suite timing scope, and the accounting boundaries.

The algebraic example, deterministic bound, and empirical comparisons
answer different questions. A distinct feature can be poorly aligned
with the target or difficult to estimate from the available sample.
A bounded correction can remain inaccurate. An unresolved improvement
margin does not establish equality of the representations. The
conclusions therefore apply to the specified reconstructions, decoder,
selection rules, and observation laws.
