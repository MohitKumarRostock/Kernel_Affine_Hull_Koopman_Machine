\section{Experimental evaluation}
\label{sec:experiments}

We assess calibration against analytically known risks, forecasting accuracy
across representations, certified improvements from additional blocks, and
accuracy decisions based on the risk bounds.
Follow-ups examine input scaling (Section~\ref{sec:input-scaling}) and geometric
families with matched preprocessing, decoders, and dictionary-size options
(Section~\ref{sec:matched-geometry}). The release retains every completed fit,
candidate, configuration, random stream, and certificate score, including
unfavorable and inconclusive outcomes. Throughout this section, MSE
denotes mean squared prediction error and RMSE its square root. A test RMSE
estimates population RMSE; a certified upper RMSE endpoint is the square root
of an upper risk bound.

\subsection{Exact-risk calibration and small retained variation}

The controlled experiment has two possible input values, called atoms. The first has probability
$1-p$, target $-1$, and representation
$u_0=((1+a)/2,(1-a)/2)$; the second has probability $p$, target $1$, and
representation $u_1=((1-a)/2,(1+a)/2)$. Here $a\in[0,1]$ controls how much
the representation separates the atoms: $\|u_1-u_0\|_2=\sqrt2a$.
Proposition~\ref{prop:two-atom} gives the exact minimum population MSE
$p(1-p)(2-\kappa\sqrt2a)_+^2$. We also fit the constrained two-atom predictor
using 256 independent training observations and compute its population risk
analytically. We compare three confidence bounds: the pairwise lower bound,
a centered variance-based lower bound for the same raw target, and the upper
bound for the fitted model. Each receives a failure-probability allocation of
$0.05/3$. Appendix~\ref{app:known-risk} gives the centered correction and the
analytic fitted-risk calculation.

The parameter grid is
\[
p\in\{.2,.5\},\qquad a\in\{0,.2,.6,1\},\qquad
\kappa\in\{.5,1,2\}.
\]
Calibration uses $n\in\{64,256,1024,4096\}$ independent observations and
300 independent repetitions per parameter combination, totaling 28,800 trials.
One pairwise lower bound exceeds its exact minimum risk; the centered lower
and fitted upper bounds have no violations. Thus 28,799 trials satisfy all
three inequalities. This finite-grid Monte Carlo check neither proves general
coverage nor provides simultaneous confidence across trials. The single
violation is retained.

Figure~\ref{fig:known-risk} shows the effect of directly estimating the pairwise
discrepancy. With $p=.5$ and $\kappa=1$, the collapsed map $a=0$ has exact
minimum MSE one. At $n=4096$, the mean pairwise lower endpoint is $0.9177$,
compared with $0.5327$ for the centered endpoint; the mean fitted upper endpoint
is $1.0200$. The centered input-variance estimate is exactly zero here, but its
additive confidence correction is subsequently square-rooted, producing an
$n^{-1/4}$-order input penalty. The pair statistic avoids that operation.
For $a=.6$ and the same $n$, the exact minimum is $0.3315$, the mean pairwise
and centered lower endpoints are $0.2980$ and $0.2230$, and the mean fitted
upper endpoint is $0.3451$. These endpoints are in MSE units. At small sample
sizes, both lower bounds frequently equal zero. These controlled comparisons
do not establish a general minimax rate or uniform finite-sample dominance.

\subsection{Bounded time-series forecasting}

The main forecasting experiment uses four generators of independent,
finite-length episodes: a noisy harmonic oscillator with random frequency and
phase, a quasiperiodic signal with two components, a nonlinear autoregression
with bounded innovations, and a logistic map with an episode-specific
parameter. Their definitions guarantee observations in $[-1,1]$.
Appendix~\ref{app:protocols} specifies their equations, initialization, noise
ranges, and burn-in conventions. Independence holds between generated episodes;
overlapping histories within an episode need not be independent.

For each generator, five complete fitting repetitions use 128 training,
512 validation, 4096 calibration, and 2048 independent test episodes of length
72. Histories have length $m\in\{4,12\}$, and the forecast horizons are
$\mathcal H=\{1,4,12\}$ time steps. The common zero-based forecast origins are
$11,15,\ldots,59$, giving 13 origins per episode. All geometric maps are
relearned in each repetition. The decoder budget is $\kappa=4$, the ridge
coefficient is $\lambda=10^{-4}$, and the KAHM affinity exponent is
$\omega=16$. The four successive dictionary blocks have $2,4,8,16$ coordinates;
retaining their prefixes gives total feature dimensions $2,6,14,30$. Up to
1600 training histories enter geometry construction and up to 24 local training
points enter each KAHM model. The decoder uses all available training rows.

Each repetition freezes 40 candidates before calibration. They include
retained KAHM, radial-basis-function (RBF), and local affine-hull features;
hard KAHM state assignments; ordinary Frobenius-budget and exact-duplicate
ablations; ridge autoregression (AR); direct quadratic regression; affine
feature-transition rollouts on KAHM and polynomial dictionaries; persistence;
and a constant model. Equal numerical norm budgets do not give different feature maps identical
statistical capacities. These fixed configurations assess the procedure;
they do not establish the best accuracy attainable by extensive tuning.

The joint failure-probability budget is $0.05$ per repetition. We allocate
$0.04$ to the $2\times40\times3$ one-sided risk bounds: one lower and one upper
bound for every candidate and horizon. This gives $\alpha=1/6000$ per bound.
The remaining $0.01$ is allocated over 18 adjacent-prefix improvement
comparisons, giving $1/1800$ each. The resulting joint confidence is 95\% per
complete fitting repetition, rather than across all 20 experimental
repetitions. The upper bound uses 4096 episode losses, and the lower bound uses
2048 disjoint episode pairs. The 13 within-episode origins do not multiply
these independent sample sizes.

Table~\ref{tab:forecast} reports independent-test errors for one candidate per
family, selected by validation MSE averaged over all three horizons. KAHM soft
states improve substantially over persistence at long horizons in the harmonic
and quasiperiodic tasks, but quadratic and autoregressive models are stronger
on these generators. In the nonlinear autoregression, KAHM has mean test RMSE
$0.0743$ at horizon 12, compared with $0.0802$ for ridge AR and $0.0813$ for
quadratic regression; the simple autoregressive models are better at horizon
one. The advantage therefore depends on the task and horizon. Direct KAHM
fitting also lowers mean horizon-12 error relative to its iterated
feature-transition counterpart on the two periodic tasks. This empirical
difference does not establish that direct fitting is always preferable to
rollout.

\subsection{Useful certificates, class exclusions, and refinement decisions}

\input{tables/certified_selection}

Table~\ref{tab:selection} reports the tolerance decisions. Within the retained
KAHM family, every nonlinear-AR repetition certifies RMSE at most $0.20$ at all
three horizons. Every quasiperiodic repetition certifies $0.35$, and every
harmonic repetition certifies $0.50$ but none certifies $0.35$. When all
comparison models are eligible, the harmonic task also certifies $0.35$ in
every repetition. No candidate certifies the $0.50$ all-horizon requirement
for the logistic task. This decision is unresolved: it does not establish
that every candidate's true RMSE exceeds $0.50$.

Even with thousands of episodes, the selected harmonic quadratic model has
mean horizon-one test RMSE $0.0311$ but mean upper RMSE endpoint $0.1497$.
These bounds resolve some tolerances and leave others undecided; coverage
alone does not establish practical usefulness.

Of 480 KAHM candidate--horizon statements, 119 have positive lower endpoints,
excluding zero risk for their sensitivity-bounded classes on the reported
confidence event. Zero endpoints leave adequacy unresolved.
Figure~\ref{fig:refinement} compares prefixes using the maximum distance
across retained blocks and budget $\kappa=4$. Added blocks can change these
distances; neither that change nor lower training error guarantees lower
population risk.

Paired comparisons certify 72 of 360 adjacent KAHM refinements under the
per-repetition joint event (Table~\ref{tab:improvements}); the remainder are
inconclusive. Nonlinear AR has no certified adjacent-prefix gain despite
meeting useful accuracy tolerances. Meeting a tolerance and improving on a
previous predictor require different certificates.

Figure~\ref{fig:duplication} isolates replication without training noise.
Numerical zero-ridge tests independently check that fitting a single block and
exact copies gives matching minimum training risks within the recorded solver
gaps. The exact-duplicate candidate in the main study uses zero ridge, whereas
the primary models use $10^{-4}$. Differences between those fitted candidates
are therefore not used as proof of replication invariance.

\subsection{Numerical and reproducibility verification}

All 20 scheduled main runs completed, retaining all 800 fitted candidates.
The largest recorded first-order optimality gap for the block-constrained
solver is below $10^{-7}$. An independent audit reconstructs all 4800 one-sided
risk endpoints and all 360 improvement bounds from the saved double-precision
episode scores, with maximum discrepancy zero. A fresh replay of the first
harmonic run matches every non-timing result and all 120 retained score arrays
exactly, including 983,040 scalar entries. These numerical checks neither replace probability arguments nor formally
verify floating-point arithmetic. Source and configuration hashes, failed-run
accounting, and verification output are archived.
