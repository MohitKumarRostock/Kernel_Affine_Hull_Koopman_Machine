\begin{abstract}
We formulate Kernel Affine Hull Machines (KAHMs) as time-series forecasters
with local reconstruction gates, principal coordinates, a retained baseline
and a finite residual decoder. The central question is whether the KAHM
reconstruction adds predictive value beyond equally supervised local
alternatives at its measured cost. Controlled interventions identify when
reconstruction scores omit predictive position; historical natural-source,
scaling and residual-learning studies delimit transfer and preserve fitting
failures. A shared supervised metric based on a residual-regression proxy
does not establish the value of its additional complexity.
We therefore train local KAHM kernel bandwidths and gate temperatures
against the actual forecast error for each horizon, with affine, locality
and RBF-local controls receiving matched supervision and optimization
ceilings. A public code, analysis and input freeze precedes 36 fresh
simulation repetitions and ten planned natural-series tasks. All 43 feasible
tasks yield 731 audited prediction records; three A-selected rideshare
tasks lack geometry-training windows and remain unavailable.
Across 18 simultaneous synthetic comparisons, two favor reconstruction-trained
KAHM, six favor the comparator and ten are unresolved. KAHM reduces mean
error by 2.87\% relative to raw RBF on nonlinear AR and by 41.43\% relative
to gate-only KAHM on Van der Pol, but matched affine controls and a
validation-selected non-KAHM policy remain stronger in key comparisons.
The planned four-collection natural aggregate is undefined. On the
explicitly available seven-series, three-collection subset, KAHM improves
ridge by 3.55\% while raw RBF, boosted trees and trained RBF-local have
lower aggregate error. KAHM's mean charged pipeline time there is 7.93
seconds, versus 0.013 for raw RBF and 0.655 for boosted trees.
These results establish a fully specified, reproducible KAHM forecasting
construction and a useful direct-training intervention in one tested
regime, while leaving distinctive superiority over matched alternatives
and efficient incremental geometry unestablished.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
A time-series forecaster must retain the aspects of its observed history
that predict a future value at a specified horizon. Kernel Affine Hull
Machines (KAHMs) provide nonlinear reconstruction maps from local reference
observations \citep{Kumar2024KAHM}. This paper develops their role as
forecasting representations: a history is encoded by local reconstruction
affinities and principal coordinates, a finite decoder predicts a target,
and a temporal protocol determines whether the added geometry is useful.
The object evaluated is the complete forecasting procedure, including
supervision, selection, numerical failures, and computational cost.

Reconstruction and prediction are different requirements. A local
reconstructor can recover every point on an affine line while its zero
residual discards position along that line. A decoder receiving only the
residual cannot use that position. Conversely, retaining raw history does
not ensure that a regularized decoder benefits from appended features.
The appropriate predictive question is whether a specified KAHM map and
decoder improve held-out forecasts beyond equally supervised alternatives.
Neither accurate reconstruction nor a favorable comparison with one weak
baseline establishes a KAHM-specific contribution.

We formulate a horizon-specific KAHM forecaster with a retained baseline
and a separately regularized geometric correction. In the newest
intervention, local kernel bandwidths and gate temperatures are trained
against the actual final forecast error. Each candidate head is fitted on
support data A, geometry is trained on separate queries B, and head penalty
and correction weight are selected on C. The selected procedure is frozen
for D. Affine, locality and RBF-local controls receive the same data roles,
parameter counts within each intervention, and optimization ceilings.
Raw ridge, a raw-input RBF model, gradient boosting and a C-selected
non-KAHM policy make the practical comparison explicit. This formulation
trains part of the reconstruction operator; it does not train reference
membership, principal directions or all KAHM parameters end to end.

The empirical record is cumulative but not pooled
(Table~\ref{tab:evidence-status}). Controlled interventions identify when
local position matters and finite-decoder studies demonstrate conditional
gains. Natural-source evaluations limit their transfer. A scaling
sensitivity reuses inspected data. An additive correction study exposes
both weak incremental benefit and fitting failures, which remain visible.
A subsequent shared metric uses a Gaussian residual-regression proxy,
not the final KAHM forecast objective. Its 48 tasks and 768 forecasts
support KAHM gains over ridge but leave incremental metric and donor-target
benefits unresolved; matched simpler controls win several comparisons.
On that study's four archive collections, learned-metric KAHM improves
ridge by 2.18\%, while fixed-metric KAHM improves it by 2.76\% at lower
cost. Those results motivate a more direct intervention rather than
establishing the value of the extra metric complexity.

A separately preserved nine-task exploratory pilot then trains gate
temperatures directly for the forecast. It improves mean KAHM error on
all three known laws while leaving simpler trained controls ahead. The
new reconstruction experiment therefore uses twelve fresh repetitions
per law and ten series from four previously unused collections, with
its code, analysis, roster and prepared inputs published before full
execution. Fresh draws from known laws and retrospective evaluation on
new collections support different claims; neither is an independent
replication by another research group.

The contribution is threefold. First, the paper specifies how KAHM
reconstruction becomes a time-series forecaster, including local
coordinates, a finite correction head, horizon-specific training and a
numerical fallback. Second, it separates representation information from
decoder access and clarifies donor-to-final residual mismatch with an
elementary risk identity. Third, it supplies comparative experiments and
replayable evidence that test the predictive contribution of the KAHM
rule under matched supervision. Local coordinates, differentiable ridge
solves \citep{Bertinetto2019Solvers} and supervised representation learning
are established ideas; we
do not claim them as new universal principles. The scientific conclusion
must follow the observed comparison, including negative results and cost,
rather than a presumption that nonlinear reconstruction is superior.

The stages intentionally vary estimands and implementations. Historical
capacity studies use local-only features and different reference budgets;
natural studies use raw augmentation and their own validation limits;
the newest experiment freezes the baseline and learns a correction from
its in-sample residuals. Between-study changes cannot be attributed to a
single component. The sections that follow state those distinctions and
report each evaluation on its own terms.

\section*{Reproducibility and research transparency}
The public repository preserves the original scientific source code,
execution freezes, and archived outcomes separately from the
\path{extensions/forecasting_utility} and
\path{extensions/target_geometry} studies. Historical bundles retain
their original scope: the natural forecast arrays reconstruct 2,496
task--method losses, and the separate scaling analysis contains 1,664
natural method runs and 1,440 controlled head fits. Its compact controlled
episode losses and recorded full-prediction audit are distinguished from
recreating the omitted full controlled prediction arrays. Those archives
are not silently replaced by the present extension.

The earlier forecasting-utility extension has its own protocol and dependency freeze, published
before full evaluation, and a reproducibility bundle retaining all 1,336
successful standalone forecasts from 1,344 attempted fits in 168
configurations. Eight geometric fits and four dependent policy evaluations
fail on two Car Parts configurations; their records remain in the bundle.
Successful policy forecasts reuse their selected standalone outputs.
A dated reporting amendment records the post-hoc matched-success archive
diagnostic, while preserving the undefined full-roster geometric losses.
The bundle also retains targets,
split/origin hashes, complete validation candidates, selected settings,
warning/failure accounting, dataset provenance, measured computation nodes,
and analysis tables. It covers this extension; it does not claim to embed
the original studies' complete bundles or the separate certificate paper.
A distinct manuscript-source bundle contains the editable \LaTeX{} and
generated displays for this revised research draft.

The independent forecasting-utility audit reconstructs final losses from saved
predictions, checks the fixed selector decisions and their cost
dependencies, and verifies exact baseline recovery at zero correction.
The manuscript asset builder independently reconstructs the residual
alignment identity and checks the reported hierarchical aggregation.
Generated tables, figures, and numerical macros carry input/output hashes.
These computational checks establish consistency of supplied evidence;
they do not create independent replications, establish a universal
complexity criterion, or replace scientific review.

The target-geometry study has a distinct public protocol checkpoint
\texttt{0d7ee3c17515eb1d922cdd302facd2b7874dc9a9}, created before its
confirmatory execution. Its 55-file scientific freeze includes the
protocol, numerical source, input roster, prepared arrays and independent
analysis. The complete 48-task run retains all 768 final forecasts,
validation candidates, donor boundaries, metric objectives, warnings,
input hashes and computation nodes. Its independent audit reconstructs
inputs and final losses, verifies donor exclusions and the metric proxy,
and checks selection against recorded grids without independently
refitting every validation candidate. No reporting amendment or task
replacement was needed for this run. That study's reproduction bundle covers
this target-geometry study and its frozen numerical dependencies; historical
archives retain their stated scope. The editable manuscript bundle includes
all generated displays needed to compile the integrated draft.

The direct-reconstruction extension has its own public checkpoint
\texttt{39174d06dffc7e28fed73dbbf866e539560dd021}. It preserves 32
scientific files and all 46 prepared input sets before model evaluation.
The three anticipated rideshare availability failures remain in its roster;
its planned four-collection efficacy aggregate is undefined. This freeze
is separate from the exploratory gate pilot and from all previous
execution checkpoints. The new reproduction bundle includes that frozen
extension, exact inherited numerical dependencies, selected fitted states,
optimizer trials, predictions and failure records, as well as the
separately preserved pilot. Repository task archives losslessly contain
original execution files; unpacking verifies their identities before the
frozen audit consumes them. These post-execution containers are packaging,
not revisions of the scientific protocol or recorded outcomes.
