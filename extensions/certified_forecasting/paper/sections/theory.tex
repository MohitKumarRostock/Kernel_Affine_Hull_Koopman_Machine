\section{Pairwise obstructions and their limitations}
\label{sec:theory}

This section defines the representation risk and derives its pairwise
obstruction. All comparisons use the same distribution of histories and
observed forecasting targets. The histories need not form a Markov state,
and neither stationarity nor exact linear feature evolution is assumed.

\subsection{A directly estimable pairwise obstruction}

Let $X\in\mathcal X$ be a random available history, let
$Y\in\mathbb R^q$ be its future target, and let
$U=\Gamma(X)\in\mathcal U$ be a fixed, measurable representation. Here
$\mathcal X$ and $\mathcal U$ denote the history and representation spaces.
A measurable pseudometric $d:\mathcal U\times\mathcal U\to[0,\infty)$ is symmetric,
vanishes on the diagonal, and satisfies the triangle inequality; unlike a
metric, it may assign zero distance to distinct points. For a sensitivity
budget $\kappa\geq0$, define
\begin{align*}
 \mathcal G_\kappa(d)
 &=\left\{g:\mathcal U\to\mathbb R^q\text{ measurable}:
       \|g(u)-g(v)\|_2\leq\kappa d(u,v)
       \text{ for all }u,v\in\mathcal U\right\},\\
 R^*_\kappa(\Gamma,d)
 &=\inf_{g\in\mathcal G_\kappa(d)}
             \mathbb E\|Y-g(\Gamma(X))\|_2^2.
\end{align*}
Thus $\kappa$ limits how much a forecast can change when its representation
changes, and $R^*_\kappa$ is the best population squared-error risk permitted
by that limit. For any predictor $f:\mathcal X\to\mathbb R^q$, write
$R(f)=\mathbb E\|Y-f(X)\|_2^2$. The representation, pseudometric, and
budget are fixed before the calibration data are observed. The class
$\mathcal G_\kappa(d)$ may contain more decoders than the fitted family;
a lower bound on its optimal risk therefore also applies to that family.
When the representation and pseudometric are clear, we abbreviate
$R^*_\kappa(\Gamma,d)$ to $R^*_\kappa$ and similarly suppress the
arguments of the witness defined below.

\paragraph{What the oracle risk measures.}
For $\mathbb E\|Y\|_2^2<\infty$, write
$M_X=\mathbb E[Y\mid X]$ and $M_U=\mathbb E[Y\mid U]$.
The standard orthogonality of conditional expectation gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
 R^*_\kappa(\Gamma,d)
 ={}&\underbrace{\mathbb E\|Y-M_X\|_2^2}_{\text{irreducible history risk}}
   +\underbrace{\mathbb E\|M_X-M_U\|_2^2}_{\text{representation information loss}}\\
 &+\underbrace{\inf_{g\in\mathcal G_\kappa(d)}
       \mathbb E\|M_U-g(U)\|_2^2}_{\text{sensitivity approximation}}.
\end{split}
\label{eq:risk-decomposition}
\end{equation}
Indeed, $U$ is measurable with respect to $X$, so
$\mathbb E[M_X\mid U]=M_U$. For every finite-risk $g$, the three
terms in $Y-g(U)=(Y-M_X)+(M_X-M_U)+(M_U-g(U))$ are pairwise
orthogonal in $L^2$. Expanding and taking the infimum proves the identity;
constant decoders ensure that this infimum is finite. This standard
decomposition is not a new result. It shows why a positive risk lower bound
alone cannot distinguish information loss from future noise or a restrictive
budget. Its information-loss term concerns the conditional mean under squared
error, not full conditional-distribution or Markov sufficiency. Restricting
the decoder further to a restricted affine family can add another approximation
gap, as the example at the end of this section demonstrates.

Any training-fitted preprocessing is part of $\Gamma$. The population
results below do not require the input coordinates to lie in $[-1,1]$.
For the finite-sample certificates, the deterministic range assumption
concerns the target and prediction loss. A common value of $\kappa$ across
preprocessing choices limits sensitivity in each resulting feature metric;
it does not impose a common Lipschitz constant in the original input units.
Each choice therefore specifies its own representation class and must be
frozen before calibration.

To define the pairwise statistic, first draw a conditioning variable $A$.
Then draw $(X,Y)$ and $(X',Y')$ independently from the same conditional law
given $A$, and set $U=\Gamma(X)$ and $U'=\Gamma(X')$. Each copy must have
the original marginal law of $(X,Y)$. Taking $A$ to be constant gives an
ordinary independent pair. With $z_+=\max\{z,0\}$, define
\begin{equation}
 W_{\kappa,A}(\Gamma,d)
 =\frac12\mathbb E\left[
       \bigl(\|Y-Y'\|_2-\kappa d(U,U')\bigr)_+^2\right].
 \label{eq:pair-witness}
\end{equation}
The expectation includes both the draw of $A$ and the two conditional draws.
We call $W_{\kappa,A}$ a \emph{pair witness}, or \emph{obstruction}: it
measures target separation that a decoder with budget $\kappa$ cannot
reproduce from the representation separation. The variable $A$ specifies
a sampling construction. Arbitrary nearest-neighbor matching need not have
this conditional independence property.

\begin{theorem}[Pairwise prediction obstruction]
\label{thm:pair-obstruction}
Assume $\mathbb E\|Y\|_2^2<\infty$. Then
\begin{equation}
 R^*_\kappa(\Gamma,d)\geq W_{\kappa,A}(\Gamma,d).
 \label{eq:pair-population}
\end{equation}
For a representation space $\mathcal U\subseteq\mathbb R^p$ with
$d(u,v)=\|u-v\|_2$, where $p$ is its ambient dimension, assume also
$\mathbb E\|U\|_2^2<\infty$. Define
\[
 \sigma_{Y\mid A}^2=\mathbb E\|Y-\mathbb E[Y\mid A]\|_2^2,
 \qquad
 \tau_{U\mid A}^2=\mathbb E\|U-\mathbb E[U\mid A]\|_2^2,
\]
where $\sigma_{Y\mid A}$ and $\tau_{U\mid A}$ are the nonnegative square
roots. These are scalar, averaged conditional variances, rather than
functions of a particular value of $A$. Then
\begin{equation}
 W_{\kappa,A}(\Gamma,\|\cdot\|_2)
 \geq (\sigma_{Y\mid A}-\kappa\tau_{U\mid A})_+^2.
 \label{eq:pair-dominance}
\end{equation}
Both inequalities use the same conditioning variable and evaluation law.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix $g\in\mathcal G_\kappa(d)$ with finite risk and define the two residuals
$r=Y-g(U)$ and $r'=Y'-g(U')$. Since
$Y-Y'=r-r'+g(U)-g(U')$, the triangle inequality gives
\[
 \|Y-Y'\|_2\leq\|r-r'\|_2+\kappa d(U,U'),
 \qquad
 (\|Y-Y'\|_2-\kappa d(U,U'))_+\leq\|r-r'\|_2.
\]
Conditional on $A$, the residuals are independent and identically
distributed. Expanding the squared norm therefore yields
\begin{align*}
 \mathbb E[\|r-r'\|_2^2\mid A]
 &=2\mathbb E[\|r\|_2^2\mid A]
           -2\mathbb E[r\mid A]^\top\mathbb E[r'\mid A]\\
 &=2\left\{\mathbb E[\|r\|_2^2\mid A]
                    -\|\mathbb E[r\mid A]\|_2^2\right\}.
\end{align*}
Square the preceding pointwise bound, take expectations, and divide by two.
This gives $W_{\kappa,A}\leq\mathbb E\|r\|_2^2=R(g\circ\Gamma)$.
Taking the infimum over $g$ proves \eqref{eq:pair-population}; predictors
with infinite risk satisfy the bound trivially.

For \eqref{eq:pair-dominance}, set
$a=\|Y-Y'\|_2$ and $b=\|U-U'\|_2$. Pointwise,
$a\leq(a-\kappa b)_++\kappa b$. For a scalar random variable $V$, the
notation $\|V\|_{L^2}=(\mathbb E V^2)^{1/2}$ denotes its root second
moment under the pairing law. The triangle inequality in $L^2$ gives
\[
 \|(a-\kappa b)_+\|_{L^2}
 \geq(\|a\|_{L^2}-\kappa\|b\|_{L^2})_+.
\]
The same conditional-independence calculation gives
$\mathbb E a^2=2\sigma_{Y\mid A}^2$ and
$\mathbb E b^2=2\tau_{U\mid A}^2$. Substitute these identities, square,
and divide by two to obtain \eqref{eq:pair-dominance}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[An exact decomposition of witness slack]
\label{prop:witness-slack}
For any admissible decoder $g$ with finite risk, put
$r=Y-g(U)$, $r'=Y'-g(U')$, and
$v=(\|Y-Y'\|_2-\kappa d(U,U'))_+$. Then
\begin{equation}
 R(g\circ\Gamma)-W_{\kappa,A}
 =\mathbb E\|\mathbb E[r\mid A]\|_2^2
   +\frac12\mathbb E\bigl[\|r-r'\|_2^2-v^2\bigr].
 \label{eq:witness-slack}
\end{equation}
Both terms are nonnegative. Consequently, $g$ attains the witness, meaning
$R(g\circ\Gamma)=W_{\kappa,A}$, if and only if
$\mathbb E[r\mid A]=0$ almost surely and
$\|r-r'\|_2=v$ almost surely. Whenever $r\ne r'$, the latter condition requires
$\|g(U)-g(U')\|_2=\kappa d(U,U')$. It also requires the decoder
difference to be a nonnegative scalar multiple of the residual difference;
the scalar may be zero.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Conditional independence gives
$\frac12\mathbb E\|r-r'\|_2^2
=R(g\circ\Gamma)-\mathbb E\|\mathbb E[r\mid A]\|_2^2$.
Subtract the witness and use $v\leq\|r-r'\|_2$ from the preceding proof.
Equality requires both nonnegative terms to vanish. If $r\ne r'$, it
also requires equality in
$\|Y-Y'\|_2\leq\|r-r'\|_2+\|g(U)-g(U')\|_2
\leq\|r-r'\|_2+\kappa d(U,U')$, giving the stated conditions.
\end{proof}

The witness directly estimates a squared distance discrepancy. It does not
estimate two variances separately and then subtract their square roots.
This distinction can matter when little input variation remains in the
representation. The certificates below use concentration for bounded means;
they establish no new minimax estimation rate. Taking a square root of a risk
bound can still produce slow convergence near zero. Moreover,
\eqref{eq:pair-dominance} compares certificates under a common pairing law.
It does not compare pairing at a common time origin with conditioning on a
different grouping, such as a reference-class label $c(X)$, where $c$
assigns a history to a group.

\begin{proposition}[Exactness on a two-atom law]
\label{prop:two-atom}
Suppose $(U,Y)$ takes only two values: $(u_0,y_0)$ with probability
$p\in(0,1)$ and $(u_1,y_1)$ with probability $1-p$. Use Euclidean
representation distance and an unconditioned independent pair, and omit the
constant conditioning variable from the notation. Then
\begin{equation}
 R^*_\kappa
 =W_\kappa
 =p(1-p)\bigl(\|y_1-y_0\|_2
                 -\kappa\|u_1-u_0\|_2\bigr)_+^2.
 \label{eq:two-atom-optimum}
\end{equation}
An affine map attains this optimum. If forecasts must lie in a nonempty
closed convex set containing $y_0,y_1$, projecting that map onto the set
preserves both the optimum and the Lipschitz budget.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $a=\|y_1-y_0\|_2$, $b=\|u_1-u_0\|_2$, and
$\mu=py_0+(1-p)y_1$. The unequal ordered pairs have total probability
$2p(1-p)$, whereas equal pairs contribute zero. Thus
\eqref{eq:pair-witness} gives the right side of
\eqref{eq:two-atom-optimum} as a lower bound.

To attain the bound, first consider $a=0$: the constant forecast $y_0$
has zero risk. If $a>0$, let $v=(y_1-y_0)/a$ be the unit target direction
and let $s=\min\{a,\kappa b\}$ be the allowed fitted separation. Choose
\[
 f_0=\mu-(1-p)sv,\qquad f_1=\mu+psv.
\]
Then $y_0-f_0=-(1-p)(a-s)v$ and
$y_1-f_1=p(a-s)v$, so their weighted squared error is
$p(1-p)(a-s)^2$, exactly the proposed bound. If $b=0$, then $s=0$
and the constant map $\mu$ realizes both fitted values. If $b>0$, use
\[
 g(u)=f_0+
 \frac{\langle u-u_0,u_1-u_0\rangle}{b^2}(f_1-f_0).
\]
Its linear part has operator norm $\|f_1-f_0\|_2/b=s/b\leq\kappa$,
so it is an admissible affine decoder. Both fitted values lie on the
segment joining $y_0$ and $y_1$. Projection onto the stated convex set
fixes them and is nonexpansive, meaning that it cannot increase distances.
\end{proof}

Exactness also holds on continuous supports. Let $T$ be a square-integrable
real random variable, set $U=T$ and $Y=b+\beta Tv$, and assume
$b,v\in\mathbb R^q$, $\|v\|_2=1$, and $\beta\geq0$.
Here $\beta$ is the target's slope, and
$\operatorname{Var}(T)=\mathbb E(T-\mathbb ET)^2$.
For independent $T,T'$ and $d(t,t')=|t-t'|$, the pair discrepancy is
$(\beta-\kappa)_+|T-T'|$. Since
$\mathbb E(T-T')^2=2\operatorname{Var}(T)$, its witness is
$(\beta-\kappa)_+^2\operatorname{Var}(T)$. The affine decoder
\[
 g(t)=b+\beta\mathbb E[T]v+
       \min\{\beta,\kappa\}(t-\mathbb E[T])v
\]
has Lipschitz constant at most $\kappa$ and residual
$(\beta-\kappa)_+(T-\mathbb ET)v$. Its risk equals the witness, so
$W_\kappa=R^*_\kappa$ also in this continuous-support example.

\paragraph{Two sources of population looseness.}
Even with independent unconditioned pairs, the witness need not approximate
the optimum by a fixed relative factor. Let $\kappa=1$ and let $(U,Y)$
take values $(3,0),(0,-1),(1,1)$ with probabilities
$1-2\varepsilon,\varepsilon,\varepsilon$, where
$0<\varepsilon<1/2$. Only the two rare atoms have a positive discrepancy,
so $W_\kappa=\varepsilon^2$. The fitted values
$g(0)=-1/2$, $g(1)=1/2$, and $g(3)=0$ attain risk $\varepsilon/2$.
Linear interpolation between these points, with constant values outside
$[0,3]$, defines a $1$-Lipschitz decoder. Conversely, any admissible fitted
values $v_0=g(0)$ and $v_1=g(1)$ satisfy $|v_1-v_0|\leq1$.
The minimum of $(-1-v_0)^2+(1-v_1)^2$ under this constraint is $1/2$,
attained at $v_0=-1/2$ and $v_1=1/2$. The two rare atoms alone therefore
contribute at least $\varepsilon/2$ to every decoder's risk. Thus
$R^*_\kappa=\varepsilon/2$ and
$W_\kappa/R^*_\kappa=2\varepsilon$ can approach zero. The same example
lies in $\Delta_2$ after mapping $u$ to $(u/3,1-u/3)$ and setting
$\kappa=3/\sqrt2$. Stratification below replaces the quadratic probability
of encountering this rare pair by the probability of its stratum.

A separate gap comes from comparing the fitted affine family with all
Lipschitz decoders. For equally likely $X\in\{-1,0,1\}$, take
$\Phi(x)=((x+1)/2,(1-x)/2)^\top$, $Y=X^2$, and $\kappa=\sqrt2$,
with Euclidean distance between feature vectors.
The target satisfies the Lipschitz inequalities on this support and has a
scalar Lipschitz extension, so $W_\kappa=R^*_\kappa=0$.
An affine function of $\Phi(x)$ is affine in $x$; clipping it to $[0,1]$
preserves monotonicity. A nondecreasing forecast cannot fit the target pair
$(1,0)$ at $x=-1,0$: its minimum sum of squared errors on that pair is
$1/2$, attained when both fitted values are $1/2$. A nonincreasing forecast
has the same obstruction on the target pair $(0,1)$ at $x=0,1$.
Averaging over the three atoms gives MSE at least $1/6$ for every clipped
affine forecast. The unprojected affine optimum is the constant $2/3$,
with MSE $2/9$. Thus a sharp lower bound for all Lipschitz decoders need
not describe the optimum of a restricted affine family.
