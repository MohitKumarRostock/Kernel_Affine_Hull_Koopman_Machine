\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What this adds to KAHM forecasting.}
KAHM reconstruction affinities become part of a complete learning procedure
with a fixed prediction target, a controlled decoder class, and explicit
accuracy decisions. Retaining dictionary blocks makes geometric refinement
comparable, and evaluating the original output connects those decisions to
the forecasting task. The matched study identifies consistent gains against
the geometric alternatives on nonlinear AR and against RBF on the fixed
PenDigits cohort, while affine-hull Frobenius and rollout heads are stronger
on the oscillators. These results make the value of KAHM geometry a question
about the task, head, and retained resolution. The reconstruction algorithm
is inherited, and the general certification arguments do not select a
preferred representation in advance.

\paragraph{What the certificate adds to validation.}
An empirical error ranks candidates, whereas an upper confidence bound makes a
statement about a requested population error tolerance. The lower certificate
answers a different question: whether every decoder in a specified sensitivity
class is obstructed by the representation. The paired improvement statistic
answers a third question about two particular fitted models. These statements
are useful precisely because they are not interchangeable. A small upper bound
can justify deployment under the stated sampling law; a positive lower bound
may justify changing the representation or budget; a large gap between the two
can remain statistically or structurally inconclusive.

\paragraph{What is and is not learned.}
The implemented method learns KAHM reconstruction models and constrained
forecasting heads, then selects among retained dictionary prefixes. It is not a
proof that unsupervised geometric clustering discovers a sufficient state.
The residual-correlation result gives a population sufficient condition for one
added direction, and the metric-expansion result gives a necessary condition
for successful refinement. Neither turns an arbitrary proposed split into a
guaranteed improvement. The experiment evaluates fixed candidate families;
fresh-block sequential refinement is a valid theoretical extension, not an
additional experiment that was carried out here.

\paragraph{Restricted comparator classes and loose obstructions.}
The sensitivity budget is substantive. Excluding all $\kappa$-Lipschitz decoders
does not exclude predictors with larger sensitivity, longer histories, or
different observations. The pair witness can be zero even when the best risk in
the class is positive. In particular, pairing at the same origin can miss
information loss that manifests only across different origins. It may also be
less informative than a class-conditioned witness available under a different
sampling protocol. We do not use a zero lower bound as a feature-learning loss
or a certificate of adequacy.

\paragraph{Independent sampling units and useful precision.}
Independent episodes are a strong but directly interpretable sampling
assumption. They are appropriate for independently initialized simulations and
can be appropriate for separate experimental runs. A chronological split of one
long record does not imply this independence. Blocking a dependent series
requires quantitative dependence assumptions and a justified correction for
the training-to-calibration separation as well as separation between blocks.
An informal autocorrelation estimate or a chosen ``effective sample size'' is
not a substitute. The real-data experiment illustrates the complementary
problem: many time points and strokes can coexist with very few defensible
independent writer-level units. In that case a valid conservative procedure may
not certify an interesting tolerance.

\paragraph{Targets, boundedness, and distribution shift.}
The experiments' synthetic target range follows from their definitions. The
real target range comes from the acquisition box and a fixed linear scale,
without normalizing each completed trajectory using its future extrema. If a
new task has genuinely unbounded outputs, the present bounded-loss certificate
requires a different tail assumption or a clearly declared capped loss. Merely
clipping predictions is insufficient. Calibration under one episode law also
does not certify performance after a change in dynamics, noise, acquisition, or
origin selection. These restrictions apply equally to all comparison methods.

\paragraph{Input preprocessing is a model choice.}
The follow-up in Section~\ref{sec:input-scaling} shows that training-only range
scaling can improve retained soft KAHM forecasts, including a replicated gain
on the fixed PenDigits cohort. The same comparison retains regressions on
other tasks and for hard assignments. These results justify an explicit
preprocessing option and task-specific validation. They do not support
replacing standardization throughout the original campaigns or treating
bounded inputs as a guarantee of better geometry. Selecting among several
preprocessing arms for certification also requires an appropriate joint
confidence allocation.

\paragraph{Experimental breadth and computational claims.}
The experiments include several controlled dynamics and one real acquisition
domain. The matched geometry comparisons concern the specified implementations
and shared selection grids; independent family-specific tuning could change
their ranking. They are not a broad benchmark of modern neural time-series models,
and the fixed configurations do not establish an accuracy frontier against
extensively tuned deep forecasters. Representation-size comparisons are
reported separately from runtime: the prototype runner recomputes some shared
feature transforms, and its per-candidate training-time fields are not a fair
runtime benchmark. The archived total execution times and environment identify
the actual experiment, rather than a deployment-speed claim.

\section{Conclusion}

KAHM soft associations can support forecasts of the observed signal together
with explicit finite-sample risk statements. Retained dictionaries and a
matching block budget preserve earlier predictors and prevent exact feature
replication from creating an artificial class improvement. A pairwise
obstruction, fitted-risk bounds, and paired refinement comparisons answer
separate questions about the representation and the learned forecast.
The same analysis permits comparison with other geometric dictionaries.
The experiments establish useful KAHM decisions and favorable geometric
comparisons on nonlinear AR and pen trajectories, while retaining stronger
oscillator alternatives, preprocessing regressions, and unresolved tolerances.
This makes the conditions for using KAHM geometry part of the
reported result: credible target bounds, an appropriate evaluation law, and
enough independent units are required for informative certification.
