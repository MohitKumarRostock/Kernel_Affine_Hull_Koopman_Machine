\section{A follow-up comparison of KAHM input scaling}
\label{sec:input-scaling}

\input{tables/input_scaling_numbers}

The preceding experiments use training-moment standardization before fitting
the geometric dictionaries. Although every raw observation in those tasks is
already bounded by $[-1,1]$, standardization generally sends the histories
outside that interval. We therefore conducted a separate follow-up to test
whether KAHMs benefit from modeling histories whose training range is
$[-1,1]$. This is an empirical preprocessing question: the reconstruction
regularization and folding score in the reused KAHM implementation depend on
input magnitudes and geometry, so bounded raw targets alone do not determine
the scale seen by a KAHM.

\subsection{A fixed intervention and two types of replication}

For each history coordinate $j$, let $a_j$ and $b_j$ be its minimum and maximum
over the training histories, computed before the original geometry
subsampling. The primary alternative replaces standardization by
\begin{equation}
 z_j(x)=
 \begin{cases}
  2\dfrac{x_j-a_j}{b_j-a_j}-1,&b_j>a_j,\\[4pt]
  0,&b_j=a_j.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:input-minmax}
\end{equation}
The training extrema are frozen for validation, calibration, and testing.
An unseen coordinate outside the training extrema can map outside $[-1,1]$;
the primary arm does not clip it. A separately specified diagnostic arm uses
$\widetilde z_j=\max\{-1,\min\{1,z_j\}\}$ for all queries. A second diagnostic
arm uses the existing bounded raw histories without standardization.
Training-constant coordinates map to zero in both min--max arms. No extrema
are estimated from a validation set, test set, or completed individual test
trajectory.

The intervention applies only to KAHM geometry and its dependent retained,
Frobenius-budget, duplicate-block, hard-assignment, and feature-rollout models.
The non-KAHM feature families, forecast targets, output clipping, coefficient
budgets, ridge coefficient, affinity exponent, history lengths, origins,
sample counts, local-point limits, and random streams are unchanged. Each
family selects its history and dictionary stage by mean validation MSE over
the horizons, separately within each preprocessing arm. The preprocessing
arms themselves are not selected using test error.

The first comparison replays all original fitting seeds and every
coefficient-budget condition. Its four arms are paired on identical data.
Because the hypothesis was proposed after inspecting the original study,
these reused streams provide a matched follow-up comparison, not a newly
untouched test set. The primary outcome, alternatives, selection rule, and
complete schedule were recorded before the new scaling results were read.
All outcomes are retained, including regressions. The exact-risk two-atom
experiment and analytic duplicate-invariance illustration do not fit KAHM
input geometry and therefore have no corresponding scaling intervention.

For confirmation, we froze the same four preprocessing arms and used fitting
seeds $4701$--$4703$ on all six synthetic systems. The original stream-offset
rule then generates new training, validation, calibration, and test episodes,
with the original sample counts and main-study budget $\kappa=4$. This adds
18 paired synthetic repetitions, with no tuning between the replay and
confirmation stages. For PenDigits, the same three new seeds select new
training subsamples from the existing development writers. Its validation
writers and held-out cohort remain the same. This is confirmation across
training subsamples on a fixed cohort; it is not a replication on newly
sampled writers.

\input{tables/input_scaling_uncertainty}

The fitting repetition is distinct from an individual test episode.
Table~\ref{tab:input-scaling-uncertainty} reports both the sample variation of
the paired MSE change across complete repetitions and its conditional
test-episode standard error. For the latter, we first subtract the two
models' losses on each common episode and average horizons within that
episode. We estimate the variance of each repetition's paired average from
its independent test episodes, then combine those variances for the mean
over repetitions. This measures Monte Carlo uncertainty conditional on the
fitted, validation-selected models. The many test episodes do not supply
many independent fits. The mean change weights effect sizes, so it need not
have the sign of a majority of repetitions. The archive retains every seed's sign and effect;
neither a small conditional standard error nor a favorable mean is treated
as a general guarantee that scaling improves a newly fitted model.

The strongest relative gain in the primary comparison occurs for the retained soft
KAHM predictor on PenDigits. Its all-horizon, coordinate-pooled cohort MSE
decreases by \ScalingPenOriginalMagnitude\% in the replay and
\ScalingPenConfirmationMagnitude\% with the new training subsamples.
Table~\ref{tab:input-scaling-pendigits} reports the horizon-wise RMSEs and
their fitting-seed variation. All three horizons improve for each coordinate
and each fitting seed in both stages. Every held-out writer also has a smaller
coordinate-pooled, all-horizon mean loss in each seed. These repeated writer
comparisons concern the same 13 writers and do not establish an iid-writer
population claim. Quadratic regression and ridge AR remain more accurate
than the scaled retained KAHM predictor on this task; the one-step retained
KAHM error also remains above persistence.

The improvement does not extend to every KAHM-derived predictor. On PenDigits,
the hard-assignment model's all-horizon MSE increases by
\ScalingHardPenOriginalIncrease\% on the original seeds and
\ScalingHardPenConfirmationIncrease\% on the new seeds, with degradation in
every fitting repetition. The complete records retain the other KAHM
ablations and rollouts as well. The evidence supports a benefit for particular
soft representations under the stated task and protocol, not a claim that
all KAHMs improve under the same scaling.

As a distinct secondary outcome, the KAHM rollout has smaller mean all-horizon
MSE on \ScalingRolloutOriginalImprovingTasks\ of seven original tasks and
\ScalingRolloutConfirmationImprovingTasks\ of seven new-seed tasks.
The new-seed reductions include \ScalingRolloutVDPConfirmationMagnitude\%
on Van der Pol, \ScalingRolloutDuffingConfirmationMagnitude\% on Duffing,
and \ScalingRolloutPenConfirmationMagnitude\% on PenDigits, while the
logistic rollout instead worsens by
\ScalingRolloutLogisticConfirmationMagnitude\%.

Clipped min--max scaling gives very similar primary-family results to the
unclipped alternative in this study, while using raw bounded histories can
give a different change in accuracy (Table~\ref{tab:input-scaling-diagnostics}).
Both diagnostics retain the logistic and Van der Pol limitations. Thus the
observation that a history lies in $[-1,1]$ is insufficient to predict whether
the learned forecasting model improves. The raw-history arm also removes
centering and variance normalization, and min--max scaling changes both
location and coordinate scale; this study does not attribute each outcome
to a single internal component of KAHM geometry.

Table~\ref{tab:input-scaling-budget} retains every original sensitivity
condition. At $\kappa=1$, the mean effects are small and their seed signs are
mixed. At $\kappa=4$ and 16, the quasiperiodic comparison improves in all three
repetitions, while the logistic comparison worsens in all three. The harmonic
mean changes sign across budgets. No budget or preprocessing arm is chosen
by test performance. These results also limit extrapolation of a gain from
one coefficient class to another.

Improved empirical accuracy does not automatically produce a new certification
decision. At each recorded tolerance $0.20$, $0.35$, and $0.50$, the number of
repetitions in which the retained KAHM family certifies the all-horizon
requirement is identical between standardization and the primary min--max
arm for every original synthetic system and budget and every synthetic
confirmation system. PenDigits retains the same small number of writer
units and its uninformative conditional bounds. The original failure
allocations apply within each frozen preprocessing arm; they do not provide
a joint event for searching across all four arms. We do not label the
cross-arm accuracy comparisons as certified preprocessing improvements.

\paragraph{Audit and resulting implementation choice.}
The standardization replay reproduces every retained non-timing numerical
result of the original campaigns exactly. Across arms, all non-KAHM numerical
results and saved synthetic score arrays are unchanged; the writer-level
control losses are unchanged as well. Four truncated score archives, two in
the original replay and two in synthetic confirmation, were regenerated by
exact replays with the same frozen source, configuration, and seeds. Their
non-timing result records and frozen candidate descriptions agree exactly;
hashes of the damaged and replayed archives and the exact-replay repair
records are retained. Final archive
integrity checks and independent aggregation of the selected-model test
scores precede reporting.

The implementation consequently exposes the scaling alternatives as explicit
options and preserves standardization as the default for the original
campaigns. The manuscript retains its original tables and reports this
follow-up separately. The replicated PenDigits and Duffing gains justify
making the option available, while the replicated regressions preclude
presenting it as a universal improvement or silently replacing every original
experimental result.
